## 1. Purpose and five-second takeaway

Show **four governed state concepts, four distinct purposes — and one route that cannot presently proceed**.

**If the audience takes one thing from this visual in five seconds:**

> Four states, four purposes — and the route to Published cannot presently be travelled.

## 2. Source and authority basis

`S1` §6.3 is **the governing definition** of all four states, and `S2` §1 elaborates each. `S2` §3 supplies the transitions: **`T1`'s authorising function is the Task-Team Lead** (`S1` §9.4) and the connector may be solid; **`T4` has no available authorising function, and information remains Shared** — the connector must be broken.

`S3` §3 maps three of the four adopted areas to states. **`0. Common Files` maps to none**, and **Record / Retained has no approved area** — the mismatch is a controlled finding, not an untidiness to correct. `CGD-C06` records that **no mandatory `04 Archive` root is approved or required**.

**`CGD-C01` and `CGD-C03` are the conditions the visual most depends on:** *"the existence or name of an area does not by itself establish an information state"*, and a state is not created by moving a file.

**On the fourth state's label.** Four label forms exist across the controlled sources. The **majority teaching form `Record / Retained`** is used; the governing `S1` §6.3 table's lower-case *retained* is recorded in [`source-inventory.md`](../../../module-04-cde-workflows-and-information-states/source-inventory.md) §6. **Neither is presented as *the* correct label**, and this visual does not adjudicate the variance.

Source identifiers `S1`–`S14` are defined in

Classification follows

## 7. Connector semantics

| Connector | Line | Arrowhead | Label block | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **WIP → Shared** | **Solid** | **Filled, at the Shared edge** | `T1` · `Task-Team Lead authority established` | **Supported.** The only supported state progression on this visual — and only because the authorising function is established (`S1` §9.4) |
| **Shared ⇢ Published / Authorised** | **Broken, with a visible break mark at the gutter centre** | **Filled, but the stroke is interrupted before it** | `T4 BLOCKED` · `Publication-authorising function: TBD` · `Information remains Shared` | **Halted.** A route that exists in governance and cannot presently be traversed |
| **Published ⇢ Record / Retained** | **Broken, terminating in an open stub** | **None — no arrowhead at all** | `Retention approach: unresolved` · `Not reachable while T4 is blocked` | **Unreachable.** Not merely halted — it cannot be entered, because its origin state has not been reached |

**The second and third connectors are drawn differently, and the difference is the teaching.** `T4` is a *blocked* route with a named unresolved authority; the third is *unreachable* and additionally has no resolved method.

- **Drawing them identically** implies both are waiting on the same thing.
- **Drawing the third as merely delayed** — a faded arrow, a dotted line reading as *in progress*, a "coming soon" treatment — **asserts a retention approach that does not exist.**

**Drawing every connector broken** reads as *non-functional* rather than *deliberately halted*, which is why `T1` is solid.

## 8. Status and warning treatment

**All eight status labels are on the panels** — four governance, four implementation — because they are content, not attribution.

**Panel sources are not printed on the panels.** Five lines per panel is the density limit. **One source line only**, 14 pt, bottom right of the annotation band: `S1 §6.3 · S2 §1, §3 · S3 §3 · S4 §7`.

**The mandatory warning is on the visual**, centred beneath the connector labels, at body size — **not in the footer, not in the notes, not shrunk to a caption.**

**The area-versus-state mismatch is a compact three-line annotation**, left-aligned, spanning less than two panel widths. **It is never drawn as a parallel four-folder row.** Four areas aligned beneath four states is the exact false correspondence `CGD-C01` and `CGD-C03` exist to prevent — and a producer's instinct is to build it, because it looks tidy. **The annotation is deliberately not aligned to any panel**, so no reader can pair a line with a column.

## 9. Build or reveal sequence

**Not applicable — no build. The whole visual appears in one frame**, including all three connectors, all eight status labels, the warning and the annotation.

**A staged reveal is prohibited.** Any frame showing four panels with a solid or absent set of connectors is a frame that claims an operating four-state lifecycle, **and a presenter cannot un-show it.**

## 10. Mandatory omissions

No `04 Archive` in any form · no folder icon as a state panel · no four-area row beneath the four panels · no completion tick · no green lifecycle · no red failure styling on the blocked route · no progress bar · no date on any unresolved item · no named publication authority · no fifth state · **no Delivered, Received or Accepted panel** — they are events, actions and statuses, **not states** (`S2` §3, §13) · no platform image.

## 11. Accessibility and projection requirements

- **Connector meaning is carried in text as well as line style.** `T1` says `authority established`; `T4` says `BLOCKED`; the third says `Not reachable`. **A viewer who cannot resolve a dashed stroke still reads the state of every route.**
- **No reliance on red or green.** The block is not red and the supported route is not green — the block is **deliberate governance, not a fault**, and red styling teaches that something has gone wrong.
- **The model is fully legible in monochrome**: solid versus broken stroke, arrowhead versus open stub, plus the text labels.
- **Reading order** — panel 1, its outgoing connector and label, panel 2, its connector and label, panel 3, its connector and label, panel 4, then the warning, then the annotation.
- **Type: panel title 20 pt, purpose and reliance 14 pt, status labels 14 pt minimum, connector labels 14 pt, warning 18 pt.** **Status labels may not be shrunk below 14 pt to win space** — if a panel will not hold five lines at that size, **shorten the purpose line, never the status.**
- **Contrast ≥ 4.5:1** for text; **≥ 3:1** for panel borders and connector strokes.

## 15. Native PowerPoint reconstruction notes

**Reconstruct with native PowerPoint shapes, tables and text boxes.** **No image
is imported, and no diagram-rendering engine is used.**

**Four rectangles, three connectors of three different kinds, four text blocks.** No SmartArt.

1. Insert four rectangles at the geometry above. **Set x, y, width and height numerically.**
2. **Connector 1:** straight connector, solid, arrowhead at the Shared end. **Connector 2:** straight connector, **dashed**, arrowhead at the Published end, then insert a small break mark centred at x 480 in the connector's own colour — or split the connector into two segments with a visible gap. **Connector 3:** straight connector, **dashed, arrowhead set to none**, ending short of panel 4 as an open stub.
3. **Set each arrowhead explicitly.** PowerPoint's default is no head, so connector 1 and 2 must be set; connector 3 must be **left** headless and checked after any theme change, because some themes apply an arrow style to all connectors.
4. Label blocks as separate text boxes beneath the gutters — **not as connector labels**, which PowerPoint attaches to the line and re-centres.
5. Warning as its own text box, 18 pt, centred, **on the slide** — not in the footer placeholder, which several themes render at 10 pt and grey.
6. Annotation as a left-aligned text box of three lines. **Do not build it as a table**, and do not align its lines to the panel columns.
7. **No entrance animation.** One static frame.
8. **After any theme or template change, re-check three things:** connector 3 still has no arrowhead, connector 2's break is still visible, and no status label has auto-shrunk below 14 pt.
