## 1. Purpose and five-second takeaway

**Purpose.** Prevent the early collapse of the six §9.2 decision acts on one side
and finding / clash / Issue on the other.

**Five-second takeaway.** *Nine things. Not one word.*

**Why this form.** The rule between the domains is load-bearing: the six and the
three come from **different sources** and govern **different things**. Six and
three are deliberately unequal counts — **a nine-card grid would erase exactly
that distinction.**

**Simplification rule.** Definitions may compress to their operative clause.
**The six terms and the three objects may never be merged into one list, one grid
or one nine-item set**; **the anti-collapse warning may not be shortened**;
**neither Issue scope may be dropped or paraphrased into the other.**

## 7. Connector semantics

**No connectors anywhere on this slide.**

An arrow between any two of the nine would assert a sequence the sources do not
state at this point. An arrow **between the domains** would assert that a
decision act produces a coordination object.

## 8. Status and warning treatment

**Mandatory warning: the three-line anti-collapse block**, placed so it is read
**after both domains and before the comparison**.

The separating rule is a status device as much as a layout device: it is what
makes the two vocabularies read as **two**, from **two sources**, with **two
governance statuses**.

**Status is carried by words, never by colour alone**, and never by a coloured
chip, badge or dot. Minimum visible type **14 pt**; principal labels **≥ 18 pt**;
text contrast **≥ 4.5 : 1**; border and rule contrast **≥ 3 : 1**.

## 11. Accessibility and projection requirements

**Reading order:** six acts → separating rule → three objects → warning →
comparison.

**The separation must be described in words** — a rule is invisible to a screen
reader. **The `≠` signs must be read aloud as *"is not"***.

No information is carried by colour alone. Warnings remain legible at projection
scale.

## 12. Screen-reader or presenter-notes description

> *Two separate domains. First domain, from BEP section 9.2: six decision acts.
> Check, verification against a defined requirement before progression. Review,
> consideration of information for a stated purpose. Authorise, permit
> information to progress, share, publish or exchange for a defined purpose.
> Accept, the recipient acknowledges information as suitable or received for the
> stated purpose. Reject, information is not accepted for the stated progression
> or use, and action is required. Coordinate, the multidisciplinary interface
> process, not design approval. These terms are not collapsed into approval.
> Second domain, visually separated, from Coordination and Review Strategy
> section 12: three coordination objects. Finding, an observed coordination
> matter requiring triage. Clash, a geometric or spatial finding. Issue, a
> governed action record. Warning: not one word; clash or finding is not an
> Issue; accepted condition is not recipient accept. Below, two rows of equal
> weight: the BEP's narrower Issue scope and the strategy's broader one. Both
> recorded. Neither rewritten.*

## 13. Producer-failure test

**Ask a reader how many vocabularies are on the slide.** If the answer is *one*,
the separating rule has failed.

**Ask which Issue definition the project uses.** If a reader can pick one from
the styling, the comparison has failed — **both must look equally
authoritative.**

## 14. STOP conditions

**Applicable module-wide STOP conditions: 6, 8, 9, 10, 53, 54, 69, 70, 72, 74.**

Stop and refer back to the accepted plan if this visual would:

- Collapse Finding, Clash and Issue **(6)**
- Collapse Check, Review, Coordinate, Authorise, Accept or Reject into "approval"
  **(8)**
- Present `Accepted condition` as recipient `Accept` **(9)**
- Present `Accepted condition` as publication authorisation, technical approval
  or regulatory acceptance **(10)**
- Rely on colour alone **(53)**
- Import external imagery or a screenshot **(54)**
- Harmonise a recorded terminology variance — here **variance 3**, the two Issue
  scopes **(69)**
- Use a coloured chip, badge, dot or traffic light in place of a status word
  **(70)**
- Name a holder, organisation or appointment for any of the six acts **(72)**
- Present Module 6 teaching material as governance **(74)**

The full seventy-five module-wide STOP conditions are in [`README.md`](README.md)
§8 and in the accepted plan §3.

would connect terms that the sources leave unconnected. STOP 6, 8.
