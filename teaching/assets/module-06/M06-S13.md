## 1. Purpose and five-second takeaway

**Purpose.** What the cycle leaves behind, how an unresolved matter is carried
out of it, and what neither a `Closed` label nor the available assurance material
proves.

**Five-second takeaway.** *Nothing produced, nothing proved — and no sampling
method exists.*

**Why this form.** **The absence is the slide's most transferable point, so it
must outrank the content.** Heading the closure block with what closure **does**
cover makes the ten limits read as **scope rather than as failure**.

**Simplification rule.** Output names and limit phrases may compress. **No output
may carry a status, tick or marker**; **the closure block must keep its `does
cover` heading before the limits**; **the ten limits may not be reduced below
ten**; **the assurance warning may not be shortened, moved above the blocks, or
reduced below the blocks in visual weight.**

**coordination input register · `COORD-01` federation / reference · check

## 7. Connector semantics

**None.** Three parallel blocks and a strip.

escalation leads to closure, **neither of which the sources state.**

## 8. Status and warning treatment

**Mandatory warning: the full-width assurance strip, visually heavier than the
three blocks** — thicker border and larger type — **and the `None is recorded as
produced` heading on the outputs block.**

**The absence outranks the content.** If the three blocks read as more important
than the strip, the weight relationship has failed.

**Status is carried by words, never by colour alone**, and never by a coloured
chip, badge or dot. Minimum visible type **14 pt**; principal labels **≥ 18 pt**;
warning heading **≥ 20 pt**; text contrast **≥ 4.5 : 1**; border contrast
**≥ 3 : 1**.

## 13. Producer-failure test

**Ask a reader how many of the nine outputs exist on this project.** If any
answer other than *none is recorded as produced* is given, block 1's heading has
failed.

**Ask what the assurance method is.** If a reader describes anything at all, the
warning has failed.

**Ask whether the warning or the blocks read as more important.** If the blocks,
the weight relationship has failed.

## 14. STOP conditions

**Applicable module-wide STOP conditions: 1, 2, 33, 40, 41, 42, 43, 44, 45, 46,
53, 54, 70, 71, 72.**

Stop and refer back to the accepted plan if this visual would:

- Imply the complete governed coordination cycle has been executed **(1)**
- **Close, date, soften or weaken `GCR-006` (2)**
- **Treat `Closed` as proof of correction, verification, approval, publication,
  acceptance, certification or compliance (33)**
- **Present every evidence output as produced (40)**
- **Duplicate the Issue database in a second report (41)**
- **Present escalation as resolving a matter or creating authority (42)**
- **Fill an unknown decision owner with a plausible role (43)**
- **Invent an assurance-sampling population, size, frequency, method, threshold
  or report form (44)**
- **Present `A1`–`A5` as an assurance-sampling procedure (45)**
- **Present assurance as certification, guarantee or regulatory proof (46)**
- Rely on colour alone **(53)** · Import external imagery or a screenshot **(54)**
- Use a coloured chip, badge, dot or traffic light in place of a status word
  **(70)**
- **Place a tick, percentage, progress bar, score or completion mark against any
  output (71)**
- Name a holder, organisation or appointment **(72)**
