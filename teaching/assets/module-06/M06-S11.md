## 1. Purpose and five-second takeaway

**Purpose.** Fix **when** verification may occur, **what** it tests and **what it
is not** — and record honestly that whether it is mandatory in every cycle is
unsettled.

**Five-second takeaway.** *You cannot verify a change nobody can see — and it is
not required in every cycle.*

**Why this form.** ***Where required* in the gate label is the honest form of
`H18`.** Drawing an unconditional gate would resolve a variance the module
expressly declines to resolve. **Placing the near-misses *outside* the gate**
shows that checking, technical response, `Ready for Verification` and status
alone do not reach it.

**Simplification rule.** Prerequisite and finding wording may compress to its
operative phrase. **All four prerequisites must remain**, **all three findings
must remain at equal weight**, **all four exclusions must remain**, and **the
gate label may never lose `where required`.** **The unresolved warning may not be
shortened or moved into the notes.**

## 4. Fixed layout

**Four prerequisites grouped as one block before a verification gate** — drawn as
**four conditions that must all hold**, not as four sequential steps.

**One gate object, labelled `Verification — where required`.**

**Three possible findings after the gate, at equal weight.**

**Four exclusions in a clearly separate strip.**

**Four near-misses placed outside the gate**, visibly not passing through it.

**One visible unresolved warning.**

## 7. Connector semantics

**One connector from the prerequisite frame to the gate**, meaning **`all four
conditions hold`**.

**Three connectors from the gate to the three findings**, of **identical
weight**, each meaning **`possible verification finding`**.

**No connector from any near-miss into the gate**, and **no connector from the
gate to the exclusion strip** — the exclusions are what verification **is not**,
not an output of it.

## 8. Status and warning treatment

**Mandatory warning: the unresolved warning, visible on the slide, at ≥ 14 pt,
visibly smaller than the three findings but never hidden.** It is a **recorded
uncertainty, not a fourth finding**.

**The gate label carries `where required` at all times.** Removing those two
words resolves `H18` by geometry.

**Status is carried by words, never by colour alone**, and never by a coloured
chip, badge or dot. Minimum visible type **14 pt**; principal labels **≥ 18 pt**;
text contrast **≥ 4.5 : 1**; border and connector contrast **≥ 3 : 1**.

## 13. Producer-failure test

**Ask a reader whether every coordination cycle requires verification.** If the
answer is *yes*, **STOP — `H18` has been resolved by the drawing.**

**Ask whether `Ready for Verification` is inside or outside the gate.** If
*inside*, the near-miss placement has failed.

**Read the unresolved warning at projection distance.** If it cannot be read, it
has failed.

## 14. STOP conditions

**Applicable module-wide STOP conditions: 32, 33, 34, 35, 36, 51, 53, 54, 63, 70,
72.**

Stop and refer back to the accepted plan if this visual would:

- Treat `Ready for Verification` as verified **(32)**
- Treat `Closed` as proof of correction, verification, approval, publication,
  acceptance, certification or compliance **(33)**
- **Treat a WIP response as verification (34)**
- **Present verification as mandatory in every cycle (35)**
- **Resolve the `H18` variance (36)**
- Resolve publication or recipient-acceptance authority **(51)**
- Rely on colour alone **(53)**
- Import external imagery or a screenshot **(54)**
- **Show verification, acceptance or closure transferring technical or design
  responsibility away from the originating task team (63)**
- Use a coloured chip, badge, dot or traffic light in place of a status word
  **(70)**
- Name a holder, organisation or appointment — including a named verifier **(72)**
