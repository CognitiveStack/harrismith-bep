## 1. Purpose and five-second takeaway

**Purpose.** Bridge from Module 5, state that allocation is not performance, and
fix the evidence boundary **before** any process content appears.

**Five-second takeaway.** *Module 6 asks what happened — and the route has not
been shown to run.*

**Why this form.** Equal panels make the bridge structural rather than asserted.
A full-width strip beneath both panels states that the qualification applies to
**everything above it** — which a corner caption cannot do.

**Simplification rule.** The two panel texts may compress to short phrases. **The
status strip may never be shortened, moved, reduced below 14 pt, or split into
per-panel notes.** If space is short, **cut panel text, never strip text**.

```text

  ↓

  ↓

  ↓

  ↓
```

**The order runs downward and never upward.** A later package, render or

material conflicts with a controlled Harrismith source, **the controlled source
wins**.

## 6. Geometry or spatial relationships

**Reference canvas: `960 × 540 pt`, 16:9. Origin top left; `x` increases right,
`y` increases down. Title zone `y = 0–72` is reserved. Side margins ≥ 48 pt.**

Panels `y = 96–392`; left `x = 48–476`, right `x = 508–912`; **equal width
≈ 428 pt, gap ≈ 32 pt; the panels never touch.**

Status strip `x = 48–912`, `y = 416–500`, **full width, one visible border,
height ≥ 84 pt** so that four entries sit at ≥ 18 pt.

## 7. Connector semantics

**None between the panels.** An arrow from left to right would read as *Module 5
leads to Module 6 having happened*.

**The strip touches nothing.** Its relationship to the panels is carried by
**full width and position**, not by a connector.

## 8. Status and warning treatment

**Mandatory warning: the four strip entries, all four, together.**

Any three of them read as a milder position than the sources record. The strip
is a **principal visual object**, sized and bordered as such — not small print,
not a footer note, not a caption.

**Status is carried by words, never by colour alone**, and never by a coloured
chip, badge or dot. Minimum visible type **14 pt**; principal labels and all
status text **≥ 18 pt**; text contrast **≥ 4.5 : 1**; border and connector
contrast **≥ 3 : 1**.

## 9. Build or reveal sequence

1. Left panel.
2. Right panel.
3. **Status strip, with all four entries already present.**

The strip is never revealed after a pause, because a pause would let the two
panels be read unqualified.

## 10. Mandatory omissions

- No person, no organisation, no role holder
- No platform image of any kind
- No progress indicator, no tick, no timeline
- No claim that a cycle ran
- No connector anywhere on the slide

## 11. Accessibility and projection requirements

**Reading order:** left panel → right panel → strip, **all four entries**.

**The strip must be read last and in full.** Status words are never colour-only
and never below 18 pt. **The equality of the two panels must be stated in
words** in the alt text, since relative size is invisible to a screen reader.

Warnings remain legible at projection scale. No information is carried by colour
alone.

## 13. Producer-failure test

**Show the finished slide for five seconds to someone who has not heard the
talk, then ask: has the governed route been demonstrated on this project?**

**If the answer is *yes*, *probably*, or *unclear*, the status strip has failed
and the visual must be redrawn.**
