## 1. Purpose and five-second takeaway

**Purpose.** Develop the **decision** between finding and Issue — what an Issue
must carry, what creating one does not establish, and the recorded scope
variance.

**Five-second takeaway.** *Detection produces output. A decision produces an
Issue.*

**Why this form.** A single decision object placed between findings and outcomes
makes *"creating an issue is a decision"* **structural**. Two equal outcomes
prevent the visual from becoming a conveyor in which every finding arrives as an
Issue.

**Simplification rule.** Finding examples may reduce to their check type. **The
decision criterion's seven limbs must remain in full**, **the two outcomes must
remain identical in size and weight**, and **neither Issue scope row may be
dropped, merged or restyled.**

## 7. Connector semantics

**Findings → decision:** one connector per finding group, meaning **`presented
for triage`**.

**Decision → each outcome:** two connectors of **identical weight and style**,
each meaning **`possible triage result`**.

**Neither outcome connector may be heavier, straighter, shorter or more central
than the other.**

**No connector bypasses the decision object.**

## 8. Status and warning treatment

**Mandatory warning: `Clash / finding ≠ Issue`**, placed **between the findings
zone and the decision object**, and the *creating an Issue does not establish…*
line beneath outcome A.

**The geometric equality of the two outcomes is itself the status treatment.**
Any asymmetry decides the question the slide exists to leave open.

**Status is carried by words, never by colour alone**, and never by a coloured
chip, badge or dot. Minimum visible type **14 pt**; principal labels **≥ 18 pt**;
text contrast **≥ 4.5 : 1**; border and connector contrast **≥ 3 : 1**.

## 9. Build or reveal sequence

1. Findings with their type labels.
2. **`Clash / finding ≠ Issue`.**
3. Decision object with its seven limbs.
4. **Both outcomes drawn together, identically.**
5. The two identical result connectors.
6. Issue-scope comparison.
7. Status.

## 10. Mandatory omissions

- **No conveyor**
- **No automatic route from finding to Issue**
- No invented identifier, number, date or priority
- **No preferred Issue scope**
- No platform-native label shown as authoritative
- No colour-coded severity on findings

## 11. Accessibility and projection requirements

**Reading order:** findings with types → the `≠` warning read as *"clash or
finding is not an Issue"* → decision with all seven limbs → **both outcomes, read
as equal** → comparison → status.

**The equality of the two outcomes must be stated in words.**

No information is carried by colour alone. Warnings remain legible at projection
scale.

## 13. Producer-failure test

**Cover the decision object.** Does the slide still read as findings becoming
Issues? If yes, the decision object is not carrying its weight.

**Ask a reader which outcome is normal.** If either is named, the equality has
failed.

**Ask which Issue definition applies.** If a reader picks one, the comparison has
failed.

## 14. STOP conditions

**Applicable module-wide STOP conditions: 6, 7, 26, 53, 54, 58, 68, 69, 70, 71.**

Stop and refer back to the accepted plan if this visual would:

- Collapse Finding, Clash and Issue **(6)**
- **Turn a finding or clash automatically into an Issue (7)**
- **Invent an Issue identifier, due date, target, priority, person or
  organisation (26)**
- Rely on colour alone **(53)**
- Import external imagery or a screenshot **(54)**
- Present a clash or finding as a proven design error or fault **(58)**
- Present the seven Issue types as Autodesk system-native labels or
  platform-provided objects **(68)**
- Harmonise a recorded terminology variance — here **variance 3**, the two Issue
  scopes **(69)**
- Use a coloured chip, badge, dot or traffic light in place of a status word
  **(70)**
- Place a tick, percentage, progress bar, score or completion mark against any
  list **(71)**
