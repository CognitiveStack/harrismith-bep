## 1. Format decision — a composition, deliberately not Mermaid

**Four equal bands are an arrangement, not a graph.** Any Mermaid layout implies
progression, and progression here reads as a **maturity ladder** — which is
prohibition 45.

## 3. Eight mandatory design requirements

1. **All four bands are the same size**, including band 4. A smaller exclusion
   band reads as a footnote; a larger band 2 reads as a result.
2. **Band 1 holds exactly one item**, and **its near-emptiness is the finding**.
   The empty space is deliberate and is **not to be filled, padded or balanced**.
3. **No band is coloured green, amber or red.** Neutral throughout — colour turns
   bands 1 and 4 into pass and fail.
4. **No ticks, no crosses, no score, no percentage, no maturity level.**
5. **The bands are not ordered as a journey.** No arrow, no numbering that
   implies a path, and **vertical stacking must not read as a ranking**.
6. **The licensed-copy note is visible on the slide.**
7. **The slide is labelled `Mapping — not assessment`.**
8. **The conclusion appears verbatim and alone**: **`No conformity conclusion
   supported`**. No qualifier, no adverb, no percentage.

## 4. Forbidden formulations

**None of these may appear, in the visual or in speech:**

`partially compliant` · `not compliant yet` · `mostly aligned` · `ISO-ready` ·
`maturity level` · `percentage aligned`

**Each smuggles in an assessment nobody performed** — prohibitions 41–45.

## 5. Companion panel — the distinctions

**A table, kept off the four bands.** Adding them inside a band would imply they
are items in it.

```text
influence          ≠ implementation
analogue           ≠ requirement satisfaction
documented process ≠ operating process
unresolved         ≠ failed
gap                ≠ non-conformity
mapping            ≠ assessment
```

And the seventh, which closes the slide:

> **Assessment requires an authorised basis, adequate evidence and a competent
> assessor.** Module 3 has none of the three, and **defines no
> conformity-assessment procedure**.

## 6. Simplify and omit

| Simplify | Omit |
|---|---|
| Band 1: one item. Band 2: up to six. Band 3: nine, or five with a count. Band 4: the seven exclusions, condensed | **Ticks. Crosses. Green. Amber. Red. Percentages. Scores. Maturity levels. Progress bars. Progression arrows** |
| One label, one licensed-copy note, one conclusion | **Any ordering from bad to good**, and any dominant alignment band |
| The distinctions as a companion panel | **Any conformity-assessment procedure**, sketched or implied |

## 7. Overclaim risk — and the hardest instruction in this set

**HIGHEST.** A near-empty band 1 beside a nine-item band 3 looks unbalanced, and
every design instinct will try to correct it — by promoting analogues upward, by
shrinking band 3, or by adding a reassuring summary line.

**The imbalance is the content.** If band 1 ever holds more than one item, someone
has made a claim the evidence does not support.

**Deliver the gaps as findings, not as confession.** Nine gaps recited flatly
sound like an apology. They are a project that wrote down what it does not know —
which is rarer and harder than the alternative.
