## 1. Format decision — a composition, deliberately not Mermaid

**Questions have no relationships to draw.** A flow would imply a required order,
and a required order is itself an answer about how a project should proceed.

## 2. Layout specification — five groups, questions only

**▸ marks the compulsory question.** It appears verbatim and is **not answered**.

## 3. Seven mandatory design requirements

1. **Every item ends in a question mark.** If a line can be read as an answer, it
   is rewritten or removed.
2. **The licensed-copy question appears visibly, verbatim** — *"Does a licensed
   copy of the applicable standard need to be obtained, and by whom?"*
3. **No answer column, no response field, no example answer**, not even a greyed
   placeholder. **A placeholder reads as a default.**
4. **No dates, no organisations, no software, no certification marks.**
5. **The end state is shown and is open.** **Not a failure state** — it is the
   correct position for a project that has not started, and it is **not styled as
   a warning**.
6. **Future-facing styling.** The visual reads as a path ahead, not a status
   report. No progress indicator, no completion marker, no stage highlighting.
7. **The three closing takeaways are visually last**, after the questions.
   Nothing follows them.

## 4. The three closing takeaways

| # | |
|---|---|
| 1 | **ISO 19650 concerns information management, not software selection.** |
| 2 | **Principles must become project-specific requirements, authority and evidence.** |
| 3 | **Alignment or conformity must never be claimed beyond the available basis.** |

**The closing message, above or beneath them:**

> ISO alignment is not inherited from a template or a software platform; it must
> be established for the specific project through applicable requirements,
> appointments, authorised processes and evidence.

*(**Teaching synthesis** — `M3-S14-08`.)*

## 6. Simplify and omit

| Simplify | Omit |
|---|---|
| **Five group headings, two or three questions each.** Thirty questions will not fit and should not be attempted | **Every Triviron fact** |
| The licensed-copy question is **compulsory** and always shown | **Any populated answer, response field or greyed example** |
| One end state, three takeaways | Any tick, status colour, progress indicator or certification badge |
| — | Any organisation name, software name or date |

**Groups 4 and 5 stay conceptual.** Modules 4 and 5 develop the detailed
controls, and assurance procedure is Module 6. The questions ask **who decides**
and **what evidence** — never **how to do it**.

## 7. Overclaim risk

**HIGH, and about Triviron rather than about ISO.** The failure mode is not
overclaiming about the standard — it is **inventing a project**. A single
plausible detail here becomes a project assumption downstream, repeated by someone
who did not hear the caveat.

**A wall of thirty questions in sixty seconds reads as a disclaimer**, not a
framework. Show the headings and a few questions; the notes carry the rest.
