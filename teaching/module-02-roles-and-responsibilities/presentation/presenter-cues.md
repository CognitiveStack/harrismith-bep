# Module 2 — Presenter Cues

**Status:** Delivery prompts. **Not a script.** Condensed from
[`../speaker-notes.md`](../speaker-notes.md).

Cues are fragments on purpose. **Speak in your own words** — if you find yourself
reading a cue aloud, it has failed.

The full notes remain available for preparation. These are for the speaker-notes
pane.

**No rehearsal has occurred and no timing has been measured.** All times are
planned allocations.

---

## Slide 1 — Roles make decisions explicit · 2.0 min

**Purpose** — Establish why role clarity matters before naming a single role.

**Cues**
- multidisciplinary work = decisions at the **interfaces**
- those decisions get made *either way*
- unassigned → **assumption · access · habit**
- the alternative: who produced it, who checked it, who authorised it, for what
  purpose
- four questions. answer them and you have roles; can't, and you have habits

**Harrismith reference** — three sourced closures: Architecture is not
automatically the Lead Delivery Party; authority is never inferred upward from
platform configuration; reconfiguring the platform *"does not make itself
legitimate by having been done"*.

**Likely question** — "Isn't this just organisation charts?"
**Safe answer** — No. The BEP's model is explicitly *not an appointment chart and
not an organisation chart*. An org chart says who reports to whom; this says who
is permitted to decide what.

**Transition** — "So — roles. And there's a confusion worth clearing first."

**Delivery warning** — **These are conditions, not misconduct.** Keep the tone
descriptive; everyone in the room has worked this way. And **never present any
route as a recorded Harrismith failure** — the sources record observations, not
failures.

## Slide 2 — Role ≠ job title · 1.0 min

**Purpose** — Separate function from label from person.

**Cues**
- *"functions, not job titles and not people"*
- one person may carry several
- **combining does not merge** them
- must know **which function** at each decision point
- delegation explicit; **platform access is not delegation**

**Harrismith reference** — approving a change as BIM Manager is a different act
from applying it as CDE Administrator — *"even when performed by one person
within a minute of each other."*

**Likely question** — "So who *is* the BIM Manager on Harrismith?"
**Safe answer** — Nobody. Every holder is TBD. Harrismith defines functions and
allocates them; it names no one. Next slide is why that's deliberate.

**Transition** — "A function isn't a title. It's also not an organisation."

**Delivery warning** — **Someone in the room whose title is "BIM Manager" may
hear their authority being reduced.** Depersonalise: the function holds
*something specific*, and specificity protects the holder. Don't drift into
Slides 5–6.

## Slide 3 — Function, organisation, person · 1.0 min

**Purpose** — Three layers, third deliberately empty.

**Cues**
- function → organisation → named person
- Harrismith does the first two and stops
- *"No names are populated"*
- **four** IM functions — not five
- *"a mapping is not an identity"*
- planning state, not a defect — **and** it can't operate

**Harrismith reference** — BEP §5.2: a *conceptual functional model, not an
appointment chart and not an organisation chart*. IM matrix §5: allocating
functions *"does not demonstrate that separate people perform them."*

**Likely question** — "Isn't a framework with no names just theoretical?"
**Safe answer** — Incomplete, and it says so. But the sequence is right — you
agree what has to be decided before arguing about who decides it. What it can't
do is operate.

**Transition** — "So those are the layers. Let's populate the middle one."

**Delivery warning** — **Highest-risk moment in Slides 1–3.** Deliver **both
halves in the same breath** — why functions-first is legitimate *and* what it
can't do. An audience given only the first half concludes the framework is
theoretical. Also: **do not say "five functions"** — the source list has four.

---

## Slide 4 — Who governs the process? · 2.0 min

**Purpose** — Governance distributed by function, each with a boundary.

**Cues**
- **four** IM functions; **nine** matrix roles
- seven different *concerns*, not seven levels
- not a reporting chart — how functions relate, not who reports to whom
- **technical authority sits with the task teams**
- every holder TBD

**Harrismith reference** — BEP §5.7: *"Technical authority sits here, not with
the BIM functions."* §5.4: the Lead Delivery Party is not automatically the BIM
Manager — different concerns, possibly different organisations.

**Likely question** — "Who is actually in charge?"
**Safe answer** — Of this process, nobody — deliberately. The BIM Manager governs
the framework; the Task-Team Lead authorises sharing; nobody currently holds
publication authority. Distributed by function, each one named. That's the
opposite of vague; it just isn't a pyramid.

**Transition** — "Let me start with the one everybody's heard of."

**Delivery warning** — **The audience will mentally reorder the functions into a
hierarchy**, because that's the shape every project diagram they've seen takes.
Never say "under", "above", "reports to" or "escalates to" between functions. Say
what each one *decides*.

## Slide 5 — What the BIM Manager does · 1.5 min

**Purpose** — Positive account, anchored on row G1.

**Cues**
- the principal **governance** function — not the principal project function
- framework · CDE strategy · responsibility architecture · change · assurance
- **row G1: one Perform, four Consult**
- drafting concentrated; agreement distributed
- **G4** — on standards the BIM Manager only *coordinates*; the task-team lead
  performs

**Harrismith reference** — G1: `BM = P`; `AP`, `LDP`, `BC`, `TTL` all `= Cs`.
Those four can't write it and can't be bypassed.

**Likely question** — "Does the BIM Manager approve the models?"
**Safe answer** — No. Design approval isn't allocated to any
information-management function; the originating task team retains technical
responsibility. That's the next slide.

**Transition** — "So that's what it does. Now the more useful half."

**Delivery warning** — **Don't start on the exclusions here.** Slide 6 needs them
intact; pre-empting turns the pair into one defensive slide. And **lead with G1**
— nine responsibilities read as an inventory, and inventories don't persuade.

---

## Slide 6 — What it does not automatically do · 1.5 min

**Purpose** — Correct the assumption of general authority, without marginalising
the function.

**Cues**
- not the appointing party · not the lead delivery party
- not a design approver · not a discipline lead · not a contractual
  decision-maker
- not automatically the platform administrator either
- publication and acceptance — **unresolved for *everyone***
- *"no single universal approver exists"*
- **defined, not diminished**

**Harrismith reference** — IM matrix §3.7: *"unlimited authority is not assigned
to the BIM Manager."* BEP §5.5: outside its authority, the route is *"to raise
and escalate it, not to decide it."*

**Likely question** — "Then what's the point of the role?"
**Safe answer** — Somebody has to maintain the framework, keep the responsibility
architecture coherent, run governance change, and check that what was approved is
what's configured. That's substantial. What it doesn't do is absorb design,
contractual and publication authority because those things touch information.

**Transition** — "Same treatment for the other function people assume they
understand."

**Delivery warning** — **The room's BIM Manager may hear their job downgraded in
public.** Deliver "defined, not diminished" **in the same breath** as the
exclusions, not as a recovery afterwards. Give the protective reading: a defined
boundary is a defence. And **don't let publication authority become a
discussion** — it's Slide 11's subject and opens onto a paused programme.

## Slide 7 — What the BIM Coordinator does · 1.0 min

**Purpose** — Process authority over interfaces; teams keep their models.

**Cues**
- runs the *process* — inputs, federation, checks, triage, assignment,
  monitoring, verification, evidence
- **not every finding becomes an Issue** — triage decision
- **federation doesn't merge ownership**
- row X3: coordinator = *coordinate*; task team = *perform*
- how this **would** work — not evidenced as running

**Harrismith reference** — Coordination §3: does not *"own discipline design
solutions; become technical approver; or acquire publication authority through
coordination."*

**Likely question** — "Can the BIM Coordinator tell the engineer how to fix the
design?"
**Safe answer** — They can facilitate agreement — the strategy says so. What they
can't do is author the solution: *"does not author a discipline solution merely
because they chair coordination."* Chairing isn't designing.

**Transition** — "Which is worth pinning down properly."

**Delivery warning** — **Say "clash detection" and the role gets filed under
software.** Keep the *decisions* in the foreground — readiness, triage,
assignment, verification. **Name no product.** And don't imply any of it is
running: no Coordination Space was observed configured.

## Slide 8 — Coordination ≠ design responsibility · 1.0 min

**Purpose** — Walk one matter through; responsibility crosses and returns.

**Cues**
- finding → triage → assign → **team fixes in its own WIP** → their checker →
  their lead authorises reshare → re-coordinate → verify
- responsibility crossed the lane and came back
- **technical content never left the team that owns it**
- assignment doesn't transfer responsibility
- "fixed in WIP" doesn't close it
- closure isn't design approval

**Harrismith reference** — the strategy's own example, **labelled by the source**
as *"an educational workflow example only"* that *"does not describe an actual
condition on the project"* — STR-01 versus MEC-01, with no geometry, coordinates,
Issue identifier, tolerance or named person.

**Likely question** — "Who owns the technical fix?"
**Safe answer** — The originating task team, throughout. If two teams change,
each remains responsible for its own information. The coordinator owns the
process that got them there.

**Transition** — "Notice how much of that happened inside one task team."

**Delivery warning** — **Say the example's label before using it, not after.**
"This is the strategy's own illustration, and it says it doesn't describe an
actual condition on the project." If asked whether it really happened: the
repository records one mapping discrepancy and no clashes at all.

## Slide 9 — Task-Team Leads, Authors and Checkers · 3.0 min

**Purpose** — The clearest separation in the framework.

**Cues**
- one container, three functions, three acts
- author creates · checker verifies · **a third function authorises**
- *"Authors do not self-promote their own information"*
- read the `Aut` column down: perform, consult, consult, **nothing**
- checking isn't authorising
- **it stops at Shared** — publication authority is unresolved
- combined author/checker: distinction remains, combination **recorded**,
  independence never claimed where it doesn't exist

**Harrismith reference** — rows P1–P4, plus the same rule applied in the delivery
schedule: every TRN-E01 row requires both checks *and* Task-Team Lead
authorisation to share, for **Shared — coordination use only**. Three documents,
one rule.

**Likely questions and safe answers**

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| "Can the Author check their own model?" | Where independence can't reasonably be provided, yes — with conditions. The distinction remains, the combination is recorded, and independence is never claimed where it doesn't exist. |
| "Does Task-Team Lead approval mean it's Published?" | No. It means Shared, for the stated purpose. Authorisation to share is expressly not authorisation to publish — and publication authority is unresolved here. |
| "Is this just RACI?" | No, and the sources say so twice. Seven terms instead of four, because RACI collapses checking with authorising and coordinating with performing — the exact distinctions this slide makes. |

**Transition** — "There's one function left that decides nothing."

**Delivery warning** — **Someone will translate this into RACI out loud.**
Correct it once, cheaply, and move on. **And do not extend the sequence to
Published** — that would invent an authority three sources record as unresolved,
and it's the exact failure this module argues against.

## Slide 10 — CDE Administration implements · 2.0 min

**Purpose** — Separate platform capability from project authority.

**Cues**
- membership · folders · permissions · team spaces · workflow · implementing
  approved change
- all essential; **none of it a decision**
- *"implements governance; it does not create it"*
- **responsibility first, permission after** — not the reverse
- divergence = a **deviation to be recorded**
- *"no user names are specified in this document"*

**Harrismith reference** — one participant may carry both the BIM Manager and CDE
Administrator functions. Same person, full access, holds the authority in one
capacity and not the other — **access was never the thing that decided it**.

**Likely question** — "Does an ACC administrator have publication authority?"
**Safe answer** — No — the BEP names that role specifically among those it isn't
automatically held by. But be precise about the second half: it isn't held by
anyone here. It's unresolved and the transition is blocked. Not "not you, someone
else" — "not yet decided."

**Transition** — "Let me put the whole chain together."

**Delivery warning** — **The administrator in the room may hear a warning about
them.** Frame it as capability versus decision, never trustworthiness — and give
the protective reading: the boundary shields them from being asked to make
governance calls in a permissions dialog. **Show no permissions screen.**

## Slide 11 — Check, authorise, publish, receive, accept · 1.0 min

**Purpose** — Several distinct decisions; two of them held by nobody.

**Cues**
- eight acts; **only two change the information state**
- delivery isn't a state · receipt isn't a state · acceptance isn't a state
- sharing: **task-team lead, established**
- publication: **unresolved → blocked**; information stays Shared
- acceptance: **unresolved**
- "approved" by whom, for what purpose, under what authority?

**Harrismith reference** — BEP §10.11's four terms — Published · Delivered ·
Received · Accepted — with *"Delivery does not prove acceptance. Receipt does not
prove suitability."* And: *"No acceptance authorities are invented."*

**Likely question** — "Isn't checking the same as approving?"
**Safe answer** — No. Checking verifies against a defined requirement and
confirms readiness for the next decision. The decision itself is separate, and
held by a different function.

**Transition** — "Which is a lot of separate authorities."

**Delivery warning** — **A left-to-right chain implies it runs end to end.** Say
"blocked" out loud at that step, not just on the slide. **Resist smoothing the
ending** — a chain that stops is the accurate picture. And don't let publication
authority become a discussion about who *should* hold it.

## Slide 12 — Several distinct authorities · 1.0 min

**Purpose** — Consolidate; answer who may decide each action.

**Cues**
- sixteen kinds of decision
- **ten allocated · two conditional · three unresolved · one outside**
- design approval isn't in this model at all — professional standards, not this
  document
- **no universal approver** — looking for one is the mistake
- a name in the gap wouldn't resolve it; it would hide it

**Harrismith reference** — IM matrix §3.7: *"No single universal approver
exists"*, and *"unlimited authority is not assigned to the BIM Manager."* Plus
§6 — a seven-row unresolved register, published as part of the approved document.

**Likely question** — "Who is the final approver?"
**Safe answer** — There isn't one, deliberately. Different decisions need
different authorities. A project looking for one usually ends up with someone
signing things they have no basis to judge.

**Transition** — "You'll have noticed something running through all of that."

**Delivery warning** — **Sixteen rows read as bureaucracy** at exactly the moment
the model comes together. **Don't read the table.** Give the distribution — ten,
two, three, one — and one row that matters: design approval, outside the model.

## Slide 13 — Functions but not holders · 1.0 min

**Purpose** — Framework versus implemented appointment structure. Both halves.

**Cues**
- definitions, grammar, boundaries, allocations — **and a register of what's
  open**
- no named holder · no appointed organisation · no signatory
- functions-first is legitimate: agree the work while the team is forming
- **but** — accountability, permissions, escalation and evidence all need a name
- defined, yes. operating, not yet. **and it says so**

**Harrismith reference** — BEP §5.2: *"No names are populated."* §6.9, in the
access section: *"No user names are specified in this document."* Against that,
§5.1's requirement — who produced it, who checked it, who authorised it, for what
purpose. Four questions that need people.

**Likely question** — "Why define roles before assigning people?"
**Safe answer** — Because assigning people first makes the argument about
individuals rather than about what the project needs. Define the function, agree
the boundary while the team's forming, then map appointments onto something
stable. What you can't do is stay there.

**Transition** — "Which brings me to what this means for us."

**Delivery warning** — **Highest-risk moment in Slides 10–14, and it fails in two
opposite directions.** Deliver both halves together: only the gaps and the
audience discounts nineteen minutes; only the establishment list and it reads as
a working system. **Documenting a function is not operating it** — say it.

---

## Slide 14 — What must Triviron assign? · 1.0 min

**Purpose** — The model as a startup checklist, in questions.

**Cues**
- seven stages, and **the order matters**
- contractual authority → IM functions → discipline functions
- **then allocate each decision authority separately**
- **then** configure permissions — fifth, not first
- named holders and deputies; who carries two functions
- who verifies it's actually happening
- every question comes from something Harrismith left open

**Harrismith reference** — the stage-5 ordering is BEP §6.9's rule, not a
preference: *"the responsibility comes first, and the permission follows it."*

**Likely questions and safe answers**

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| "Who accepts the delivery?" | On Harrismith, nobody — acceptance authority is unresolved and depends on a delivery arrangement that doesn't exist. On a real project it follows the appointment, which is question one. |
| "Can one person perform several functions?" | Yes, and the BEP expects it at this size. Conditions: the functions don't merge, the person knows which they're exercising, delegation is explicit, and any independence limitation is recorded. |
| "Can we copy these assignments to Triviron?" | There are no assignments to copy — every holder is TBD. What transfers is the model: the functions, the grammar, the boundaries, and the discipline of recording what's open. |

**Transition** — None. Final slide. Takeaways, then questions.

**Delivery warning** — **The audience wants a recommendation and you have a
checklist.** The pressure to say "I'd suggest X takes the BIM manager role" is
real, and it's the most damaging thing available here — a suggestion made in a
teaching session gets repeated afterwards as a decision. **No Triviron facts
exist.** The questions *are* the deliverable. And **don't let stage 5 slip
earlier in the telling** — the ordering is the practical payload.

## Fallbacks

### 30-second opening fallback

> "This is about who does what on a project's information — and more usefully,
> who's *allowed to decide* what. Every multidisciplinary project has decisions
> sitting at the interfaces: is this ready, has it been checked, can we rely on
> it. Those get answered either way — deliberately, or by whoever has access and
> a habit. I'll use the Harrismith Fire Station as the worked example. It's a
> training implementation, so nobody's actually appointed to anything — which
> turns out to make the functions easier to see."

### 30-second closing fallback

> "Three things to take away.
>
> Roles describe functions, not prestige and not job titles. A title doesn't tell
> you what someone's allowed to decide on your project.
>
> Authority has to match the specific decision. There's no universal approver,
> and a project looking for one usually finds someone signing things they've no
> basis to judge.
>
> And permissions follow the responsibility model, not the reverse. Decide who's
> responsible, then configure the software to match.
>
> Harrismith gives us a functional model. Triviron has to assign the
> organisations, the people and the authorities to it before we rely on it in
> delivery. That's the work."

### 10-second fallback closing

> "Roles are functions, not titles. Authority matches the decision — there's no
> universal approver. And permissions follow responsibility, never the reverse.
> Harrismith gives us the model; Triviron has to assign people and authority to
> it."

### Recovery sentences — one per block, if running over

| Block | Recovery sentence |
|---|---|
| 1 — Why role clarity matters | "Those interface decisions get made either way; the only choice is whether you make them on purpose." |
| 2 — Function, title, organisation, person | "A role is a function, not a title and not a person — and Harrismith defines the functions while leaving every holder open." |
| 3 — The distributed governance model | "Four information-management functions, nine roles, seven different concerns — and no top." |
| 4 — BIM Manager and Coordinator boundaries | "Both functions are defined rather than general: the manager governs the framework, the coordinator runs the process, and neither approves design." |
| 5 — Task-team separation of duties | "Author performs, checker checks, task-team lead authorises — and the chain stops at Shared." |
| 6 — CDE Administration and permission | "Responsibility comes first and permission follows it; changing the software doesn't make a decision." |
| 7 — Authority chain and unresolved authorities | "Ten authorities allocated, three unresolved, one outside the model — and no universal approver." |
| 8 — Assigning the model to Triviron | "Harrismith's open questions are Triviron's required decisions." |

**Recovery compresses; it does not skip.** Blocks 7 and 8 are the payoff — if
time is short, recover Block 3 or Block 6 and protect Slides 13 and 14.
