## 3. Developed slides — initial teaching baseline

### Slide 1 — BIM roles exist to make decisions explicit

**Purpose of the slide**

Establish why role clarity matters *before* naming a single role. Show that
multidisciplinary work contains decisions at the interfaces, and that unassigned
authority does not produce no decision — it produces an accidental one.

**The argument, in three steps**

| Step | |
|---|---|
| 1 | Multidisciplinary delivery contains **decisions at the interfaces** — not just work |
| 2 | Those decisions get made either way |
| 3 | If nobody is assigned, they are made by **assumption, access or habit** |

**Central message**

> If responsibility is not assigned deliberately, the project still makes
> decisions — but through assumption, access or habit.

*(Teaching wording. Consistent with BEP §1.5, §4.8 and §5.9, but no source
sentence says it.)*

**What "deliberate assignment" actually requires** — sourced, and the strongest
line available for this slide:

> For any container it must be answerable who produced it, who checked it, who
> authorised it and for what purpose.

*(BEP §5.1 — "Responsibility must be traceable")*

And the aim it serves (BEP §1.1): each information container has **a known
originator, a known state, a known route to the next state, and a known role
accountable for moving it there**.

**The three accidental routes**

| Route | What it looks like | What the source says |
|---|---|---|
| **Assumption** | "The architect usually leads that" | Architecture is **not automatically** the Lead Delivery Party — an observation about files is not an appointment (BEP §5.4) |
| **Access** | "I could write to that folder, so I did" | **Authority is never inferred upward from platform configuration** (BEP §1.5) |
| **Habit** | "That's how we did it last time" | Reconfiguring a team, space, permission or mapping **does not constitute a governance decision, and does not make itself legitimate by having been done** (BEP §4.8) |

**Honesty note for this slide.** These are generic conditions of
multidisciplinary projects. **None is presented as a recorded Harrismith
failure** — the sources record observations, not failures, and the only recorded
discrepancy is UD-001, which is a team-space mapping observation.

**Harrismith introduced here** as the worked example: a training and reference
implementation with a complete governed documentation set, used because it makes
the reasoning explicit.

### Slide 2 — A role is not the same as a job title

**Purpose of the slide**

Separate the *function* from the *label on someone's email signature*, and
locate authority in the project's agreed arrangements rather than in the title.

**The sourced statement that carries the slide**

> These are functions, not job titles and not people.

*(BEP §4.6 — quotable verbatim)*

**Four consequences**

| # | |
|---|---|
| 1 | **One participant may carry more than one function.** In a larger project each might be carried by a different organisation |
| 2 | **Combining functions does not merge them.** The responsibilities remain distinct in meaning |
| 3 | A participant carrying two functions **must know which one they are performing at each decision point** |
| 4 | **Delegation must be explicit** — stated, scoped and recorded. **Platform access is not delegation** |

**The worked example — grounded, and better than any invented one**

BEP §5.11's own illustration:

> Approving a governance change as **BIM Manager** is a different act from
> applying it as **CDE Administrator**, even when performed by one person within
> a minute of each other.

That is one person, one minute, two functions, two different authorities. It
makes the point faster than any diagram.

**The boundary example** — BEP §5.5 and §5.4:

- The BIM Manager is **not automatically** the Appointing Party.
- The BIM Manager is **not automatically** the Lead Delivery Party.
- The Lead Delivery Party is **not automatically** the BIM Manager — *"different
  functions with different concerns, and may sit in different organisations."*

**Teaching framing, marked as such:** the same job title may carry different
authority on different projects. The sources address one project, so this is the
presenter's observation, not theirs.

**No person is named on this slide.** No holder is established anywhere in the
Harrismith set.

### Slide 3 — Function, organisation and named person

**Purpose of the slide**

Show three layers, and explain why Harrismith populates the first two and
deliberately leaves the third empty.

**The three layers**

| Layer | What it is | Harrismith's position |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Function** | What must be done or decided | **Established.** Four IM functions at organisation level; nine functional roles in the matrix |
| **2 — Organisation or party** | Who is responsible contractually or organisationally | **Party categories defined; no organisation appointed.** Training organisation model under TA-03 |
| **3 — Named role holder** | The individual assigned to perform the function | **TBD throughout.** *"No names are populated"* |

**The four IM functions** (BEP §4.6) — note it is **four**, not five:

| Function | Concern |
|---|---|
| BIM Manager | Governance of the information-management framework |
| BIM Coordinator | Operational multidisciplinary coordination |
| CDE Administration | Implementation of approved governance in the platform |
| Lead Delivery Party | Project-level delivery coordination |

**Five concepts deliberately not interchangeable** (BEP §4.2) — the sharpest
version of this slide's point:

| Concept | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Party** | An organisation |
| **Task team** | The group producing a defined package of information |
| **Discipline** | A technical classification of information |
| **Autodesk collaboration team** | A platform construct |
| **IM role** | A governance function |

> These may map to one another — often they do — but **a mapping is not an
> identity**. Each mapping is a decision to be recorded, not an assumption to be
> inherited.

**Why the third layer is empty, and why that is not a defect**

The BEP states it plainly: the role model is a **conceptual functional model, not
an appointment chart and not an organisation chart**, and *"role holders are TBD
throughout Section 5."*

Two things follow, and both must be said:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Why it is useful** | Functions can be defined, allocated and reviewed before anyone is appointed. The framework can be agreed while the team is still forming — which is exactly when a BEP is written |
| **What it does not do** | It does not run a project. **Implementation eventually requires named holders**, and Harrismith records that it has not reached that point |

**A caution the matrix itself supplies** (IM matrix §5): allocating functions to
roles *"does not demonstrate that separate people perform them, and it must not
be read as evidence of independence."*

### Slide 4 — Who governs the project information process? · 2.0 min

**Purpose of the slide**

Introduce the principal project functions **without drawing an organisational
hierarchy** — and show that governance is distributed across defined functions,
with no single role owning every decision.

**The four information-management functions** (BEP §4.6) — established at
organisation level, and it is **four**, not five:

| Function | Concern |
|---|---|
| **BIM Manager** | Governance of the information-management framework |
| **BIM Coordinator** | Operational multidisciplinary coordination |
| **CDE Administration** | Implementation of approved governance in the platform |
| **Lead Delivery Party** | Project-level delivery coordination |

**The nine functional roles in the responsibility matrix** (IM matrix §2) — the
wider set the process actually needs:

| Column | Role | Holder |
|---|---|---|
| `AP` | Owner / Appointing Party | **Not established — TBD** |
| `LDP` | Lead Delivery Party | **Not established — TBD** |
| `BM` | BIM Manager | **TBD** |
| `BC` | BIM Coordinator | **TBD** |
| `TTL` | Task-Team Lead | **TBD** for every task team |
| `Aut` | Author | **TBD** |
| `Chk` | Checker | **TBD** |
| `CDE` | CDE Administration | **TBD** |
| `Rcp` | Receiving / recipient function | **Not established** |

**Seven kinds of concern, deliberately separated**

| Concern | Held by |
|---|---|
| **Project authority** | Owner / Appointing Party — identity **not established** |
| **Information-management governance** | BIM Manager |
| **Technical production** | Author, within the task team |
| **Technical checking** | Checker |
| **Coordination** | BIM Coordinator |
| **Platform administration** | CDE Administration |
| **Recipient acceptance** | Receiving / recipient function — authority **UNRESOLVED** |

**This is not a reporting hierarchy, and the slide must not draw one.** BEP §5.2
states the role model is *"a conceptual functional model, not an appointment
chart and not an organisation chart. It shows how information-management
functions relate to one another, not who reports to whom contractually."*

**Two consequences worth stating aloud**

| | |
|---|---|
| **The BIM functions are not above the discipline leads technically** | BEP §5.7: *"Technical authority sits here, not with the BIM functions."* Neither the BIM Manager nor the BIM Coordinator acquires technical responsibility by governing or coordinating the information that describes it |
| **The Lead Delivery Party is not the BIM Manager** | BEP §5.4: different functions with different concerns, which *"may sit in different organisations"* |

**Required message**

> BIM governance is distributed by function, but each function must still have a
> defined boundary.

*(Supported interpretation. Both halves are sourced — §4.6 and §5.2 establish
distribution by function; IM matrix §4's Holds / Does not hold table establishes
the boundaries — but not in this sentence.)*

### Slide 5 — What does the BIM Manager actually do? · 1.5 min

**Purpose of the slide**

Give a clear, positive account of the function **before** its limits. Slide 6
carries the limits; this slide must not pre-empt them or the pairing collapses
into a single defensive slide.

**What the source allocates** (BEP §5.5)

| The BIM Manager |
|---|
| Maintains the BEP governance framework |
| Maintains the CDE strategy |
| Coordinates information standards |
| Maintains the responsibility architecture |
| Coordinates delivery-planning governance |
| Manages governance decisions and controlled changes |
| Supports onboarding and BIM capability development |
| Performs governance assurance |
| Checks that approved governance is reflected in platform and process configuration |

**Where the matrix puts it** (IM matrix §3.1, §3.2, §3.7)

| Row | Function | BM |
|---|---|---|
| G1 | Maintain the BEP governance framework | **P** |
| G2 | Manage governance decisions and the change process | **P Co** |
| G3 | Maintain the IM responsibility architecture | **P** |
| G4 | Maintain and coordinate project information standards | **Co** *(Task-Team Lead performs)* |
| G5 | Provide BIM onboarding and capability support | **P** |
| C1 | Define the CDE governance strategy | **P** |
| A1 | Assess governance change | **P Co** |
| A5 | Retain decision and change evidence | **P** |
| C4 / A4 | Verify configuration / implementation after change | **Ck** |

**G4 is worth pausing on.** Even on project information standards, the BIM
Manager only **coordinates** — the Task-Team Lead is marked `P Cs`. The function
is narrower than its title suggests, and the matrix says so.

**Required worked example — row G1**

| # | Function | AP | LDP | BM | BC | TTL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | Maintain the BEP governance framework | `Cs` | `Cs` | **`P`** | `Cs` | `Cs` |

**One Perform, four Consult.** The BIM Manager maintains the framework. The
Appointing Party, Lead Delivery Party, BIM Coordinator and every Task-Team Lead
must be **asked**. They do not write it; they cannot be bypassed.

That single row is the whole slide: **drafting is concentrated, agreement is
distributed.**

**Required message**

> The BIM Manager maintains the information-management framework; the project's
> other functions supply, review, authorise and implement the decisions within
> it.

*(Supported interpretation. Assembled from G1's Perform/Consult pattern, §5.5's
responsibility list, and the separate allocation of authorisation and
implementation elsewhere in the matrix.)*

### Slide 6 — What the BIM Manager does not automatically do · 1.5 min

**Purpose of the slide**

Correct the assumption that "BIM Manager" means general project authority — and
do it without making the function sound marginal.

**Every exclusion below was verified in the source before use.**

| Not automatically | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|
| The **Appointing Party** | BEP §5.5; IM matrix §4 | **DIRECT** |
| The **Lead Delivery Party** | BEP §5.5, §5.4; IM matrix §4 | **DIRECT** |
| A **design approver** | BEP §5.5; IM matrix §4; Coordination §3 — *"Does not automatically approve technical design"* | **DIRECT** |
| A **discipline technical lead** | BEP §5.5 | **DIRECT** |
| A **contractual decision-maker** | BEP §5.5; IM matrix §4 | **DIRECT** |
| The **publication authority** | BEP §9.7 — expressly not automatically the BIM Manager; IM matrix D4 `TBD` | **DIRECT — and UNRESOLVED for everyone** |
| The **recipient acceptance authority** | BEP §9.8; IM matrix D7 `TBD Ac` | **DIRECT — and UNRESOLVED for everyone** |
| The **universal governance approver** | IM matrix §3.7 A2 note — *"No single universal approver exists"*, and *"unlimited authority is not assigned to the BIM Manager"*; BEP §12.7 | **DIRECT** |
| The **platform administrator** | BEP §4.6 lists CDE Administration as a separate function; §5.11 gives BIM Manager + CDE Administrator as an example of two functions one participant *may* carry | **DIRECT that they are separate functions** |
| **Responsible for every model** | Model matrix §3.1 allocates each container to an originating task team; BEP §5.7 — *"Technical authority sits here, not with the BIM functions"* | **INTERPRETATION** — the sources allocate containers elsewhere rather than excluding the BIM Manager by name |

**Two-column structure for the slide**

| The BIM Manager function | Not automatically held |
|---|---|
| Maintains the BEP governance framework | Design approval |
| Maintains the CDE strategy | Contractual decisions |
| Maintains the responsibility architecture | Publication / exchange authority — **unresolved** |
| Manages governance decisions and change | Recipient acceptance — **unresolved** |
| Performs governance assurance | The Appointing Party or Lead Delivery Party function |

**Give both columns equal weight.** A smaller right-hand column reads as a
caveat; it is half the teaching.

**Required message**

> Expertise in BIM does not create contractual, technical or publication
> authority.

*(Teaching synthesis. The individual exclusions are directly sourced; this
sentence generalising them is not source wording.)*

**Delivery boundary.** The point is **not** that the function has little
authority. It is that its authority is **defined rather than assumed** — which
protects the holder as much as it constrains them. A BIM Manager who is not the
design approver cannot be blamed for a design decision.

**Where the function meets its own limit** (BEP §5.5): where the BIM Manager
identifies a problem outside the function's authority, *"the route is to raise
and escalate it, not to decide it."*

### Slide 7 — What does the BIM Coordinator do? · 1.0 min

**Purpose of the slide**

Define the coordination function as **process authority over interfaces** — and
establish, before Slide 8 argues it, that this is not ownership of design.

**What the source allocates** (BEP §5.6; Coordination Strategy §3)

| The BIM Coordinator |
|---|
| Organises coordination inputs and identifies required inputs |
| Manages federation |
| Manages and coordinates checks; runs or coordinates clash and interface review |
| Triages findings |
| Creates and coordinates managed Issues **where required** |
| Coordinates assignment; monitors assigned actions |
| Coordinates re-review and re-coordination |
| Verifies the coordination disposition |
| Escalates blockers and unresolved interface problems |
| Retains and reports coordination evidence |

**Where the matrix puts it** (IM matrix §3.5)

| Row | Function | BC |
|---|---|---|
| X1 | Organise coordination inputs | **P Co** |
| X2 | Perform / manage the multidisciplinary coordination process | **P Co** |
| X3 | Resolve technical coordination issue | `Co` *(Task-Team Lead and Author perform)* |
| X4 | Verify coordination resolution / process disposition | **Ck** |
| X5 | Escalate unresolved multidisciplinary interfaces | **P** |

**X3 is the row that matters.** The BIM Coordinator only **coordinates**
technical resolution. The Task-Team Lead and Author **perform** it.

**Six things separated, in one line each**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Coordination management** | The process — inputs, federation, checks, triage, assignment, verification |
| **Technical design** | The originating task team's; never the coordinator's |
| **Model authoring** | The Author, in the team's own WIP |
| **Task-team checking** | The Checker, against a defined requirement |
| **Authorisation for reshare** | The Task-Team Lead |
| **Design approval** | **Allocated to no information-management function** |

**Three cautions the slide must carry** — all direct source wording:

- **Not every finding becomes an Issue.** Creating one is a decision taken at
  triage, not an automatic consequence of detection.
- **Federation does not merge ownership.** `COORD-01` does not merge authorship,
  transfer technical ownership, create a new design author, or replace the
  discipline containers.
- The BIM Coordinator does **not** own discipline design solutions, become
  technical approver, or acquire publication authority through coordination.

**Required message**

> The BIM Coordinator manages the coordination process; the originating task
> teams remain responsible for their technical solutions.

*(Directly supported. BEP §5.6: "The originating task team retains
responsibility for its technical solution, before and after coordination."
Coordination §3 and §18 say the same from two other directions.)*

### Slide 8 — Coordination responsibility is not design responsibility · 1.0 min

**Purpose of the slide**

Make the boundary memorable by walking one matter end to end and showing where
responsibility sits at every step.

**The eight-step lifecycle** — compressed from the Coordination Strategy §17
cycle, which runs sixteen steps:

| # | Step | Held by |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Coordination finding identified | Whoever performs the check |
| 2 | Matter triaged — does it become a managed Issue? | **BIM Coordinator** |
| 3 | Relevant task team assigned | **BIM Coordinator** coordinates the assignment |
| 4 | Technical response developed **in the team's own WIP** | **Originating task team** |
| 5 | Revised information checked | **Checker**, within the team |
| 6 | Controlled reshare authorised | **Task-Team Lead** |
| 7 | Re-coordination | **BIM Coordinator** |
| 8 | Resolution verified — or escalated | **BIM Coordinator** verifies the *disposition* |

**Responsibility crosses the lane twice and comes back.** Steps 2–3 are the
coordinator's, 4–6 are the task team's, 7–8 return to the coordinator. The
technical content never leaves the originating team.

**The five boundaries, stated plainly**

| # | |
|---|---|
| 1 | **The BIM Coordinator does not design the fix.** *"An Issue assigned to a task team does not make the BIM Coordinator responsible for designing the fix"* |
| 2 | **Assigning an Issue does not transfer technical responsibility.** Where multiple teams must change, *"each remains responsible for its own information"* |
| 3 | **"Fixed in WIP" does not justify closure.** *"A material Issue is not closed solely because someone says it was fixed in WIP"* — closure follows re-coordination against reshared, controlled information, because *"a change nobody can see in Shared information has not been demonstrated"* |
| 4 | **Closing an Issue is not design approval** |
| 5 | **Verification is not technical certification.** *"Verification does not equal design approval, professional certification, publication authority, or recipient acceptance"* |

**The worked example — the source's own, and labelled by the source**

Coordination Strategy §27 carries an **"educational workflow example only"**,
which states expressly that it *"does not describe an actual condition on the
project."* It is safe to use precisely because it was constructed under that
label:

> A mechanical service route conflicts with structural information —
> **STR-01** versus **MEC-01**.

**Say the label out loud.** The source records that the example contains *"no
actual project geometry, no clash coordinates, no Issue identifier, no tolerance
value, and no named person."* Present it the same way. **This is not a recorded
live Harrismith clash.**

The detail worth keeping: *if only MEC-01 changes, TRN-E02-MEC activates and
TRN-E02-STR does not.* Only affected containers reshare.

**Required message**

> The coordinator owns the process; the task team owns the technical response.

*(Supported interpretation. Both halves are directly sourced — Coordination §3
and §18, BEP §5.6 — but the paired formulation is teaching wording.)*

### Slide 9 — Task-Team Leads, Authors and Checkers · 3.0 min

**Purpose of the slide**

The clearest separation of duties in the whole Harrismith framework, and the
module's load-bearing example. Three functions, one container, three different
acts.

**The three roles**

| Role | Does | Does not |
|---|---|---|
| **Author** | Creates or modifies information in the originating task team's WIP; complies with project conventions; responds to assigned comments and issues; remains responsible for authorship | **Does not self-authorise.** *"Authors do not self-promote their own information"* |
| **Checker** | Verifies information against the **defined checking requirement**; records or provides evidence of checking; identifies deficiencies before controlled progression | **Does not authorise.** *"Checking is not authorising"* |
| **Task-Team Lead** | **Authorises information to progress from WIP to Shared**; ensures required checking occurs; owns the team's technical response and interfaces | **Does not become the author** by authorising. Does not hold project-level delivery or publication authority |

**The process model** — source-supported, and it stops where the source stops:

```text
Author performs
  → Checker checks
  → Task-Team Lead authorises controlled sharing
  → Information enters Shared for the defined purpose
```

**The sequence does not continue to Published.** Publication authority is
**UNRESOLVED**, and the transition is recorded as blocked. Drawing a fourth arrow
would invent an authority the sources refuse to assign.

**The worked example — IM matrix rows P1 to P4**

| # | Function | `Aut` | `Chk` | `TTL` | `BC` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Author information in WIP | **`P`** | — | `Co` | — |
| P2 | Perform task-team technical / content check | `Cs` | **`Ck`** | `Co Ck` | — |
| P3 | Perform information-quality / readiness check | `Cs` | **`Ck`** | `Co` | `Cs` |
| P4 | Authorise WIP information for controlled sharing | **—** | `Cs` | **`Au`** | `In` |

**Read the `Aut` column down.** Perform, consult, consult, **nothing**. The
Author does not appear in the authorisation row at all.

**And the delivery schedule applies the same rule** (Information Delivery
Schedule §3.2, every TRN-E01 row):

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Checking Requirement | Task-team technical / content check **and** information-quality / readiness check |
| Authorisation Requirement | **Task-Team Lead authorisation to share** |
| State / Suitability | **Shared — coordination use only** |

Three documents, one rule, no duplication.

**The four distinctions**

| | Why they are not the same |
|---|---|
| **Perform ≠ Check** | Producing information is not verifying it against a requirement |
| **Check ≠ Authorise** | *"Checking confirms readiness for the next controlled decision"* — the progression itself is a separate decision |
| **Authorise for Shared ≠ Publish** | *"Authorisation to share is not authorisation to publish or exchange."* Those are separate decisions with wider consequences |
| **Publish ≠ recipient acceptance** | Acceptance is a separate act by an identified recipient — and that authority is **unresolved** |

**On combining Author and Checker** (BEP §5.8, §9.12; IM matrix §5) — allowed on
a project this size, with three conditions:

- the functional distinction remains — a self-check is still a checking act
  against a defined requirement;
- the combination is **recorded**, so the limitation is visible in the evidence;
- **"Independence is never claimed where it does not exist."** Overstating
  independence is worse than lacking it, because it removes the reader's ability
  to judge the information's reliability.

**Required message**

> Separation of duties prevents information from becoming reliable merely
> because its author says it is ready.

*(Supported interpretation. BEP §5.8 states the principle — progression requires
the checking and authorisation defined by governance, "not the author's
confidence in their work" — but not in this sentence.)*

**This is not RACI, and the slide must not say it is.** BEP §5.12 and IM matrix
§1 both state that RACI is **not adopted**, and both give the same reason: RACI
collapses checking from authorising and coordinating from performing — the exact
distinctions this slide exists to make. Seven terms are used instead of four.
