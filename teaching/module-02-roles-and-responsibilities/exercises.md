# Module 2 — Exercises

**Status:** Initial exercise set for Slides 1–3. Teaching material; **not
governance.**

Rehearsal exercises for the presenter, not assessments for an audience. Do them
aloud, alone, before delivering to anyone. Exercise 8 is done after each of the
others.

---

## Exercise 1 — Why role clarity matters, without "chain of command"

**Task.** Explain why BIM role clarity matters, in under a minute, **without
using the phrase "chain of command"** — and without "hierarchy", "reporting
line", "org chart" or "who's the boss".

**Why this exercise.** The command metaphor is the wrong shape. It implies
seniority decides things; on a multidisciplinary project, defined authority
does. The BEP is explicit that its role model is *not* an appointment or
organisation chart, and the metaphor smuggles one in.

**What good looks like:** you describe *decisions at interfaces* and what happens
when nobody has been assigned to make them — assumption, access or habit.

**Self-check**

- [ ] Did I get through it with no banned term?
- [ ] Did I talk about **decisions**, not about seniority?
- [ ] Did I name at least two of the three accidental routes?
- [ ] Would a project manager with no BIM background follow it?

**Common failure:** substituting "governance structure" and carrying on. Same
sentence, different costume.

---

## Exercise 2 — Role versus job title

**Task.** Explain the difference between a role and a job title. Thirty seconds.

**Why this exercise.** It is Slide 2's whole content, and it is the first thing
an audience with mixed titles will test.

**What good looks like:** a role is a defined project function; a job title is a
label an organisation gives a person. One participant may carry several
functions, and carrying two does not merge them.

**Reference.** BEP §4.6, verbatim: *"These are functions, not job titles and not
people."*

**Self-check**

- [ ] Did I define the role as a **function**?
- [ ] Did I say that one person may hold several?
- [ ] Did I make clear that combining does not merge?
- [ ] Did I avoid naming anyone?

**Common failure:** "a role is what you actually do; a title is what you're
called." Close, but it misses the point — the role is what you are **permitted to
decide**, which is not the same as what you do.

## Exercise 3 — Function, organisation and person, in one example

**Task.** Explain the three layers using **one** practical example. Ninety
seconds.

**Why this exercise.** Three abstractions need one concrete thread, or the
audience holds three empty boxes.

**What good looks like:** pick one function — *authorisation to share* is the
best available — and run it down the layers. The **function** is authorising a
container to move from WIP to Shared. The **organisation** is the party whose
task team produced it. The **named holder** is the Task-Team Lead — **TBD on
Harrismith**.

**Self-check**

- [ ] Did I use exactly one example throughout?
- [ ] Did all three layers appear distinctly?
- [ ] Did I say the holder is TBD without implying the framework is broken?
- [ ] Did I avoid inventing a company or a person?

**Common failure:** switching examples between layers — a function from one
context, a party from another. The thread is the teaching.

## Exercise 4 — "Is the BIM Manager in charge of BIM?"

**Task.** Answer it. Forty-five seconds.

**Why this exercise.** It arrives in almost every session, it sounds harmless,
and answering "yes" undoes Slides 5–6 before you reach them.

**What good looks like:** the BIM Manager is the principal **governance**
function — the framework, the CDE strategy, standards coordination, the
responsibility architecture, governance change, assurance. And then the boundary,
which is where the real answer lives: **not automatically** a design approver, a
discipline technical lead, a contractual decision-maker, the Appointing Party or
the Lead Delivery Party.

**Reference.** BEP §5.5: the function governs *how information is managed*;
authority over technical design, appointment and contract lies elsewhere **and is
not acquired by holding this function**. IM matrix §3.7: *"unlimited authority is
not assigned to the BIM Manager."*

**Self-check**

- [ ] Did I answer the boundary as well as the content?
- [ ] Did I name at least three things it is *not* automatically?
- [ ] Did I say what happens when the function finds a problem outside its
      authority? (Raise and escalate — not decide.)
- [ ] Did I resist making it sound like a diminished role?

**Common failure:** "yes, they run BIM on the project." That is the sentence the
module exists to prevent, and it will be quoted back.

## Exercise 5 — Name one function whose holder is still TBD

**Task.** Name one, and say what that means in practice.

**Why this exercise.** It tests whether you can state an incompleteness plainly
rather than glossing it.

**What good looks like:** any of them — every project role holder on Harrismith is
TBD. Appointing Party, Lead Delivery Party, BIM Manager, BIM Coordinator,
Task-Team Leads, Authors, Checkers, CDE Administration. What it means in practice:
the function is defined and allocated, but no one is appointed to perform it, so
the workflow cannot be operated.

**Self-check**

- [ ] Did I use the exact source term for the function?
- [ ] Did I say what the consequence is, not just that it is TBD?
- [ ] Did I avoid suggesting a plausible holder?
- [ ] Did I present it as a planning state rather than a failure?

**Common failure:** picking one and implying the others are settled. **All** of
them are TBD.

## Exercise 6 — Name one unresolved authority

**Task.** Name one authority that remains unresolved, and say why it matters.

**Why this exercise.** Unresolved *authority* is a sharper problem than an
unnamed holder — a function without a holder waits; an authority without a
resolution **blocks**.

**What good looks like:** four are available.

| Authority | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **Publication / exchange** | The transition from Shared to Published cannot be authorised at all — it is recorded as blocked |
| **Recipient acceptance** | No acceptance workflow is defined; no appointing party is established |
| **Governance change, by class** | No single universal approver exists; the authority for each class is TBD |
| **Implementation verification** | No single universal verifier is defined; it varies by change type |

**Self-check**

- [ ] Did I distinguish an unresolved **authority** from a TBD **holder**?
- [ ] Did I say what it prevents?
- [ ] Did I resist naming who "should" hold it?
- [ ] For publication authority, did I note it is expressly **not** automatically
      the BIM Manager, BIM Coordinator, CDE Administrator or Architect?

**Common failure:** offering a plausible resolution to be helpful. That is the
exact failure the sources refuse: assigning a plausible authority to make a row
look finished manufactures governance that does not exist.

## Exercise 7 — Access without decision authority

**Task.** Explain why a software administrator may have full access and no
decision authority. One minute, to someone who administers the CDE.

**Why this exercise.** It is Slide 10's content, it lands close to home, and it
is easy to deliver as an accusation.

**What good looks like:** CDE Administration **implements approved governance; it
does not create it.** Platform access confers the technical ability to change
something and nothing more — not authority to share, publish, technically approve
or accept. Access is configured to *follow* approved responsibility:
responsibility comes first, permission follows.

**Self-check**

- [ ] Did I state the direction — responsibility first, permission after?
- [ ] Did I name at least two authorities that access does **not** confer?
- [ ] Did I say what happens when access and responsibility diverge? (Recorded as
      a **deviation**.)
- [ ] Did I keep it about the *capability* rather than about the person?

**Common failure:** framing it as a restriction on the administrator. It is not a
limit on them; it is a statement about where authority comes from — and it
protects them, because a configuration they applied under an approved decision is
not theirs to defend.

## Exercise 8 — Self-assessment: skill or authority?

**Task.** After each exercise above, review what you actually said. Did you
confuse technical capability with governance authority?

| If the explanation leaned on | It was really about | What that means |
|---|---|---|
| Who knows the software, who is most experienced, who is senior | **Technical skill or seniority** | You explained competence. Authority is a different thing, and the audience learned the wrong one |
| Who has permissions, who can access what | **Technical capability** | You explained the platform. Permission is not authority |
| Who is permitted to decide, against what criteria, recorded where | **Governance authority** | This is BIM Management. This is the target |

**Do this honestly.** The skill answer is the most seductive, because competence
is visible and authority is not.

**Self-check**

- [ ] Did I use "senior", "experienced" or "knows the software" as a reason for
      authority?
- [ ] If I removed every reference to permissions, would the explanation survive?
- [ ] If I removed every reference to seniority, would it survive?
- [ ] Could I say, for each authority I mentioned, **where it comes from**?

The last question is the real test. On Harrismith, authority comes from approved
governance — never from a label, an access level, an ownership setting or a
platform role.
