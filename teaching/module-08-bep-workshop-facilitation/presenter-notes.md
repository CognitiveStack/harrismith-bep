## Slide 2 — What a BEP workshop is — and is not — `1.4 min`

- **Opening cue:** "Every failure mode in this module is one sentence: the
  meeting started being treated as the decision authority. Here's the
  boundary that prevents it."
- **Explanation sequence:** (1) what the workshop can be taught as — a
  structured forum for examining evidence, understanding options, identifying
  decisions required, exposing authority requirements, recording reasoning
  and follow-up; (2) the one thing it is not — decision authority; (3) the
  controlled support: BEP §8.9 — meetings support decisions, discussion is
  not itself the controlled record, consequential decisions belong in the
  appropriate controlled record; (4) the worked pattern: the Working Process
  — always with its own status, `FOR REVIEW · Authority: None · Not
  approved` — which says it itself: *a workshop is not a parallel decision
  authority*; *contributing is open, deciding is not*.
- **Key sentence:** *"The workshop itself is not decision authority."*
- **Boundary warning:** invent nothing — no chair, no quorum, no facilitator
  holder, no participant names, no authority holder (`M8-S01`–`M8-S03`);
  never call the Working Process an approved process or anyone's procedure;
  if chairing comes up, chairing a meeting would not by itself establish
  approval or decision authority — teaching-safety synthesis.
- **Source-safe illustration:** the Working Process's own design — an intake
  route anyone can use precisely *because* nothing in it by itself changes
  approved governance or creates an authoritative governance decision — is
  the worked example of participation kept separate from governance, and its
  non-governing status is exactly what makes it useful.
- **Transition cue:** "What keeps it safe isn't hope — it's boundaries in
  three places: before, during, after."

## Slide 3 — The workshop control model: Before → During → After — `1.4 min`

- **Opening cue:** "Here is the whole module in one picture — and the label
  on the picture matters as much as the picture."
- **Explanation sequence:** (1) show the label first: **teaching synthesis —
  not a current Harrismith or Triviron governance workflow**; (2) walk the
  phases: BEFORE — prepare the decisions, evidence and authority questions;
  DURING — examine evidence, discuss options, distinguish proposals from
  decisions; AFTER — reconcile governed records, authorised updates,
  follow-up and verification; (3) restate the spine underneath: discussion ≠
  governed decision ≠ implementation ≠ verification; (4) the route reminder:
  no workshop output updates a BEP by itself — governed decision route, then
  authorised implementation, then verification.
- **Key sentence:** *"The workshop is controlled by boundaries before, during
  and after — not by giving the meeting authority."* — teaching synthesis.
- **Boundary warning:** never call the phases an approved procedure, project
  workflow, Triviron workflow or Harrismith controlled workshop process
  (`M8-S11`, `M8-S12`, `M8-S13`); evidence informs, it never decides
  (`M8-S07`).
- **Illustration:** none required — the framework diagram is the content.
- **Transition cue:** "So let's do BEFORE properly — starting with the
  backlog." *(Slides 4–14 continue from here — `NOT YET DEVELOPED`.)*

## Slide 4 — Before: turn the backlog into a decision agenda — `1.5 min`

- **Opening cue:** "Twenty-one questions sit in a register. None of them is
  an agenda item yet — and the difference is the whole of this slide."
- **Explanation sequence:** (1) the move: select a backlog matter for
  preparation; (2) what carries forward — the decision question, the evidence
  requirement and position, dependencies, the required authority *type*, the
  current status, the controlled destination; (3) what does not happen — no
  answer, no approval, no populated value, no authority holder; (4) land on
  the label: the result is a **workshop-preparation artefact — not a
  decision**.
- **Key sentence:** *"The backlog tells you what needs controlled attention.
  The decision agenda selects and frames what a future conversation would
  need to address."* — teaching synthesis, and say so if asked.
- **Boundary warning:** the register is an input, never a drafting surface —
  Module 8 modifies nothing in it (`M8-S13`); no row has been selected for
  any actual workshop, and none is planned (`M8-S12`); placement on an
  agenda creates neither authority nor a candidate (`M8-S11`).
- **Illustration:** none required — the carry-forward sequence is the
  content.
- **Transition cue:** "A framed question is half the preparation. The room
  also needs to see what the evidence can actually support."

## Slide 5 — Before: build the evidence position — `1.5 min`

- **Opening cue:** "Most bad workshop arguments are two people holding
  different unstated evidence positions. Preparation makes the position
  explicit."
- **Explanation sequence:** (1) the triad — evidence **required** (what the
  decision question needs), evidence **available** (what has actually been
  identified, with classification and source authority visible), evidence
  **missing** (the unsupported remainder); (2) the evidence position informs
  the discussion; (3) the two boundaries — evidence is not a decision, and
  missing evidence is not evidence of absence: `NONE IDENTIFIED` ≠
  `NONE EXISTS`; (4) even a fully evidenced matter still waits for the
  appropriate decision function.
- **Key sentence:** *"Good facilitation makes the evidence boundary visible
  before anyone starts arguing about the answer."* — teaching synthesis.
- **Boundary warning:** more evidence never automatically produces a
  decision, and evidence never identifies the authority holder (`M8-S07`);
  an unknown is never tidied into `TBD` (`M8-S09`).
- **Source-safe illustration:** the workshop template's own evidence section
  — which asks for the classification of every item examined — is the worked
  pattern (`FOR REVIEW · Authority: None · Not approved`).
- **Transition cue:** "Evidence says what is known. It says nothing about
  who may decide — that's the next question to prepare."

## Slide 6 — Before: functions, participants and authority — `1.5 min`

- **Opening cue:** "Five different things hide inside the word 'attendees'.
  Preparation names all five."
- **Explanation sequence:** (1) the five concepts — required function ·
  participant · authority holder (if established by controlled governance) ·
  facilitator (if such a function/holder is established) · action owner;
  (2) the rules — function ≠ person, participant ≠ authority, attendance ≠
  authority, facilitator ≠ decision owner or approver, action owner ≠
  technical owner, permissions ≠ governance authority; (3) the discipline
  where no holder is established: **keep the typed absence — never
  substitute the facilitator**.
- **Key sentence:** *"Invite people for what they can contribute; recognise
  authority only where governance establishes it."* — teaching synthesis.
- **Boundary warning:** invent nothing — no chair, no facilitator holder, no
  participant names, no authority holder (`M8-S01`–`M8-S03`); a tool role
  identifies nobody's governance function (`M8-S15`).
- **Source-safe illustration:** the workshop template records *participating
  functions — roles and functions, not participant names* — the pattern in
  one line (`Authority: None`).
- **Transition cue:** "Question, evidence, people, authority — now put one
  matter's preparation on a single page."

## Slide 7 — Before: design the decision agenda — `1.4 min`

- **Opening cue:** "Slide 4 chose the matter. This slide is the anatomy of
  the prepared item — what one agenda entry must let the room see."
- **Explanation sequence:** (1) the seven conceptual fields, shown blank —
  decision question · evidence required · evidence available/missing ·
  dependencies · required authority · current status · controlled
  destination; (2) the label does the governing work: **teaching artefact
  concept** — not Harrismith governance, not Triviron governance, not a live
  agenda, not an approved template; (3) the design principle: expose what
  the room needs to know; never pre-fill what the room is supposed to
  decide; (4) blank or typed absence always beats invented content.
- **Key sentence:** *"A decision agenda should expose what the room needs to
  know — not pre-fill what the room is supposed to decide."* — teaching
  synthesis.
- **Boundary warning:** no agenda ID, filename, number, date, participant
  value, authority holder or populated example — ever (`M8-S12`); a
  destination field authorises no update (`M8-S11`); an empty field is not a
  `TBD` (`M8-S09`).
- **Illustration:** the controlled analogue, named with its status — the
  publication-planning framework's *"agenda of decisions, their evidence
  requirements, and their boundaries"*, itself expressly not approved and
  not proposed governance; the analogue only, nothing publication-specific.
- **Transition cue:** "Preparation done. The room is live — take one
  decision conversation at a time."

## Slide 8 — During: one decision conversation at a time — `1.5 min`

- **Opening cue:** "Here is the whole DURING phase as one loop — and the
  checkpoint in the middle is what keeps the meeting honest."
- **Explanation sequence:** (1) the loop, expressly a teaching loop and not
  anyone's procedure: state the question → show the evidence position →
  expose the required authority → discuss options → identify what outcome is
  actually supportable → record / route / park → next matter; (2) the
  authority checkpoint, verbatim: *a useful discussion can occur without
  decision authority; a governed decision cannot be claimed merely because
  the discussion occurred*; (3) legitimate outputs without engaged
  authority: reasoning, clarification, evidence requests, actions,
  proposals, a recognised decision requirement, unresolved matters; (4) the
  precision that survives T8-C-R: **discussion alone establishes no governed
  decision** — and where an appropriate established authority genuinely
  exercises its function through the governed route, that governed act is
  separate from attendance, consensus, facilitation and the discussion
  itself; (5) why one-at-a-time (labelled inference): evidence doesn't bleed
  between matters, authority stays unambiguous, decisions stay traceable,
  unresolved items stay visible.
- **Key sentence:** *"A useful discussion can occur without decision
  authority. A governed decision cannot be claimed merely because the
  discussion occurred."*
- **Boundary warning:** consensus is never automatic authority (`M8-S04`);
  a record of the session is not automatic approval (`M8-S05`); parking a
  matter does not resolve it (`M8-S10`); never say no decision can ever
  occur — the boundary is that discussion alone establishes none.
- **Illustration:** none required — the loop diagram is the content.
- **Transition cue:** "Inside every conversation, four words get confused —
  discussion, option, proposal, decision. Next slide keeps them apart."
