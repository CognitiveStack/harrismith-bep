## Slide 2 — What a BEP workshop is — and is not — `1.4 min`

- **Opening cue:** "Every failure mode in this module is one sentence: the
  meeting started being treated as the decision authority. Here's the
  boundary that prevents it."
- **Explanation sequence:** (1) what the workshop can be taught as — a
  structured forum for examining evidence, understanding options, identifying
  decisions required, exposing authority requirements, recording reasoning
  and follow-up; (2) the one thing it is not — decision authority; (3) the
  controlled support: BEP §8.9 — meetings support decisions, discussion is
  not itself the controlled record, consequential decisions belong in the
  appropriate controlled record; (4) the worked pattern: the Working Process
  — always with its own status, `FOR REVIEW · Authority: None · Not
  approved` — which says it itself: *a workshop is not a parallel decision
  authority*; *contributing is open, deciding is not*.
- **Key sentence:** *"The workshop itself is not decision authority."*
- **Boundary warning:** invent nothing — no chair, no quorum, no facilitator
  holder, no participant names, no authority holder (`M8-S01`–`M8-S03`);
  never call the Working Process an approved process or anyone's procedure;
  if chairing comes up, chairing a meeting would not by itself establish
  approval or decision authority — teaching-safety synthesis.
- **Source-safe illustration:** the Working Process's own design — an intake
  route anyone can use precisely *because* nothing in it by itself changes
  approved governance or creates an authoritative governance decision — is
  the worked example of participation kept separate from governance, and its
  non-governing status is exactly what makes it useful.
- **Transition cue:** "What keeps it safe isn't hope — it's boundaries in
  three places: before, during, after."

## Slide 3 — The workshop control model: Before → During → After — `1.4 min`

- **Opening cue:** "Here is the whole module in one picture — and the label
  on the picture matters as much as the picture."
- **Explanation sequence:** (1) show the label first: **teaching synthesis —
  not a current Harrismith or Triviron governance workflow**; (2) walk the
  phases: BEFORE — prepare the decisions, evidence and authority questions;
  DURING — examine evidence, discuss options, distinguish proposals from
  decisions; AFTER — reconcile governed records, authorised updates,
  follow-up and verification; (3) restate the spine underneath: discussion ≠
  governed decision ≠ implementation ≠ verification; (4) the route reminder:
  no workshop output updates a BEP by itself — governed decision route, then
  authorised implementation, then verification.
- **Key sentence:** *"The workshop is controlled by boundaries before, during
  and after — not by giving the meeting authority."* — teaching synthesis.
- **Boundary warning:** never call the phases an approved procedure, project
  workflow, Triviron workflow or Harrismith controlled workshop process
  (`M8-S11`, `M8-S12`, `M8-S13`); evidence informs, it never decides
  (`M8-S07`).
- **Illustration:** none required — the framework diagram is the content.
- **Transition cue:** "So let's do BEFORE properly — starting with the
  backlog." *(Slides 4–14 continue from here — `NOT YET DEVELOPED`.)*
