## Slide 1 exercises — decision backlog → workshop

### E1

- **Prompt:** A colleague says: *"Module 8 is where we finally answer the
  twenty-one questions."* Correct the statement precisely.
- **Expected reasoning / answer:** Module 8 teaches the **conversation
  method** — how a decision workshop would be prepared, facilitated,
  concluded and followed up. It does not populate the register: the accepted
  position (21 questions · zero controlled Triviron evidence identified ·
  zero candidate decisions) is unchanged by Module 8, and answers would be
  earned only through evidence, authority and the governed decision route.
- **Basis:** Outline Slide 1 fields 6, 11; Module 8 `README.md` §5.
- **Misconception detected:** Teaching a method confused with producing
  answers (`M8-S13`).

### E2

- **Prompt:** Distinguish the Module 7 decision backlog from a workshop
  agenda in use.
- **Expected reasoning / answer:** The backlog is a controlled record of
  typed translation questions with their evidence and authority requirements
  — an *input* to workshop preparation. An agenda in use would be a prepared
  artefact for an actual scheduled session. No such session exists in this
  programme, so no agenda is in use; the decision agenda taught later is a
  **teaching artefact concept**, not a live document.
- **Basis:** Outline Slide 1 field 7; framework §2.
- **Misconception detected:** Backlog read as a live agenda; a roadmap
  statement read as a scheduled event (`M8-S14`).

### E3

- **Prompt:** Does anything on Slide 1 tell you whether Triviron has held or
  planned a BEP workshop in reality?
- **Expected reasoning / answer:** No. The slide states the programme's
  evidence position only: no controlled Triviron evidence of an actual
  workshop or listed workshop-governance item was identified in T8-A —
  `NONE IDENTIFIED`, which is not `NONE EXISTS`. Nothing may be inferred in
  either direction about reality.
- **Basis:** Module 8 `README.md` §6; outline header.
- **Misconception detected:** Census absence inflated into real-world
  non-existence, or the module read as evidence a workshop exists
  (`M8-S12`).

## Slide 2 exercises — what a workshop is, and is not

### E4

- **Prompt:** Suppose, hypothetically, every required function attends a
  future decision workshop and the room unanimously agrees an answer. What
  has been decided?
- **Expected reasoning / answer:** The unanimous agreement alone establishes
  no governed or authorised decision — attendance and consensus do not create
  authority. A governed decision can be claimed only if the appropriate
  established authority actually exercises that decision function through the
  governed route and the outcome is recorded in the appropriate controlled
  record. The hypothetical does not say whether that happened; without that
  evidence, the workshop result remains reasoning, agreement and proposal
  rather than an established governance decision. (Hypothetical — no such
  workshop exists.)
- **Basis:** BEP §8.9 (controlled); WP5/WP6 (working pattern,
  `Authority: None`); outline Slide 2 field 7.
- **Misconception detected:** Attendance or consensus treated as authority
  (`M8-S01`, `M8-S04`).

### E5

- **Prompt:** The facilitator ran an excellent session. May the record show
  them as the decision owner for the matters discussed?
- **Expected reasoning / answer:** No. Facilitating does not make anyone
  decision owner, approver, authoriser or technical owner — the coordination
  strategy's own pattern is *facilitate agreement but do not author*.
  Decision ownership requires a source-grounded allocation; where none is
  established, the record carries a typed absence, never the facilitator's
  name by default.
- **Basis:** Strategy facilitate-not-author line (class B); schema §5 via
  Module 7 (teaching interpretation); outline Slide 2.
- **Misconception detected:** Facilitation converted into ownership
  (`M8-S02`).

### E6

- **Prompt:** Why does Slide 2 quote the Working Process while insisting it
  is not approved workshop governance? What makes it useful anyway?
- **Expected reasoning / answer:** Its own declarations control — `FOR
  REVIEW`, `Authority: None`, `Not approved`, working material. It is useful
  as a **worked pattern** precisely because participation and working
  outcomes remain separate from governance: nothing in the Working Process
  by itself changes approved governance or creates an authoritative
  governance decision, however its own working statuses resolve. Usefulness
  and detail never upgrade authority.
- **Basis:** `working/README.md` own status block and WP1/WP5;
  `source-inventory.md` W1.
- **Misconception detected:** A useful source promoted into approved
  governance (`M8-S11`).

### E7

- **Prompt:** A meeting record exists, well written and complete. What does
  it prove by itself?
- **Expected reasoning / answer:** It evidences what the record says occurred
  and was discussed — and it may form part of evidence. By itself it does
  **not automatically** establish that approval or a governed decision
  occurred. If a consequential governed decision was actually taken, its
  authority, decision and status must be traceable in the appropriate
  controlled record; the meeting record may reference or support that
  evidence, but it does not acquire decision authority merely by existing.
  *"A decision nobody can produce afterwards did not happen."*
- **Basis:** BEP §8.9 (controlled); `M8-S05`; framework §6 minutes boundary
  (labelled synthesis where the word "minutes" is used).
- **Misconception detected:** Meeting record treated as automatic approval
  (`M8-S05`).

## Slide 3 exercises — the control model

### E8

- **Prompt:** The Before → During → After model looks like a procedure. Why
  must it be labelled `TEACHING SYNTHESIS — NOT A CURRENT HARRISMITH OR
  TRIVIRON GOVERNANCE WORKFLOW`, and what happens if the label is dropped?
- **Expected reasoning / answer:** The three-phase packaging is an original
  teaching construction: its components are source-supported (BEP §12's
  decision → authorisation → implementation → verification; the working
  pattern's routes), but no controlled source defines a workshop workflow.
  Dropping the label would present synthesis as governance — the exact
  promotion the module's boundaries exist to prevent — and would imply a
  procedure someone is bound to follow.
- **Basis:** Framework §1; outline Slide 3 fields 6, 11.
- **Misconception detected:** Teaching synthesis masquerading as controlled
  procedure (`M8-S11`, `M8-S12`).

### E9

- **Prompt:** Place these four objects in the control model and state why no
  two collapse: a heated discussion; a governed decision; a matrix update; a
  verification check.
- **Expected reasoning / answer:** Discussion happens DURING; discussion
  alone establishes no governed decision. A governed decision is a separate
  governed act, taken by the appropriate established authority through the
  governed route and recorded — it may follow the discussion but is not the
  discussion. The matrix update is implementation, permitted only through
  the authorised implementation route AFTER the decision. Verification then
  confirms the implementation matches the decision. Collapsing any pair —
  talk into decision, decision into edit, edit into verified — breaks the
  spine: discussion ≠ governed decision ≠ implementation ≠ verification.
- **Basis:** BEP §12.7–§12.9 (controlled); framework §5; outline Slide 3
  field 7.
- **Misconception detected:** The four acts merged; a workshop output
  treated as a document update (`M8-S07`, `M8-S11`).

### E10

- **Prompt:** After a future workshop, someone proposes editing the BEP that
  afternoon "while it's fresh". Trace the safe route and name what is missing
  from the proposal.
- **Expected reasoning / answer:** There is no `workshop → BEP edit` route.
  The safe route: workshop discussion / working record → where governance
  significance exists, the governed decision route → controlled decision
  record → where authorised, controlled implementation → verification and
  traceability. Missing from the proposal: the governed decision by the
  established authority, and the authorisation to implement. Freshness is
  not authority. (Hypothetical — no workshop exists.)
- **Basis:** Framework §5 route diagram; source-map §2 prohibited routes;
  WP7 (working pattern).
- **Misconception detected:** Momentum substituting for governance
  (`M8-S11`).

### E11

- **Prompt:** In the control model, where does evidence do its work — and
  where must it stop?
- **Expected reasoning / answer:** Evidence is assembled BEFORE (required vs
  available vs missing), presented and examined DURING, and cited in records
  AFTER. It informs every stage; it decides at none. Evidence does not
  approve, does not identify authority and does not create authority — a
  fully evidenced matter still awaits its established authority.
- **Basis:** Framework §2 steps 3–5, §4 authority checkpoint; `M8-S07`.
- **Misconception detected:** Evidence treated as decision (`M8-S07`).

### E12

- **Prompt:** Which parts of Slide 3 are controlled Harrismith support, and
  which are Module 8 synthesis? Name one of each.
- **Expected reasoning / answer:** Controlled: the decision → authorisation →
  implementation → verification separation (BEP §12.7–§12.9), and §8.9's
  meeting/record boundary. Synthesis (labelled): the Before → During → After
  packaging itself, and the takeaway sentence about boundaries. Keeping the
  classes distinct is what lets the slide teach confidently without
  promoting anything.
- **Basis:** Outline Slide 3 field 8; framework §1–§5 basis columns.
- **Misconception detected:** Source classes blurred on a mixed slide.
