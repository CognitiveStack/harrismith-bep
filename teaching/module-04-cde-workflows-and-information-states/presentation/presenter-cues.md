> "Twenty minutes on what a common data environment actually controls. Not

no claim not already carried by Slide 1.*

**Purpose.** Show **three ordered layers**, governance above configuration, **each carrying its own status**.

**Cues.**

- CDE = **process supported by technology** — `S1` §6.1, quotable project wording
- Folder tree implements **part** of it; reorganising folders changes no governance
- Six things the CDE controls — develop · check · share · authorise · deliver · retain
- **Permissions create no authority** — `S1` §6.9, `S2` §14
- Four earlier-module lines, **fifteen seconds**, then move — do not re-teach them

**Principal source basis.** `S1` §6.1, §6.9, §12.1; `S2` §1, §14, §17; `S3` §2, §3; `S4` §9; **`CGD-C01`, `CGD-C07`**

**Governance-status warning.** **Say the second row.** `S2` classifies its own workflow as **`PROPOSED GOVERNANCE`**, not as operating practice.

**Implementation-status warning.** **Band 3 is `IMPLEMENTATION UNVERIFIED`** (`CGD-C07`). The topology is adopted; its implementation is not verified.

**Harrismith evidence boundary.** `S4` §9 — *being able to perform an action in the software says nothing about who was authorised to decide it.*

**Likely question.** "So the CDE is just ACC, then?"

**Safe answer.**

> "ACC is one way to implement part of it. If we moved to a different platform tomorrow, the governance wouldn't change — which tells you the governance was never the platform."

**Transition.**

**Delivery warning.** **Fifteen seconds on the earlier-module lines, no more.** They are orientation, not revision.
