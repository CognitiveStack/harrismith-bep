## 2. Subject mapping

### Subject 1 — Why a BEP exists

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Support | **Direct** |
| Primary | S1 §1.1 Purpose; S1 §3.1–3.2 objectives; S1 §3.4 success principles |
| Secondary | S8 G2 (plain-language framing) |

S1 §1.1 states the aim directly: to make multidisciplinary delivery
**controlled, traceable and repeatable**, so that each information container has
a known originator, a known state, a known route to the next state, and a known
role accountable for moving it there. That sentence is the source of the seven
uncertainties on Slide 2, and the slide should not stray beyond it.

The Slide 2 teaching statement — *good individual models do not automatically
create good project information* — is **teaching wording, not source wording**.
It is consistent with S1 §5.1 ("Two kinds of responsibility") and S6 §1
("Interfaces, not geometry alone"), but no source sentence says it. Marked
**Partial** at statement level.

### Subject 2 — Who prepares, reviews and approves it

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Support | **Direct** for the functions; **None** for holders |
| Primary | S1 §5.2 role model; §5.3–5.9 role definitions; §9.2 decision terminology; §9.10 progressive BEP agreement and sign-off; S2 §2 roles |
| Secondary | S7 §1 register summary (the approval functions actually exercised) |

The role model in S1 §5.2 is explicitly a **conceptual functional model, not an
appointment chart**. S2 §2 lists the nine functional columns — AP, LDP, BM, BC,
TTL, Aut, Chk, CDE, Rcp.

**Every holder is TBD.** No appointing party, lead delivery party, BIM manager,
coordinator, task-team lead, author, checker or CDE administrator is established
(S1 §2.3, S2 §2). The presentation must not name or imply holders.

For *approval* specifically, S7 records the functions that were actually created
and exercised on this implementation — AG-001 Training Baseline Approver,
AG-002, AG-003, AG-004, AG-005 — each a **training-only function under TA-02**
carrying no real professional authority. This is unusually good teaching
material: it shows an approval function being established *before* an approval
decision is taken.

**Teaching caution.** S1 §9.1 warns that "approve" is used only where a defined
approval decision is intended, and that check, review, authorise, coordinate,
accept and reject are not collapsed into it. The slide title says "approves"; the
spoken explanation should introduce *authorise* as the more precise word.

### Subject 3 — Template versus project-specific BEP

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Support | **Partial — the weakest-supported subject in the module** |
| Primary | S1 §1.2 (structured as a real project BEP, no appointment behind it); §1.4 (four document types kept distinct); §1.5 precedence |
| Secondary | S1 §11.8; S9 §2 |

**No source document in this repository discusses a "template BEP", a "generic
company template", or the distinction between a template and a project-specific
BEP.** A search of the controlled set returns nothing on the subject. The terms
"template" and "templates" appear in the sources only in the sense of *authoring
templates* — S1 §11.8 records that **no approved project template set currently
exists** and that software defaults and office templates are not project
resources. That is a genuine and useful point, but it is about Revit templates,
not about BEP templates.

**What is legitimately supported.** S1 §1.4 draws a sharp line between the BEP
(governs), the BIM Delivery Guide (explains), the Working Process (no authority)
and project standards (govern within their subject), and states that a proposal
becomes governance only by being decided, recorded in the register, and reflected
in the BEP. S1 §1.5's precedence hierarchy makes the same point from another
direction. Both support the underlying teaching claim — **a template proposes; a
project BEP agrees** — without ever using the word "template".

**Marked for the presenter.** Deliver this subject as reasoning from the
document-type distinction in S1 §1.4, not as a claim quoted from the Harrismith
material. Do not attribute the template/project distinction to the BEP.

### Subject 4 — The Harrismith BEP structure

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Support | **Direct** |
| Primary | S1 §1.4 relationship to supporting resources; S1 full section architecture (13 sections); S9 §3.1–3.2 |

The thirteen-section architecture is **Architecture Baseline v1, frozen at Gate
B** — S1's header states sections are not to be renumbered, merged, split or
resequenced. S9 §3.1 lists the thirteen; S9 §3.2 lists the six supporting
resources.

The teaching point worth making is the division of labour in S1 §1.4: the BEP
states *what is required*; the supporting resources hold the detail; **detail is
not duplicated**. S1 §5.12 states it precisely — "meaning is defined once, here;
allocation is recorded once, there."

### Subject 5 — Responsibility matrices

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Support | **Direct** |
| Primary | S1 §5.12 responsibility grammar; S2 §1–3; S3 §1–3 |

Two matrices answering two different questions:

| Matrix | Question |
|---|---|
| S2 Information Management Responsibility Matrix | Who performs which information-management **function**? |
| S3 Model / Information Responsibility Matrix | Who produces and maintains which **information container**? |

**The approved grammar is Perform, Check, Authorise, Coordinate, Accept,
Consult, Inform** (S1 §5.12, S2 §1). **RACI is expressly not adopted** — S1
§5.12 states it is not to be introduced unless explicitly approved later, because
it collapses checking from authorising and coordinating from performing. This is
a strong teaching moment and it is directly sourced.

Concrete rows available for illustration: S2 P1/P4 (an Author does not
self-authorise; authorisation to share is the Task-Team Lead's separate
decision), and S2 C2/C3 (CDE Administration implements approved governance; a
platform permission is not authority).

S3 §3.1 gives six containers — ARC-01, STR-01, MEC-01, ELE-01, PLM-01, FIR-01 —
with MEC, ELE and PLM all originating from a **single MEP Consultant** party and
FIR from a **separate Fire Consultant**. All allocations are **PROPOSED
GOVERNANCE under TA-03**; no organisation is appointed and none is named.

### Subject 6 — Information-delivery planning

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Support | **Direct**, with an important constraint |
| Primary | S4 §2–5; S1 §10.1–10.4 |

S4 defines three restrained training events:

| Event | Concept | Status |
|---|---|---|
| TRN-E01 | Design coordination share | PROPOSED |
| TRN-E02 | Coordination reshare / resolution update | PROPOSED — conditional, per affected container, repeatable |
| TRN-E03 | Controlled design review / project-facing exchange | **PROPOSED — BLOCKED** pending governance decisions |

**The constraint matters more than the events.** S4 states that **no calendar
dates, frequencies or contractual milestones exist**, that no formal client
information requirements have been established, and that none are invented.

is an act with a recipient and a purpose, not a location.

Do not present TRN-E01/E02/E03 as a project programme. They are training
planning instruments.

### Subject 7 — CDE workflow

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Support | **Direct** |
| Primary | S5 §1–2; S1 §6.1, §6.3, §6.8 |

Four state concepts (S5 §1, S1 §6.3): **WIP**, **Shared**, **Published /
Authorised**, **Record / Retained**.

Three sourced teaching points, all strong:

1. **The CDE is a process, not a folder tree** (S1 §6.1, S5 preamble).
   Reorganising folders does not change how information is governed.
2. **States are not folders** (S1 §6.3). Specifically, **no mandatory CDE root
   named `04 Archive` is required or approved**, and the project's retention
   approach is TBD.
3. **Progression is controlled** — movement between states happens by decision,
   not by drift, and each transition has an owner (S1 §6.1, S5 §2).

S5 §2 gives the transition sequences — the production and sharing route, the
delivery route, and the correction route — which are directly usable as a slide
diagram. S5 §2 also lists the **five acts never collapsed into one another**:
check, authorisation, review, coordination, acceptance.

**Separately note:** CGD-001 (S7) approved **with conditions** the as-found
four-area root topology — `0. Common Files`, `01. WIP (Work in Progress)`,
`02. Shared`, `03. Published` — as intended training CDE governance, with **verification
pending** and **no child structures approved**. If the presentation shows a
folder structure, this is the accurate status to state.

### Subject 8 — Coordination and review

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Support | **Direct** |
| Primary | S6 §1, §8, §12–17, §19; S1 §8, §9.1–9.5 |

S6 §17 gives the coordination cycle end to end — controlled Shared inputs →
readiness check → federation → coordination checks → findings → triage → Issues
where required → originating task-team WIP correction → check → authorise
reshare → Shared → re-coordinate → verify → close → retain evidence.

Sourced distinctions worth teaching, all from S6 §1:

- **federation does not merge ownership** — aggregation creates a coordination
  artefact, not a jointly-authored model;
- **a finding is not a managed Issue** — creating an Issue is a deliberate
  decision;
- **clash detection is one technique**, not the whole coordination process;
- **the BIM Coordinator manages the process, not the technical solution**;
- **verification is not design approval**.

S1 §9.2 supplies the decision vocabulary — check, review, authorise, accept,
reject, coordinate — and states these are not collapsed into "approval".

### Subject 9 — Approval versus implementation

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Support | **Direct — and this is the best-evidenced subject in the module** |
| Primary | S1 §12.1, §12.3; S7 §1 and throughout; S9 §2, §5 |

S1 §12.3 distinguishes three states explicitly:

| State | Is |
|---|---|
| As-found / observed | Evidence of current reality |
| Intended governance | The approved target state |
| Implemented state | The operational state after an approved change |

S1 §12.1's principles supply the teaching lines directly: **evidence is not
decision**, **proposed is not approved**, **decision precedes configuration**.
S1 §1.2 adds: **development state is not issue state** — content existing in Git
means it has been drafted, not approved and not issued.

**Harrismith is its own worked example here, and the honest version is the
strong version.** The documentation set is approved with conditions (AD-001,
2026-08-01), conditions remain active, **publication remains NOT AUTHORISED**,
and CGD-001's adopted CDE topology has **verification pending**. An approved
governance position that has not yet been implemented is exactly what this
subject is about.

**UD-001 is the sharpest single illustration.** An observed Design Collaboration
cross-binding — the MEP Consultant team bound to a Structural-labelled WIP space
and the Structural team to an MEP-labelled space — was confirmed as current
through manual UI evidence in Increment 7C. It **remains unresolved, is carried
forward, and is deliberately not corrected** anywhere in the documentation. It
demonstrates in one example that observed reality, intended governance and
implementation are three different things, and that a discrepancy is resolved
only by an explicit recorded decision.

## 4. Standing constraints on all Module 1 material

Applicable to every subject above:

1. **No holder is named.** Every role holder in the Harrismith set is TBD. The
   presentation names functions, never people or companies.
2. **No source is issued.** All seven principal sources are approved with
   conditions and **not published**. Nothing may be described as issued,
   delivered or accepted.
3. **Training assumptions are not facts.** TA-01 (post-appointment context),
   TA-02 (simulated roles) and TA-03 (training delivery organisation) are
   scaffolds. Any slide showing the organisation model shows TA-03 and should
   say so.
4. **No ISO compliance is claimed.** ISO 19650 principles inform the approach;
   that is a statement of influence and nothing more (S1 §13.4). See
   [`../shared/glossary.md`](../shared/glossary.md) section 3.
5. **Observed discrepancies are not corrected.** UD-001 in particular is
   presented as observed and unresolved, never as fixed or normalised.
6. **Absence of observation is not observation of absence.** Only Architecture
   was observed as a populated production stream at the level inspected
   (OF-002); no discipline may be described as absent or inactive.
