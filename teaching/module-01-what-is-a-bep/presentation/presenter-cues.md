# Module 1 — Presenter Cues

**Status:** Delivery prompts. **Not a script.** Condensed from
[`../speaker-notes.md`](../speaker-notes.md).

Cues are fragments on purpose. They prompt; they do not supply sentences. **Speak
in your own words** — if you find yourself reading a cue aloud, it has failed.

The full notes remain available for preparation. These are for the speaker-notes
pane.

## Slide 1 — What Is a BIM Execution Plan? · 1.0 min

**Purpose** — Establish the BEP as an agreement, name the worked example, and be
straight about what Harrismith is.

**Cues**
- agreement between people — not a document, first
- multidisciplinary; the interfaces, not one team's work
- Harrismith = worked example; training/reference, no appointment
- Triviron = why I'm learning it here
- *don't define it yet — slide 3*

**Harrismith reference** — complete governed documentation set, developed as a
real one would be; no appointment behind it.

**Likely question** — "Is this a real project?"
**Safe answer** — Training and reference implementation. Structured as a real
project BEP would be; no appointment behind it. The honesty is what makes the
rest credible.

**Transition** — "So why does a project need one written down?"

## Slide 2 — Coordination becomes accidental · 2.0 min

**Purpose** — Make the audience recognise the problem from their own experience.

**Cues**
- seven questions, every multidisciplinary project
- they get answered *either way* — deliberately or accidentally
- **not a slide about bad work**
- failure is at the edges, between models
- concede competence, then relocate the problem

**Harrismith reference** — BEP §1.1's aim: controlled, traceable, repeatable —
known originator, known state, known route, known accountable role.

**Likely question** — "We manage fine without one."
**Safe answer** — Don't argue. Ask what happens on their project when two
versions of the same drawing exist.

**Transition** — "That's what a BEP is for. Here's what it actually is."

---

## Slide 3 — The agreed operating plan · 1.0 min

**Purpose** — Deliver the definition, then bound it.

**Cues**
- six verbs: produce, manage, exchange, coordinate, review, deliver
- everything later is one of them opened up
- what it isn't: Revit manual / naming convention / copied template / one
  person's procedure
- **last one: not proof it's being implemented** — seed for slide 13

**Harrismith reference** — the BEP's own thirteen sections map onto the six.

**Likely question** — "So it's a big document?"
**Safe answer** — Written down, yes — but the document records the agreement; it
isn't the agreement.

**Transition** — "Six verbs. None of them happen by themselves."

---

## Slide 4 — Who prepares, reviews and approves? · 2.0 min

**Purpose** — Five functions, not one owner.

**Cues**
- "writes" is the wrong verb — five different things
- BIM Manager drafts and maintains — *that part is concentrated*
- but AP, LDP, coordinator, task-team leads all marked **Consult**
- approval = separate defined function
- implementation = everyone who touches project information
- **distributed ≠ vague** — every function is named

**Harrismith reference** — the BEP's own route: draft → review → decisions
resolved → candidate → authorised training baseline → *controlled publication
(not reached)*.

**Likely question** — "Doesn't the BIM manager just decide everything?"
**Safe answer** — No. Publication authority is expressly not automatically
theirs, and the sources record that no single universal approver exists.

**Transition** — "They agree it. Agree to what, though?"

---

## Slide 5 — Template versus project-specific · 2.0 min

**Purpose** — Separate a framework from a set of decisions.

**Cues**
- the forty pages that turn up on every project — that's a template
- templates are useful; they just haven't answered anything for *your* project
- test isn't filled-in headings — it's whether a decision was taken
- Harrismith cuts both ways: lots decided, and a list of what isn't
- **it says so out loud** — that's the difference between incomplete and
  dishonest
- *this framing is mine, not the BEP's*

**Harrismith reference** — publication authority. Could have said "the BIM
Manager authorises"; instead recorded **unresolved**, and expressly not
automatically the BIM Manager, coordinator, CDE administrator or architect.

**Likely question** — "Isn't 'unresolved' just an excuse for not finishing?"
**Safe answer** — Only if unmarked. Recording an open decision keeps it visible
and gives it a route. Filling it in creates authority nobody agreed.

**Transition** — "So where do all those answers live?"

## Slide 6 — The Harrismith structure · 2.0 min

**Purpose** — One system, seven documents, and why the split matters.

**Cues**
- seven documents, not one — the split is the interesting part
- BEP in the middle: *states what is required*
- five supporting resources hold the detail; register records decisions and open
  questions
- **detail lives in exactly one place**
- duplication → divergence → both copies look authoritative
- one big document: every date change reopens the whole agreement

**Harrismith reference** — one rule across three documents: BEP §9.4 defines
*authorise*; matrix row P4 allocates it; delivery schedule applies it.

**Likely question** — "Isn't seven documents harder to keep in sync?"
**Safe answer** — Less so — nothing is stated twice, so there's nothing to fall
out of sync.

**Transition** — "Two of those seven get mixed up constantly."

## Slide 7 — Two matrices, two questions · 1.5 min

**Purpose** — Process responsibility ≠ model-content responsibility.

**Cues**
- both called responsibility matrices; completely different questions
- first: who performs / checks / authorises each **process step** — a grid
- second: which team produces each **model** — a register
- what happens *to* information vs whose information it *is*
- **not RACI** — seven terms, deliberately

**Harrismith reference** — P1/P4: the Author performs the authoring and doesn't
appear in the authorisation row at all.

**Likely question** — "Is this RACI?"
**Safe answer** — No, deliberately. RACI collapses checking with authorising and
coordinating with performing — the distinctions this whole BEP depends on.

**Transition** — "That's still just an allocation of work."

---

## Slide 8 — Information-delivery planning · 1.5 min

**Purpose** — Responsibility becomes commitment; and no dates, on purpose.

**Cues**
- allocation → commitment when something is actually *due*
- row: what, who, for whom, why, which event, what state
- checking + authorisation requirements = what makes it real
- **no dates. none.** no client requirement, no programme — event-triggered/TBD
- third event **blocked**, five reasons, left blocked
- *plausible authority would manufacture governance*

**Harrismith reference** — TRN-E01: six containers, six *separate* exchanges;
suitability coordination-only; format TBD.

**Likely question** — "When is this information due?"
**Safe answer** — Nothing has a date. No milestones, no contractual programme —
so timing is event-triggered or TBD. On a real project the dates come from the
appointment, and Harrismith has none.

**Transition** — "Every row named a state. So what is a state?"

---

## Slide 9 — Controlled CDE states · 1.0 min

**Purpose** — Four states; each about permitted use.

**Cues**
- WIP / Shared / Published-Authorised / **Record-Retained**
- a state = **what you may do with it**, not where it sits
- Shared ≠ approved; Published ≠ final, ≠ accepted
- nothing moves on its own — trigger, check, authorisation, named function
- new version ≠ new revision ≠ new state ≠ approval

**Harrismith reference** — the fourth state isn't tied to a folder; **no
`04 Archive` root approved**; retention TBD.

**Likely question** — "Where's the Archive?"
**Safe answer** — Harrismith's fourth state is *Record / Retained*, deliberately
not tied to a folder. No `04 Archive` root is required or approved, and the
retention approach is still TBD.

**Transition** — "There's one place this goes wrong in practice."

---

## Slide 10 — Folder ≠ status · 1.0 min

**Purpose** — Technical movement vs governed decision; permission vs authority.

**Cues**
- someone drops the model in the Shared folder — nothing has happened
- no check, no authorisation, producing team may not know
- **permission is not authority** — capability vs being permitted to decide
- responsibility first; permission configured to follow
- Harrismith marks the publication transition **blocked** rather than inventing
  an authority

**Harrismith reference** — T4: trigger and check defined, performing function
**UNRESOLVED — TBD**, status **blocked**, information remains Shared.

**Likely question** — "But I have write access."
**Safe answer** — Access lets you perform the action. It doesn't make you the
person permitted to decide it.

**Transition** — "The main reason information moves at all is coordination."

---

## Slide 11 — Coordination as a cycle · 1.0 min

**Purpose** — A management cycle, not a clash test.

**Cues**
- clash test is *one thing inside* coordination
- controlled Shared inputs → readiness → federate → check → **triage** →
  assign → resolve in own WIP → reshare → re-coordinate → verify → close
- triage is the step people skip — not every finding becomes an Issue
- federation doesn't create a jointly-owned model
- **completion isn't zero clashes**
- *this is how it would work — not evidenced as running*

**Harrismith reference** — COORD-01: temporary controlled aggregation; doesn't
merge authorship or transfer responsibility; not a deliverable unless scheduled.

**Likely question** — "Is this running on Harrismith now?"
**Safe answer** — No — and be precise: no Coordination Space was observed
configured, the status model isn't claimed to be configured, and the platform
mapping hasn't been made.

**Transition** — "Triage, and what happens after it, is where this works or
quietly doesn't."

---

## Slide 12 — Issue to recorded resolution · 1.0 min

**Purpose** — Detecting and recording ≠ resolving and closing.

**Cues**
- finding → *triage decision* → New → Triaged → Assigned → In Progress →
  **Ready for Verification** → Closed; plus Deferred / Escalated
- coordinator coordinates; **task team owns the fix**
- correction in the team's own WIP, checked, **reshared under control**
- "fixed in WIP" doesn't close it — closure follows re-coordination
- **closing an issue is not approving the design**

**Harrismith reference** — the source's own STR-01 / MEC-01 example, explicitly
labelled illustrative: no geometry, no coordinates, no Issue ID, no named person.

**Likely question** — "Does closing the ACC issue approve the design?"
**Safe answer** — No. Verification confirms the coordination process reached a
disposition. Not design approval, not certification, not publication authority,
not acceptance. The originating team keeps technical responsibility throughout.

**Transition** — "All of that is what the team agreed to do."

---

## Slide 13 — Approval ≠ implementation · 1.5 min

**Purpose** — Five different states; a signed BEP is not self-executing.

**Cues**
- approved / authorised / implemented / evidenced / verified — five, not one
- **quote it**: approval is not implementation, implementation is not
  verification
- be straight about my own example: approved with conditions, **not** a live
  workflow, **not** verified through a coordination cycle
- unresolved matters carried straight past approval
- **a BEP with no open questions is an unfinished conversation**
- *frame as discipline, not failure*

**Harrismith reference** — UD-001: observed, recorded, confirmed current, stopped
at *decide intended state*. Every downstream step unreachable.

**Likely question** — "So the BEP doesn't work?"
**Safe answer** — It's an agreed position that hasn't been fully exercised.
Normal for a baseline — and it's recorded rather than hidden, which is what makes
it usable for teaching.

**Transition** — "So that's Harrismith. What does it mean for us?"

---

## Slide 14 — What must Triviron decide? · 1.5 min

**Purpose** — Worked example → the audience's own decisions, as questions.

**Cues**
- not "we copy Harrismith" — a structure and a list of questions
- questions come from what Harrismith decided *or left open*
- what the client requires / who's the authority / who leads / real milestones /
  who authorises and accepts
- which decisions can come from a template, which must be agreed here
- **what evidence will show we're doing it?**
- what transfers: architecture, grammar, state model, the discipline of recording
  gaps

**Harrismith reference** — Harrismith's open questions *are* Triviron's required
decisions: appointing party TBD, no information requirements, no dates,
publication and acceptance authority unresolved, standards not established.

**Likely question** — "Can we reuse Harrismith directly?"
**Safe answer** — The structure and method, yes. The answers, no — they're
Harrismith's answers to Harrismith's conditions, and several are explicitly
unresolved.

**Transition** — None. Final slide. Move to questions.

## Fallbacks

### 30-second opening fallback

> "A BIM Execution Plan is a project team's agreement about how they'll handle
> information together — who produces what, who checks it, who's allowed to say
> it can be relied on. I'll use the Harrismith Fire Station as a worked example.
> It's a training implementation, so there's no appointment behind it, but it has
> a complete governed documentation set — which makes it a good thing to learn
> on. And at the end I'll come back to what it means for Triviron."

### 30-second closing fallback

> "So — a BEP is the team's agreed method for producing, coordinating, checking,
> sharing and approving information. The matrices, the schedule and the strategies
> turn that agreement into something a project can actually run on. And every
> project has to complete that system for itself — its own appointments, its own
> requirements, its own delivery conditions. Harrismith doesn't hand us a Triviron
> BEP. It hands us a worked example, a set of questions, and a demonstration of
> how to record what we haven't decided yet."

### 10-second closing fallback

> "A BEP is the team's agreed method for managing project information. Harrismith
> shows us the structure and the questions. Triviron has to answer them for
> itself — and write down what it hasn't answered yet."

### Recovery cues — one sentence per section, if running over

| Block | Recovery sentence |
|---|---|
| 1 — The problem | "Those seven questions get answered on every project; the only choice is whether you do it on purpose." |
| 2 — The response | "Six verbs — produce, manage, exchange, coordinate, review, deliver — and everything else is one of them opened up." |
| 3 — Roles and instruments | "Seven documents: one says what's required, five hold the detail, one records what's still open — and nobody owns all of it alone." |
| 4 — CDE and coordination | "Information has a state that says what you may do with it, and it only changes state when somebody with the authority decides it." |
| 5 — Approval vs implementation | "Approval is not implementation, and implementation is not verification — and Harrismith says so about itself." |
| 6 — Transfer | "Harrismith's open questions are Triviron's required decisions." |

**Use recovery cues to compress, not to skip.** Blocks 5 and 6 are the payoff —
if time is short, recover Block 3 or Slide 11 and protect Slides 13 and 14.
