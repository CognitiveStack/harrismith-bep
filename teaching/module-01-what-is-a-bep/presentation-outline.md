## 3. Developed slides — initial teaching baseline

Speaker notes for these three slides are in
[`speaker-notes.md`](speaker-notes.md).

### Slide 1 — What Is a BIM Execution Plan?

**Subtitle:** *The Harrismith Fire Station as a worked example*

**Purpose of the slide**

- establish that the BEP is an **operating agreement for multidisciplinary
  information**, not primarily a document;
- avoid presenting it as merely a long technical document;
- introduce Harrismith as the worked example;
- identify Triviron as the future transfer context.

**On the slide**

| Element | Content |
|---|---|
| Title | What Is a BIM Execution Plan? |
| Subtitle | The Harrismith Fire Station as a worked example |
| Visual | Harrismith Fire Station project or model image — see [`visual-demonstration-plan.md`](visual-demonstration-plan.md) V1 |
| Framing line | An operating agreement for how a project team handles information |

**Not on the slide:** the definition itself. It is spoken, and it lands on Slide
3 once the problem has been established. A definition given before the audience
feels the problem is heard as jargon.

**Honesty note.** Harrismith is introduced here as a training and reference
implementation. That is said once, plainly, at the start — not buried in a
disclaimer at the end.

### Slide 2 — Without an agreed execution plan, coordination becomes accidental

**Purpose of the slide**

Establish the problem before offering the solution. The audience must recognise
the situation as one they have been in.

**The project uncertainties a BEP resolves**

| # | The question | What it is really asking |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Who produces each information item? | Is there exactly one accountable originator, or does everyone assume someone else has it? |
| 2 | Which information is current? | When two versions exist, which one is the project's? |
| 3 | When must it be delivered? | Is the timing a shared expectation or a private one? |
| 4 | Where must it be shared? | Is there one known place, or several plausible ones? |
| 5 | Who checks it? | Has anyone verified it against a stated requirement? |
| 6 | Who accepts or authorises it? | Is anyone permitted to say this may now be relied on? |
| 7 | How are coordination problems resolved? | Is there a route, or does resolution depend on who shouts? |

**Teaching statement for this slide:**

> Good individual models do not automatically create good project information.

**Why this statement matters.** It separates competence from coordination. The
audience contains people who are good at their own work and who reasonably
resent the implication that they are not. The statement concedes their
competence and relocates the problem to the interfaces between them — which is
exactly where a BEP operates.

### Slide 3 — A BEP is the agreed operating plan for project information

**Purpose of the slide**

Deliver the definition, now that the problem is established, and immediately
bound it by saying what a BEP is not.

**The six verbs**

| Verb | What it covers |
|---|---|
| **Produce** | Who authors each information container, to what requirement, in what environment |
| **Manage** | How information is identified, versioned, stated and controlled through its life |
| **Exchange** | How information moves between parties, for a stated purpose, in an agreed form |
| **Coordinate** | How multidisciplinary interfaces are checked, and how findings become tracked actions |
| **Review** | How information is checked, considered and decided upon before it may be relied on |
| **Deliver** | How identified information reaches an identified recipient for an identified purpose |

The six verbs are the spine of the whole presentation. Every later section is
one of them expanded: responsibilities and delivery planning is *produce* and
*deliver*; CDE workflow is *manage* and *exchange*; coordination and review are
their own verbs.

**A BEP is not:**

| Not | Why the confusion arises |
|---|---|
| Only a Revit modelling manual | Authoring conventions are part of it, so people mistake the part for the whole |
| Merely a naming-convention document | Naming is visible and concrete, so it stands in for the invisible governance around it |
| A generic company template copied unchanged into a project | A template is a starting shape; it has not agreed anything with this project's team |
| Only the BIM manager's private procedure | If only one person knows it, no agreement exists — just a preference |
| Proof that the agreed process is actually being implemented | An agreed process and a running process are different states, and the gap between them is where the work is |

**The last row is the most important and the least expected.** It is also the
seed of the *Approval versus implementation* section later in the talk. Plant it
here; harvest it there.

## 4. Sections not yet developed into slides

Seven sections remain to be developed. Recorded here as intent, not as content:

| Section | The one message it must land |
|---|---|
| Who prepares, reviews and approves it | Preparing, checking and authorising are three different acts, and collapsing them is how accountability disappears |
| Template versus project-specific BEP | A template proposes; a project BEP agrees — and only the second binds anyone |
| Harrismith BEP structure | Thirteen sections, six supporting resources, and a deliberate division of labour between "what is required" and "how it is allocated" |
| Responsibilities and delivery planning | Who does what, to which container, by when, for what purpose — written down once, in one place |
| CDE workflow | Information has a knowable state, and it changes state by decision rather than by drift |
| Coordination and review | Federation is not merging; a finding is not an Issue; coordination is not design approval |
| Approval versus implementation | Harrismith's documentation is approved with conditions and not published — and saying so is the honest position, not a weakness |
| Transfer to Triviron | Structure and method transfer; project-specific decisions do not, and must be taken again |

Each will need its own speaker notes and its own entry in the visual plan before
the presentation can be considered complete.
