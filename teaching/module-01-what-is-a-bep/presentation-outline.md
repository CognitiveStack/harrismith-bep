### Slide 1 — What Is a BIM Execution Plan?

**Subtitle:** *The Harrismith Fire Station as a worked example*

**Purpose of the slide**

- establish that the BEP is an **operating agreement for multidisciplinary
  information**, not primarily a document;
- avoid presenting it as merely a long technical document;
- introduce Harrismith as the worked example;
- identify Triviron as the future transfer context.

**On the slide**

| Element | Content |
|---|---|
| Title | What Is a BIM Execution Plan? |
| Subtitle | The Harrismith Fire Station as a worked example |
| Visual | Harrismith Fire Station project or model image — see [`visual-demonstration-plan.md`](visual-demonstration-plan.md) V1 |
| Framing line | An operating agreement for how a project team handles information |

**Not on the slide:** the definition itself. It is spoken, and it lands on Slide
3 once the problem has been established. A definition given before the audience
feels the problem is heard as jargon.

**Honesty note.** Harrismith is introduced here as a training and reference
implementation. That is said once, plainly, at the start — not buried in a
disclaimer at the end.

### Slide 2 — Without an agreed execution plan, coordination becomes accidental

**Purpose of the slide**

Establish the problem before offering the solution. The audience must recognise
the situation as one they have been in.

**The project uncertainties a BEP resolves**

| # | The question | What it is really asking |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Who produces each information item? | Is there exactly one accountable originator, or does everyone assume someone else has it? |
| 2 | Which information is current? | When two versions exist, which one is the project's? |
| 3 | When must it be delivered? | Is the timing a shared expectation or a private one? |
| 4 | Where must it be shared? | Is there one known place, or several plausible ones? |
| 5 | Who checks it? | Has anyone verified it against a stated requirement? |
| 6 | Who accepts or authorises it? | Is anyone permitted to say this may now be relied on? |
| 7 | How are coordination problems resolved? | Is there a route, or does resolution depend on who shouts? |

**Teaching statement for this slide:**

> Good individual models do not automatically create good project information.

**Why this statement matters.** It separates competence from coordination. The
audience contains people who are good at their own work and who reasonably
resent the implication that they are not. The statement concedes their
competence and relocates the problem to the interfaces between them — which is
exactly where a BEP operates.

### Slide 3 — A BEP is the agreed operating plan for project information

**Purpose of the slide**

Deliver the definition, now that the problem is established, and immediately
bound it by saying what a BEP is not.

**The six verbs**

| Verb | What it covers |
|---|---|
| **Produce** | Who authors each information container, to what requirement, in what environment |
| **Manage** | How information is identified, versioned, stated and controlled through its life |
| **Exchange** | How information moves between parties, for a stated purpose, in an agreed form |
| **Coordinate** | How multidisciplinary interfaces are checked, and how findings become tracked actions |
| **Review** | How information is checked, considered and decided upon before it may be relied on |
| **Deliver** | How identified information reaches an identified recipient for an identified purpose |

The six verbs are the spine of the whole presentation. Every later section is
one of them expanded: responsibilities and delivery planning is *produce* and
*deliver*; CDE workflow is *manage* and *exchange*; coordination and review are
their own verbs.

**A BEP is not:**

| Not | Why the confusion arises |
|---|---|
| Only a Revit modelling manual | Authoring conventions are part of it, so people mistake the part for the whole |
| Merely a naming-convention document | Naming is visible and concrete, so it stands in for the invisible governance around it |
| A generic company template copied unchanged into a project | A template is a starting shape; it has not agreed anything with this project's team |
| Only the BIM manager's private procedure | If only one person knows it, no agreement exists — just a preference |
| Proof that the agreed process is actually being implemented | An agreed process and a running process are different states, and the gap between them is where the work is |

**The last row is the most important and the least expected.** It is also the
seed of the *Approval versus implementation* section later in the talk. Plant it
here; harvest it there.

### Slide 4 — Who prepares, reviews and approves the BEP?

**Purpose of the slide**

Show that preparing, reviewing, approving, authorising and implementing a BEP
are **related but different functions**, held by different parts of the project.

The misconception to defeat is specific: that one "BIM manager" writes the BEP
alone and then imposes it on everyone else. The Harrismith sources contradict
that directly, and the contradiction is worth showing rather than asserting.

**Five functions, five different questions**

| Function | The question it answers | In the Harrismith sources |
|---|---|---|
| **Prepare / maintain** | Who drafts and keeps the governance framework coherent? | BIM Manager performs it; **Owner/Appointing Party, Lead Delivery Party, BIM Coordinator and Task-Team Leads are all *consulted*** (IM matrix G1) |
| **Contribute** | Who supplies the discipline reality the framework must fit? | Task-Team Leads and their teams, through consultation and the Working Process |
| **Review / check** | Who verifies it against a defined requirement? | Controlled review, before any baseline candidate (BEP §9.10) |
| **Approve / authorise** | Who is permitted to say this now stands? | A **separate, defined function** — for Harrismith's baseline, the Training Baseline Approver (AG-001) |
| **Implement** | Who has to actually operate it? | The whole delivery team; CDE Administration performs approved platform changes (IM matrix A3) |

**The route the BEP itself travelled** (BEP §9.10):

```
working draft
  → controlled review
  → decisions resolved
  → baseline candidate
  → authorised training baseline
  → controlled publication
```

Harrismith reached "authorised training baseline" — approved with conditions —
and has **not** reached controlled publication.

**Three sourced statements that do the heavy lifting**

1. **The BIM Manager is not automatically the Appointing Party, the Lead
   Delivery Party, a design approver, a discipline technical lead or a
   contractual decision-maker** (BEP §5.5). The function governs *how information
   is managed*; authority over design, appointment and contract lies elsewhere
   and is not acquired by holding it.
2. **No single universal approver exists.** Authority corresponds to the nature
   of the decision, and unlimited authority is not assigned to the BIM Manager
   (IM matrix A2 note; BEP §12.7).
3. **CDE Administration implements governance; it does not create it.** Changing
   the software does not make a decision, and platform permission is not
   authority (BEP §5.9).

**The teaching question this slide must answer**

> Who owns the BEP — the person who writes it, the organisation that approves
> it, or the team that must implement it?

**The answer, in one line:** no one function owns all of it, and that is not
vagueness — each function is named, and each is accountable for a different act.
Drafting is concentrated. Agreement is distributed. Approval is a separate,
defined function. Implementation belongs to everyone who touches project
information.

**Honesty note.** Every holder in the Harrismith set is **TBD** — no appointing
party, lead delivery party, BIM manager, coordinator, task-team lead, author,
checker or CDE administrator is established (BEP §2.3; IM matrix §2, §6). Name
functions, never people.

**Boundary.** This slide *foreshadows* approval versus implementation; it does
not deliver it. The dedicated section later in the talk carries that argument.
Here, implementation appears only as the fifth function in the row — enough for
the audience to notice it is separate.

### Slide 5 — Template versus project-specific BEP

> **Teaching synthesis.** The Harrismith sources do **not** discuss BEP templates
> or teach this distinction. The comparison below is constructed for teaching.
> It must not be quoted or attributed to the Harrismith documents. What the
> sources *do* supply is the right-hand column: evidence of what
> project-specific completion actually looks like. See
> [`source-map.md`](source-map.md).

**Purpose of the slide**

Separate a reusable starting framework from a completed BEP containing decisions
for one actual project.

**The two-column comparison**

| A reusable template or framework | A project-specific BEP |
|---|---|
| Standard headings and section architecture | The project's purpose and scope, stated |
| Standard role *categories* | The participating organisations and functions, identified |
| Common naming principles | The information responsibilities, allocated |
| Standard CDE concepts | The required information deliverables, defined |
| Standard review prompts | The delivery events or gates, agreed |
| Typical delivery-planning fields | The adopted CDE states and workflows, decided |
| — | The coordination and review arrangements, agreed |
| — | The approval and authorisation functions, established |
| — | The project's exceptions and unresolved decisions, recorded |

The left column is largely teaching synthesis. The right column is drawn from
what the Harrismith set actually contains and actually leaves open — which is
why the honest example below matters more than the comparison.

**Required message**

> A template asks the right questions; a project-specific BEP records the
> project's agreed answers.

*(Teaching wording. Not source wording.)*

**The second message, which is the harder one**

> A populated document is not necessarily a complete one. Every heading can
> contain text while the project's decisions remain unmade.

**Harrismith is the honest worked example — in both directions.**

What it *has* decided, project-specifically:

- a thirteen-section architecture, frozen as Architecture Baseline v1;
- a training organisation model with six disciplines across four consultant
  parties (TA-03);
- six named information containers, each with one originating task team;
- a responsibility grammar of seven terms, with **RACI expressly not adopted**;
- a four-state CDE model, with the root topology adopted with conditions;
- three delivery events, with their checking and authorisation requirements.

What it has **not** decided, and says so:

- who the appointing party is, and who leads delivery — **TBD**;
- who holds **publication / exchange authority** — **UNRESOLVED** (BEP §9.7;
  IM matrix D4);
- who holds **recipient acceptance authority** — **UNRESOLVED** (IM matrix D7);
- **governance-change approval authority by change class** — **UNRESOLVED**
  (IM matrix A2);
- level of information need — **not defined**;
- naming, coordinate, titleblock and template standards — **not established**;
- the Design Collaboration team-space mapping — **UD-001, unresolved**.

**This is the point of the slide.** Harrismith's sections are all populated. Its
supporting resources are all substantial. And it still records a long list of
things the project has not decided — deliberately, rather than filling them with
plausible values. **A completed heading is not a completed decision.**

### Slide 6 — The Harrismith BEP structure

**Purpose of the slide**

Show that the implementation is **one connected management system**, not a single
enormous document — and show why that separation makes it easier to maintain,
review and teach.

**The seven documents**

| # | Document | Holds |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `bep/Harrismith-Fire-Station-BEP.md` | The central operating agreement — *what is required* |
| 2 | `supporting/information-management-responsibility-matrix.md` | Who performs which information-management function |
| 3 | `supporting/model-information-responsibility-matrix.md` | Which task team produces and maintains each container |
| 4 | `supporting/information-delivery-schedule.md` | What is exchanged, at which event, to whom, why and in what form |
| 5 | `supporting/cde-workflow-state-strategy.md` | Container states, transitions and the controls on each |
| 6 | `supporting/coordination-review-strategy.md` | Coordination cycles, interface management, review and verification |
| 7 | `supporting/governance-decision-register.md` | Decisions, assumptions, deviations and unresolved matters |

Sources for this table: BEP §1.4 and §13.2, which list the six supporting
resources and what each holds.

**The relationship, in one shape**

- the **BEP** sits at the centre as the operating agreement;
- the **matrices, schedule and strategies** sit around it, each holding the
  detail for one part of it;
- the **governance-decision register** sits alongside all of them, recording
  what was decided, assumed and left open.

**Why it is split — the sourced reason**

BEP §1.4: the BEP states *what is required*; the supporting resources hold the
detail; **detail is not duplicated**. BEP §13.1 gives the reason explicitly:

> Duplication creates divergence, and divergence in governance documentation is
> worse than absence, because both copies appear authoritative.

BEP §5.12 states the same principle as a rule of construction: **meaning is
defined once, allocation is recorded once.** Section 5 defines what each function
*means*; the matrix records *how it is allocated*.

**Three cautions the slide must carry**

1. **No legal or contractual hierarchy is being claimed.** BEP §1.5's precedence
   order is about document conflict, and the BEP's own authority is recorded as
   **none** — training / reference, non-contractual.
2. **Reference does not imply approval.** Listing a resource records that the BEP
   depends on it; the resource's own declared status determines whether it may be
   relied upon (BEP §13.1, §13.6).
3. **Not every supporting document is complete or operational.** Each declares
   its own status; all seven are approved with conditions and **not published**;
   several carry explicit "unresolved" sections of their own.

**The takeaway for the audience:** if all of this lived in one document, every
change to a delivery date would be a change to the BEP, every reader would have
to search the whole thing to find one allocation, and the reviewer of a
coordination change would have to re-approve the entire agreement.

### Slide 7 — Two responsibility matrices, two different questions

**Purpose of the slide**

Show that **process responsibility and model-content responsibility are related
but not the same thing** — and that Harrismith keeps them in two separate
documents on purpose.

**The two questions**

| Document | The question it answers |
|---|---|
| **Information Management Responsibility Matrix** | Who **performs, checks, authorises, coordinates and accepts** each information-management process function? |
| **Model / Information Responsibility Matrix** | Which **party and task team produces and maintains** each information container? |

Both questions are quoted from the documents' own purpose statements. The first
matrix expressly states it does *not* answer which organisation authors each
container; the second expressly states it does *not* answer who performs which
process function.

**They are not built the same way, and that is the tell**

| | IM Responsibility Matrix | Model / Information Responsibility Matrix |
|---|---|---|
| Shape | A grid — **functions × roles** | A register — **one entry per container** |
| Rows | 25 process functions, grouped G / C / P / S / X / D / A | 6 discipline containers plus COORD-01 |
| Columns | 9 functional roles: AP, LDP, BM, BC, TTL, Aut, Chk, CDE, Rcp | Originating party, task team, discipline, purpose, format, intended states, interfaces |
| Cells say | **Perform, Check, Authorise, Coordinate, Accept, Consult, Inform** | Which party and task team — plus Lead and Contributors |
| Unit of allocation | A **verb** | A **thing** |

**RACI is not used, and this is explicit.** BEP §5.12 states the seven-term
grammar is used **instead of** RACI, because RACI collapses checking from
authorising and coordinating from performing — distinctions the whole BEP depends
on. It is not to be introduced unless explicitly approved later. Do not translate
these cells into RACI on a slide.

**Worked example from the IM matrix — rows P1 and P4**

| # | Function | Aut | Chk | TTL | BC |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Author information in WIP | **P** | — | Co | — |
| P4 | Authorise WIP information for controlled sharing | — | Cs | **Au** | In |

The Author *performs* the authoring. The Author does **not** appear in the
authorisation row at all. **An Author does not self-authorise merely because they
authored the information** — authorisation to share is a separate decision, held
by the Task-Team Lead.

**Worked example from the Model matrix — the MEP rows**

| Ref | Container | Originating party | Task team | Discipline |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MEC-01 | Mechanical design / coordination model | **MEP Consultant** | Mechanical task team | MEC |
| ELE-01 | Electrical design / coordination model | **MEP Consultant** | Electrical task team | ELE |
| PLM-01 | Plumbing design / coordination model | **MEP Consultant** | Plumbing task team | PLM |
| FIR-01 | Fire design / coordination model | **Fire Consultant** | Fire task team | FIR |

**One party, three task teams, three containers** — and Fire is a *separate*
party, not an MEP sub-team. This is exactly what the first matrix cannot tell
you, and exactly why the second exists.

**Required message**

> Process responsibility and model-content responsibility are related, but they
> are not the same thing.

**Both matrices are subject to the same constraint:** every role holder is
**TBD**, and every model-matrix allocation is **PROPOSED GOVERNANCE under
TA-03**. No organisation is appointed and no individual is named.

### Slide 8 — Information-delivery planning

**Purpose of the slide**

Show how a BEP turns responsibilities into **planned information deliveries** —
and what a delivery plan looks like when no dates exist.

**Required message**

> A responsibility without a planned delivery is only an allocation of work; the
> delivery schedule turns it into a coordinated commitment.

*(Teaching wording. Not source wording.)*

**The questions a schedule row answers**

The schedule uses sixteen fields, approved in BEP §10.13. Grouped for teaching:

| The question | The fields that answer it |
|---|---|
| **What** must be delivered? | Information Container, Format |
| **Who** is responsible? | Originating Party, Task Team, Discipline, Lead |
| **For whom, and why?** | Recipient, Purpose |
| **At which event?** | Exchange / Milestone, Delivery ID |
| **In what state, usable for what?** | State / Suitability |
| **Under what control?** | Checking Requirement, Authorisation Requirement, Acceptance Criteria |
| **Depending on what?** | Dependencies, Status |

The last two rows are the ones that make it a commitment rather than a list. A
container without a checking requirement, an authorisation requirement and an
acceptance criterion is a hope, not a delivery.

**The three Harrismith events**

| Event | Concept | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **TRN-E01** | Design coordination share — six containers, six separate exchanges | PROPOSED |
| **TRN-E02** | Coordination reshare after a finding — **conditional and repeatable** | PROPOSED — activates per affected container only |
| **TRN-E03** | Controlled design review / project-facing exchange | **PROPOSED — BLOCKED** |

**No dates. This is deliberate, and it must be said.**

**No real delivery milestones, dates or contractual programme have been
established.** No client information requirements are available to the
implementation, and none are invented. All timing is **event-triggered or TBD**.
The identifiers `TRN-E01-ARC` and similar are training references, not
contractual document numbers — no naming standard exists.

**Why TRN-E03 is blocked — five recorded reasons**

| Blocking matter | Status |
|---|---|
| Publication / exchange authorisation authority | **UNRESOLVED — TBD** (BEP §9.7; IM matrix D4) |
| Recipient acceptance authority | **UNRESOLVED — TBD** (BEP §9.8, §10.11; IM matrix D7) |
| Recipient identity | Not established |
| Required formats | Not established — no approved standard |
| Deliverable set | Not defined |

The source states the reasoning plainly, and it is the best line available for
this slide:

> Assigning a plausible authority to make the row look finished would manufacture
> governance that does not exist.

**Four states that are routinely conflated** (from the TRN-E03 entry):

| Published | Delivered | Received | Accepted |
|---|---|---|---|
| Authorised for a defined purpose | Sent to an identified recipient | Arrived and registered | Acknowledged as suitable for the stated purpose |

Delivery does not prove acceptance. Receipt does not prove suitability.
Acceptance applies only to the identified purpose, and **does not transfer
technical responsibility from the originator**.
