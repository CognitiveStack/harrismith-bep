# Module 1 — What is a BIM Execution Plan?

**Status:** Active module. Teaching material in development. **Not governance,
and not a final presentation.**

---

## 1. Objective

Enable the presenter to explain, to a mixed project audience, what a BIM
Execution Plan is, why it exists, who acts on it, and what it changes about how a
project handles information — using the Harrismith Fire Station as the worked
example throughout.

The audience is assumed to be mixed: some Revit users, some who never open a
model, and at least one person whose interest is entirely commercial. The
explanation must work for all of them.

## 4. Central teaching message

> A BIM Execution Plan is the project team's agreed method for producing,
> coordinating, checking, sharing and approving information.

Fuller working definition, where the audience needs more precision:

> A BIM Execution Plan defines how the project team will produce, manage,
> exchange, coordinate, review and deliver information to satisfy the project's
> information requirements.

**These are teaching definitions.** They are written for spoken delivery to a
mixed audience. They do **not** replace, amend or restate the wording of the
authoritative BEP, whose own purpose statement is in
[`bep/Harrismith-Fire-Station-BEP.md`](../../bep/Harrismith-Fire-Station-BEP.md)
section 1.1. Where the two differ in emphasis, the BEP is correct.

## 5. Scope of this module

**In scope:**

- what a BEP is, and what it is not;
- the roles that prepare, review and authorise it;
- the shape of the Harrismith documentation set and why it is shaped that way;
- the distinction between agreed governance and evidence of implementation;
- the transfer question for Triviron, at the level of *what decisions Triviron
  will have to take*.

**Out of scope for Module 1:**

- detailed role allocation — Module 2;
- ISO 19650 in its own right — Module 3;
- the CDE state model in detail — Module 4;
- reading the matrices row by row — Module 5;
- the coordination cycle in detail — Module 6;
- actual Triviron BEP development — Module 7;
- workshop facilitation technique — Module 8.

Module 1 introduces each of those at the depth needed for the audience to
understand why they exist. It does not teach them.

## 7. Honesty constraints on delivery

Three things must be said, or the presentation misrepresents its own example:

1. **Harrismith is a training and reference implementation.** It is
   non-contractual, no appointment exists behind it, and no participant holds
   real professional authority through a role in it (BEP 1.2, TA-01, TA-02).
2. **The documentation set is approved with conditions, not issued.** Training
   Baseline 0.1 was approved with conditions through AD-001 on 2026-08-01;
   conditions remain active and **publication remains NOT AUTHORISED** (BEP 1.2).
3. **Agreement is not implementation.** Harrismith demonstrates a governed
   agreement about information. It does not demonstrate a team currently running
   that agreement on live work (BEP 12.3).

Stating these does not weaken the presentation. It is the point of slide 9 —
approval versus implementation — and saying it out loud is the most credible
thing in the talk.

## 8. Success criteria

The module has succeeded when the presenter can:

- deliver 20 minutes without reading from notes;
- explain the need for a BEP without using the phrase "BIM Execution Plan";
- answer "isn't this just a Revit standard?" without becoming defensive;
- say accurately what Harrismith has and has not established;
- name the decisions a Triviron BEP would have to take for itself.

Exercises for each of these are in [`exercises.md`](exercises.md).
