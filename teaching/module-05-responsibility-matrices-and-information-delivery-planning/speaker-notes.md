## Slide 1 — Module 5: from who holds a role to what must be produced

**Allocated: 1.0 min.** Opening slide — keep it moving.

### What you are doing here

Three things, in order: name the module, connect it backwards, and **fix the
status of the three resources before anyone sees their contents.**

The third is the one that matters. Once the audience has seen a matrix, they
stop asking what it is and start reading cells. **The status has to land first.**

### Delivery

**Open on the handover, not the title.** The title is on the slide; say the
handover instead:

> *"Module 2 told us who holds each role. Module 4 told us how information moves
> between states. Put those two together and you still cannot tell me what your
> task team has to produce next week, for whom, or in what form. That gap is
> this module."*

**Then the three resources.** Read the three questions from the slide. Do not
elaborate — Slide 3 does that properly. This is a naming, not an explanation.

**Then the status, deliberately slowly.** This is the part presenters skip.

> *"All three are approved with conditions, on the first of August, under
> decision AD-001. The conditions are still active. Publication is not
> authorised. And two of the three go further — they classify their own contents
> as proposed governance."*

**One sentence to close:**

> *"Nothing in this module is claimed to be running."*

### The trap on this slide

**Do not let "approved" become "approved" full stop.** `M5-S1-04`, `M5-S1-05`
and `M5-S1-06` are a set. If you deliver only the first, the audience leaves
believing three documents sit at one status level. They do not:

- **`S3`** — every §3 allocation is `PROPOSED GOVERNANCE`, from `TA-03`.
- **`S4`** — **all** entries are `PROPOSED GOVERNANCE` / training delivery
  planning.
- **`S2`** — a **split rule**: allocations are *"functional governance proposals
  unless the BEP already expressly establishes the allocation."*

That last one is subtle and worth the extra breath. Two `S2` allocations **are**
expressly established by the BEP: Task-Team Lead authorisation to share (`S1`
§9.4), and publication authority as **unresolved** (`S1` §9.7). Everything else
is a proposal.

### If asked

**"So is it approved or isn't it?"**
Both, at different levels. The *document* is approved with conditions. Most of
its *content* is classified as proposed. That is not a contradiction — it is a
document whose approval says *this is the agreed proposal*, not *this is now
how the project runs*.

**"Who approved it?"**
The Training Baseline Approver function, under `AG-001`, exercised by the
Training Implementation Owner. **A training function, not a professional
appointment.** Do not go further; `E2` sources are outside this module.

**"Can we publish this?"**
No. The publication hold is active and publication is not authorised. If someone
raises the publication-arrangement approval — `PAD-001` — see the warning under
Slide 3.

### Do not say

- That any matrix or schedule is "in place", "live", "in use" or "implemented".
- Any person's or company's name.
- That the three resources have the same status.

## Slide 2 — Knowing the role and the transition is not yet a plan

**Allocated: 1.5 min.** The module's argument slide.

### What you are doing here

Two moves. First, establish the question the module answers, **from the BEP's own
list**. Second — and this is the harder one — separate *what is allocated* from
*what has been observed to happen*.

### Delivery

**Start with the BEP's eight fields.** They are on the slide. Read them as a
list, because the list is the point: this is what BEP §10.5 says every task team
should be able to answer.

Then stop on the one that is missing.

> *"Notice what is not in that list. Not 'by when'. The BEP asks 'for which
> event'. And the delivery schedule is blunt about why: no real delivery dates or
> client milestones have been established. Every timing entry reads
> 'event-triggered or TBD'."*

**This is a correction, and it is worth naming as one.** The obvious version of
this module's question ends *"and by when?"*. It cannot, here. **There is no
programme.** Say so plainly — it is one of the module's most useful honesty
moments, and it takes ten seconds.

**Then the second half.** Deliver the *Allocated / Observed* comparison as a
single unit. **Never pause between the two columns.**

> *"Six containers are allocated. One — the architectural model — has a live
> equivalent observed. The other five were not observed as live coordination
> inputs at the inspected level."*

**Immediately, without a beat:**

> *"That is not a statement that five disciplines have failed to deliver. The
> record is explicit: absence of observation is not observation of absence."*

### The trap on this slide

**`M5-S2-10` alone is a defamation of five task teams.** It must never be
delivered without `M5-S2-11` following it in the same breath. If you are rushed
and can only deliver one, **deliver neither** — drop to the summary line
*"allocation is not evidence of performance"* and move on.

The same discipline applies to `GCR-006`. *"No complete governed coordination
cycle has been evidenced"* is a statement about **evidence**, not about
competence, effort or intent.

### The point to land

> *"A matrix tells you what someone is responsible for. It never tells you
> whether they have done it. Those are two different questions and they need two
> different sources."*

`S2` §5 says it best, and it is worth quoting directly if you have the time:

> *"It does not demonstrate that separate people perform them, and it must not be
> read as evidence of independence."*

### If asked

**"Why is only Architecture populated?"**
The record does not say, and neither should you. It records what was observed at
one level of one environment on one occasion. **Nothing else follows from it.**

**"When is the delivery due?"**
There is no date, for any event. Timing is event-triggered. If the audience finds
that uncomfortable, that discomfort is the lesson — a project without established
requirements does not acquire dates by writing them down.

**"Isn't an empty schedule a failure?"**
The schedule is not empty. It is populated with what is known and explicit about
what is not. Slide 13 covers this properly.

### Do not say

- Any date, frequency, programme or milestone.
- That five disciplines are behind, absent, inactive or uncommitted.
- That a coordination cycle has been completed.
- That the observed architectural model proves the workflow operates.

## Slide 3 — Three resources, three questions — and why they are not one RACI

**Allocated: 1.5 min.** The structural slide the rest of the module rests on.

### What you are doing here

Give the audience a durable way to tell the three resources apart, establish that
**the project refused RACI on the record**, and show the dependency direction —
then stop, before cell grammar.

### Delivery

**Lead with the three questions, verbatim from the resources.** They are short,
they are quotable, and they do the work:

- *"who performs which information-management function?"*
- *"who produces and maintains which information container?"*
- *"what is exchanged, at what event, to whom, why, in what form"*

**Then the useful observation:**

> *"Each of these three documents tells you what it does not answer, and points
> at whichever of the other two does. You do not have to work out the boundaries
> — they are written down."*

**Then the refusal.** This is the slide's centrepiece and it deserves the weight:

> *"Somebody could have written one RACI matrix here and been done. The BEP says
> no, and says why: RACI collapses checking into authorising, and coordinating
> into performing. This project depends on those distinctions, so it refused the
> tool that loses them."*

Worth adding, because it changes how people hear it:

> *"That refusal is in the BEP itself. It is not a preference somebody expressed
> — it is governance."*

**Then the dependency chain, one way only.** The schedule consumes container
refs from the container matrix. The container matrix defers Author and Checker to
the function matrix. Nothing depends on the schedule.

Quote the rule if you have time — it is a good example of a document defending
its own integrity:

> *"No container is scheduled that does not exist in that matrix."*

**Close on the word *row*.**

> *"One last thing, and it catches everybody. In the function matrix a row is a
> function. In the container matrix a row is a container. In the schedule a row
> is a container inside an event. Same word, three objects. If you are ever lost
> in one of these documents, ask what a row is."*

### The trap on this slide

**Do not start reading cells.** The audience will want to; Slides 6 to 8 own
cell grammar and Slides 9 to 12 own the delivery events. If a question pulls you
there, park it explicitly: *"That is two slides away."*

**Do not present the change-propagation gap as sloppiness.** No source defines
how a change in one resource propagates to the others, and the change-approval
authority (`A2`) is itself `TBD`. That is a **recorded unresolved matter**, not a
drafting error — and pointing it out is exactly the kind of reading this module
is teaching.

### The publication warning

**If anyone says "but publication was approved" — stop and separate the two
senses.** This is the single most dangerous available misreading in the whole
module.

| Sense | Status |
|---|---|
| **Project information** publication / exchange — `S1` §9.7 | **UNRESOLVED.** `T4` blocked; information remains **Shared** |
| **Training-baseline publication arrangement** — `PAD-001` | Arrangement approved with conditions; **`PE-3` not authorised**; hold **active** |

`PAD-001`'s scope is *"Training Baseline 0.1 publication arrangement only"* —
publishing the Harrismith **documentation set**. And `AD-001` states expressly
that it does **not** *"establish project publication / exchange authority"*.

**Neither resolves the other. Say that, and move on.**

### If asked

**"Why not just merge them?"**
Because the merge is where the distinctions die. The moment *performs* and
*authorises* share a cell, you have lost the thing the whole BEP is built on.

**"Isn't this over-engineered for one fire station?"**
It is a training implementation, and the structure is the subject. The honest
answer is that a smaller project might combine documents — but it would still
have to keep the questions apart, and it would have to say so.

**"Which one do I read first?"**
Follow the dependency backwards. If you want to know what your team owes,
start at the schedule; it will send you to the container matrix, which will send
you to the function matrix.

### Do not say

- That the three could be shown as one table "for simplicity".
- That `P`, `Ck` and `Au` are RACI by another name.
- Anything about cell values, delivery events or `TRN-E03`.
- That the undefined change-propagation route is an error in the documents.

## Slide 4 — Who performs which function — the IM Responsibility Matrix

**Allocated: 1.5 min.** The first of the two matrix slides.

### Opening sentence

> *"This is the matrix most people think they already understand, and it is the
> one they most often misread. So let us be precise about what it allocates."*

### Conceptual explanation

Two objects, and the whole slide depends on holding them apart.

A **function** is an activity the process requires — *maintain the BEP
framework*, *perform the readiness check*, *authorise the share*. A **role** is
the governance position that holds a function in that activity — Task-Team Lead,
Checker, CDE Administration.

The matrix crosses them: **33 functions down, nine roles across**, in seven
subject groups. Each intersection records what that role holds in that activity.

Say the division of labour explicitly, because it explains why the matrix looks
sparse:

> *"BEP Section 5 defines what each function means. The matrix records how those
> functions are allocated. Meaning is defined once; allocation is recorded once.
> That is why the matrix carries almost no prose — the definitions are somewhere
> else on purpose."*

### Harrismith worked example

Use the production sequence, **without reading the codes**:

> *"Take authoring a model. One role produces it. A different role checks it. A
> third role authorises it to be shared. Three roles, three functions, three
> separate rows — and the project deliberately refused a notation that would
> have let checking and authorising share a cell."*

That points forward to Slide 6 without teaching it.

### Critical distinction

**The matrix allocates to roles, not to people or companies.** The wording is
worth quoting because it is unusually blunt:

> *"Functional roles only. No holder is established for any of them."*

and

> *"…not to companies or people. No organisation is appointed and no individual
> is named."*

The BEP backs both up: *"These are functions, not job titles and not people"*
(§4.6), and the role model is *"not an appointment chart and not an organisation
chart"* (§5.2).

### Status qualification

**Do not let this matrix flatten into one status.** The document is approved with
conditions; the allocations are **proposals unless the BEP expressly establishes
them**. Give the audience one of each:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Established** | Task-Team Lead authorises WIP → Shared — BEP §9.4 |
| **Established as unresolved** | Publication / exchange authority — BEP §9.7 |

> *"Same matrix, same column format, two completely different governance
> weights. One is settled. One is recorded as unsettled — and recorded that way
> on purpose, rather than filled with whichever role looked closest."*

### Likely audience misunderstanding

**"So this tells us who is responsible."** No — it tells you which *role* holds
which function. Whether anybody holds that role is a separate question, and the
answer here is that nobody does yet.

The second common one: **"we could just put names in the columns."** That would
convert a governance instrument into a staffing chart and destroy the thing that
makes it traceable.

### Prohibited inference

- That a populated allocation proves someone was **appointed**.
- That a populated allocation proves the activity **happened**.
- That the matrix demonstrates **independence** — *"it must not be read as
  evidence of independence."*
- **Do not define a single function code.** You may say a code vocabulary
  exists; Slides 6 and 7 own it. If you find yourself explaining what `Ck`
  means, stop.

### Transition to Slide 5

> *"That matrix tells you who performs each function. It deliberately does not
> tell you who authors the architectural model. For that there is a second
> matrix — and it allocates a completely different object."*

### Recovery line if time is short

> *"One matrix, one question: who performs which information-management
> function. Roles, not people — and not every allocation carries the same
> weight."*

Then go straight to Slide 5.

## Slide 5 — Who produces which container — the Model / Information Responsibility Matrix

**Allocated: 1.5 min.** The second matrix slide, and the module's organisational
trap-defusing slide.

### Opening sentence

> *"Different matrix, different object. This one does not allocate functions at
> all — it allocates information containers."*

### Conceptual explanation

The chain is the slide:

**party → task team → discipline → information container**

Four stages, each a different kind of thing. A *party* is an organisation. A
*task team* produces a defined package of information. A *discipline* classifies
the information domain. A *container* is the thing produced.

Six discipline containers are allocated — `ARC-01`, `STR-01`, `MEC-01`,
`ELE-01`, `PLM-01`, `FIR-01` — plus `COORD-01`, recorded separately.

### Harrismith worked example

**Count the parties on the slide with the audience.** It is the most effective
ten seconds in the module:

> *"Six containers. Six discipline codes. How many organisations? Four.
> Mechanical, Electrical and Plumbing are three task teams inside one MEP
> Consultant. And Fire is not part of MEP at all — it is a separate consultant."*

Then the rule that generalises it:

> *"A discipline code identifies the information domain of a container. That is
> all it does. It does not tell you the organisation, the task team, the author,
> the collaboration team, or who carries contractual responsibility."*

The BEP goes further and lists five concepts that must not be swapped — party,
task team, discipline, Autodesk collaboration team, IM role — and says *"a
mapping is not an identity."* They often line up. That is not the same as being
the same thing.

### Critical distinction

**Originator responsibility does not move.** It stays with the producing task
team *"through sharing, consumption, coordination and publication. No downstream
act relieves it."*

And the corollary that catches people who work in the platform daily:
**"Authorship is not inferred from folder location."** Where a container sits
tells you where it sits. The originator is recorded, not deduced.

### `COORD-01` — say only this

> *"There is a coordination information class as well. It is a construct for
> bringing separate discipline information together temporarily. It does not
> merge authorship, it does not create a new author, it does not move technical
> responsibility, and it is not a deliverable unless somebody explicitly
> schedules it as one."*

**Stop there.** Federation mechanics and the coordination cycle are Module 6.

### Status qualification

Every allocation in section 3 is **`PROPOSED GOVERNANCE`**, from the training
organisation model. And the document says plainly what it is not:

> *"It is not an inventory of what currently exists in the CDE."*

Deliver the allocated-versus-observed contrast as one unit, exactly as on
Slide 2:

> *"Six containers are allocated. One — the architectural model — has a live
> equivalent observed. The other five were not observed as live coordination
> inputs at the inspected level. And that is not a statement that they are
> missing: absence of observation is not observation of absence."*

Also worth naming, briefly: **no format is mandated**, and **level of information
need is not defined for any container**. Revit appears because it was observed,
not because it was required.

### Likely audience misunderstanding

**"So there are six consultants."** No — four parties. This is the single most
common misreading of a discipline-coded container list, and the BEP flags it
itself.

**"The other five disciplines have not started."** The record does not say that
and neither should you.

**"So we should be modelling in Revit."** Observed use is not a delivery
requirement.

### Prohibited inference

- That every planned container **exists**.
- That `ARC-01`'s observation proves the **matrix** is implemented.
- That a discipline code identifies an organisation, author or platform team.
- That `COORD-01` is jointly authored, carries responsibility, or is a
  deliverable.
- That originator responsibility passes to a checker, coordinator, recipient or
  accepter.
- Container dependencies, scheduling, formats or delivery fields — **Slides
  9–12**.

### Transition to Slide 6

> *"So one matrix allocates functions and the other allocates containers. Both
> are read cell by cell — and the cells have a vocabulary the project chose very
> deliberately. That is next."*

### Recovery line if time is short

> *"Second matrix, second question: who produces which container. Party, task
> team, discipline, container — four different things, and six discipline codes
> do not make six companies."*

Then go to Slide 6.
