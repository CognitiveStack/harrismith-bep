# Module 5 — Exercises

**Status:** Presenter rehearsal and self-assessment material. **Not governance.**
**Not an audience examination.**

These exercises test whether the presenter can deliver Module 5 **without
manufacturing an answer the sources do not contain**. Most of them are failed by
saying too much, not too little.

**79 exercises, numbered consecutively**, across eight sections.

**No rehearsal has been performed.** Every timing in this file is an
**allocation**. `E79` defines a full twenty-minute run; **it records no
performance, and until it is performed externally the module's timing remains
`20.0 minutes allocated — not measured`.**

Slide content: [`presentation-outline.md`](presentation-outline.md). Delivery
guidance: [`speaker-notes.md`](speaker-notes.md). Statement classification and
the prohibited-claims register: [`source-map.md`](source-map.md).

## Section A — One exercise per slide

Each tests the slide's **load-bearing point**, not a summary.

### E1 — Slide 1 · three statuses, not one

**Task.** From memory, state the status of the three principal resources at
three levels: the document status, the content classification, and the
publication position.

**Why.** The most common Slide 1 failure is delivering *"approved with
conditions"* and stopping, which leaves the audience believing all three sit at
one status level.

**What good looks like.** Approved with conditions under `AD-001`; `S3` §3 and
all of `S4` additionally classified `PROPOSED GOVERNANCE`; `S2` carries a **split
rule**; publication **NOT AUTHORISED**.

**Success criteria.** All three levels, and the split rule named.

**Common failure.** Flattening the three into one status.

### E2 — Slide 2 · the pair that must not be separated

**Task.** Deliver the *Allocated / Observed* comparison in one breath, with the
absence-of-observation line attached.

**Why.** Delivering the observed column alone is a statement about five task
teams that the record expressly forbids.

**What good looks like.** Six allocated; `ARC-01` observed at the inspected
level; *"absence of observation is not observation of absence"* — with no pause
between.

**Success criteria.** The caution arrives before the audience can draw the
inference.

**Common failure.** A pause, a slide change, or a question taken between the two
halves.

### E3 — Slide 3 · the refusal, with its reason

**Task.** State that RACI is not adopted, and give the recorded reason.

**Why.** Without the reason it sounds like a preference. With it, it is
governance.

**What good looks like.** *"RACI is not adopted"* — because it *"collapses"*
checking from authorising and coordinating from performing — **and** that this is
in the BEP itself, at §5.12.

**Success criteria.** Refusal, reason and source, in under twenty seconds.

**Common failure.** Presenting the refusal as the author's stylistic choice.

### E4 — Slide 4 · the split rule, both ways

**Task.** Explain the `S2` split status rule using one allocation the BEP
establishes and one it establishes as unresolved.

**Why.** The rule is abstract until it is shown cutting in both directions.

**What good looks like.** Proposals *"unless the BEP already expressly
establishes the allocation"*; `P4` established by §9.4; `D4` established as
**unresolved** by §9.7.

**Success criteria.** Both examples, correctly attributed.

**Common failure.** Giving only the established case, so the rule reads as a
formality.

### E5 — Slide 5 · count the parties

**Task.** Given the six discipline codes, state how many parties there are and
why.

**What good looks like.** **Four.** MEC, ELE and PLM are three task teams inside
one MEP Consultant; FIR is a separate Fire Consultant, **not an MEP sub-team**.
And: *"a discipline code identifies the information domain of a container. That
is all it does."*

**Success criteria.** Four parties, with both traps named.

**Common failure.** Six organisations; or Fire treated as part of MEP.

### E6 — Slide 6 · seven, plus two

**Task.** Give all seven function terms with their controlled definitions, then
the two values that are not functions.

**Why.** The two non-function values carry every unresolved allocation in the
matrix, and are the ones most often left out.

**What good looks like.** Perform · Check · Authorise · Coordinate · Accept ·
Consult · Inform, each with its §5.12 wording; then `TBD` and `—`, **explicitly
not codes and not equivalents**.

**Success criteria.** Nine values, correct definitions, and the distinction
stated.

**Common failure.** Seven only; or offering a RACI mapping "for convenience".

### E7 — Slide 7 · what a cell does not prove

**Task.** State what a populated cell records, then four things it does not.

**What good looks like.** It records which function that role holds in that
activity. It does **not** record a name, an appointment, that the activity
occurred, or that a check was independent. *(Competence, contractual authority,
platform permission and completion are equally acceptable.)*

**Success criteria.** The positive statement first, then at least four
negatives.

**Common failure.** Reading the cell as a record of work done.

### E8 — Slide 8 · columns are not people

**Task.** Explain why separate Author and Checker columns do not establish
separate people, in under thirty seconds.

**What good looks like.** A column is a functional role, not a headcount; the
matrix *"must not be read as evidence of independence"*; where roles combine the
functional distinction survives and the combination is **recorded**.

**Success criteria.** No claim of independence, and no suggestion of a defect.

**Common failure.** Presenting role combination as a failing.

### E9 — Slide 9 · the four misread fields

**Task.** Name the four schedule fields most often misread, and say what each
actually records.

**What good looks like.** `Status` — the schedule entry's status. `State /
Suitability` — the CDE state and permitted use. `Acceptance Criteria` — process
criteria for receipt, **not** who may accept. `Delivery ID` — an internal
training reference, **not** a document number.

**Success criteria.** All four, with the negative attached to each.

**Common failure.** Treating `Status` as the information state.

### E10 — Slide 10 · three statuses

**Task.** Give the three event statuses and what each means.

**What good looks like.** `PROPOSED` — planned governance, not an executed
exchange and not approval to execute. `CONDITIONAL` — activates only on its
stated trigger. `BLOCKED` — cannot proceed while required governance or inputs
are unresolved.

**Success criteria.** Three statuses, three distinct meanings, and *blocked is
not overdue*.

**Common failure.** Reading `PROPOSED` as approved, or `BLOCKED` as late.

### E11 — Slide 11 · uses, not is

**Task.** State the general rule, then apply it to all three events.

**What good looks like.** An event may require, depend upon or exercise a
transition **without being it**. `TRN-E01` uses `T1` then `T3`. `TRN-E02` uses
`T8`, then `T1` and `T3`. `TRN-E03` depends on `T4`. **None is the transition it
uses.**

**Success criteria.** Rule first, then three correct mappings and three explicit
"no"s.

**Common failure.** *"`TRN-E01` is basically `T1`."*

### E12 — Slide 12 · five, with their statuses

**Task.** List the five `TRN-E03` blocking matters **with the exact status word
recorded against each**.

**What good looks like.** Publication authority `UNRESOLVED — TBD`; acceptance
authority `UNRESOLVED — TBD`; recipient identity `Not established`; required
formats `Not established`; deliverable set `Not defined`. **And that they are
independent.**

**Success criteria.** Five matters, five typed statuses, and the independence
rule.

**Common failure.** Listing them as "missing information", losing the typing.

### E13 — Slide 13 · the pair that looks alike

**Task.** Distinguish `TBD` from `—` in two sentences.

**What good looks like.** `TBD` — the allocation is unresolved and the BEP
records the authority as not established. `—` — **a populated cell recording a
resolved answer**: this role has no function in this activity. **Not blank, not
unresolved, not equivalent.**

**Success criteria.** Both definitions, and the word *blank* used only to deny
it.

**Common failure.** Calling the dash a blank.

### E14 — Slide 14 · answer nothing

**Task.** Deliver Slide 14 and stop, without answering a single question on
behalf of Triviron.

**Why.** This is the only slide where the failure mode is being helpful.

**What good looks like.** Five groups named, two or three questions voiced per
group, the four transferable habits, and the closing marker: *`Triviron
responsibility and delivery basis — not yet established.`*

**Success criteria.** No Triviron fact, no recommendation, no "I would suggest".

**Common failure.** Offering the Harrismith answer as a starting point.

## Section B — Resource-boundary drills

### E15 — Which matrix?

**Task.** In one sentence each, state what the Information Management
Responsibility Matrix answers and what the Model / Information Responsibility
Matrix answers.

**What good looks like.** *"Who performs which information-management
function?"* against *"Who produces and maintains which information container?"*

**Common failure.** Describing both as "who does what".

### E16 — Matrix or schedule?

**Task.** Given a question, say which of the three resources answers it. Work
through: *who checks this model* · *who produces the fire model* · *what format
is this exchanged in* · *who may accept it* · *what event does this belong to*.

**What good looks like.** IM matrix · container matrix · schedule · IM matrix
`D7` **and** the schedule's `Acceptance Criteria`, both unresolved · schedule.

**Common failure.** Sending format or event questions to a matrix.

### E17 — Function or holder?

**Task.** Explain the difference between an information-management **function**
and the **holder** of a role, and state how many holders Harrismith has
established.

**What good looks like.** A function is an activity the process requires; a role
holds a function; a holder is a person. **No holder is established for any
role.**

**Common failure.** Treating a role column as a person.

### E18 — Role or organisation?

**Task.** State why a functional role is not an organisation, citing the BEP.

**What good looks like.** *"These are functions, not job titles and not
people"* (§4.6); the role model is *"not an appointment chart and not an
organisation chart"* (§5.2).

### E19 — Party or task team?

**Task.** Define both, and give the Harrismith example where one party holds
three task teams.

**What good looks like.** A party is an organisation; a task team produces a
defined package of information. **MEP Consultant** holds Mechanical, Electrical
and Plumbing.

### E20 — Discipline or organisation?

**Task.** State the five things a discipline code does **not** identify.

**What good looks like.** The organisation · the task team · the author · a
Design Collaboration team · contractual responsibility. *"That is all it does."*

**Common failure.** Reading `MEC-01` as a company.

### E21 — Container or deliverable?

**Task.** Distinguish an information container from a deliverable, and say what
Harrismith has defined of each.

**What good looks like.** *"A delivery is not synonymous with a file"* — one
exchange may carry several containers. Six containers plus `COORD-01` are
allocated; **no final deliverable set exists**, and the BEP *"does not define the
project's final deliverable list"*.

### E22 — Allocation or performance?

**Task.** State the single sentence that separates allocation from performance,
and name its source.

**What good looks like.** *"It does not demonstrate that separate people perform
them, and it must not be read as evidence of independence"* — IM Responsibility
Matrix §5.

**Common failure.** Attributing it to the BEP, or to a professional standard.

## Section C — Grammar and cell-reading drills

### E23 — The seven, cold

**Task.** Write out all seven function terms and their controlled definitions
without notes, then check against BEP §5.12.

**Success criteria.** Seven terms, seven meanings, no invented eighth.

### E24 — `TBD` against `—`

**Task.** State four things `TBD` does not mean, and three things `—` does not
mean.

**What good looks like.** `TBD` ≠ a holder exists unnamed · the answer is known
informally · it will necessarily be resolved · the field may be inferred.
`—` ≠ blank · unresolved · absent from the project.

### E25 — Combined values

**Task.** Explain what `Co Ck` records, then list what it does **not** record.

**What good looks like.** The role holds both functions in that activity. It
records **no** sequence, priority, chronology, hierarchy, competence, separate
people or successful performance — because **no source states an order**.

**Common failure.** *"Coordinate first, then check."*

### E26 — `P4` against `D4`

**Task.** Explain why two structurally identical rows carry different authority
statuses.

**What good looks like.** `P4` carries **`Au`**, established by **BEP §9.4**.
`D4` carries **five `TBD` cells** — AP, LDP, BM, BC, TTL — and **four `—` cells**
— Aut, Chk, CDE, Rcp. Publication / exchange authority is **unresolved** under
**BEP §9.7**, and **no allocation is made**. **Neither row is blank.** Both are
governed positions.

**Common failure.** Saying `D4` is `TBD` in every column — four of its nine cells
are `—`; or treating the five `TBD` roles as a shortlist of candidates.

**Common failure.** Treating `D4` as an oversight.

### E27 — `TBD Ac`

**Task.** Say what `TBD Ac` at `D7` records, and what it proves.

**What good looks like.** Acceptance is the relevant function at that cell; the
allocation and the authority are unresolved. **It proves no exchange has been
accepted**, and resolves nothing.

### E28 — Checking is not authorising

**Task.** Give the controlled wording, from both sources.

**What good looks like.** *"Check is not Authorise. Checking confirms readiness
for the next controlled decision; it does not permit progression"* (`S2`); and
*"Checking does not automatically authorise sharing"* (BEP §9.3).

### E29 — Role combination

**Task.** Explain what survives when Author and Checker are the same person.

**What good looks like.** The functional distinction; a self-check is *"still a
checking act with a defined requirement, not an omission of one"*; and the
combination is **recorded** so the limitation stays visible.

### E30 — Independence

**Task.** Answer, in one sentence: *has independent checking been established on
this project?*

**What good looks like.** **No** — and independence is *"never claimed where it
does not exist"*.

**Common failure.** Any answer that implies it has, or that this is a defect.

### E31 — Two `Au` cells

**Task.** State how many cells in the matrix carry `Au`, and what that tells
you.

**What good looks like.** **Two**, in 297 cells — both held by the Task-Team
Lead. Authorising is rare and deliberately so. **Say that the count is an
observation from reading the document, not a governance statement.**

### E32 — One `Ac` cell

**Task.** State where `Ac` appears and what follows from it.

**What good looks like.** **Once**, inside `TBD Ac` at `D7`. **No acceptance
allocation is resolved anywhere in the matrix.**

## Section G — Misconception and hostile-question responses

Each answer should be **bounded, short and source-anchored**.

### E61 — "Why not just use RACI?"

**Bounded answer.** Because the BEP refused it on the record: RACI *"collapses"*
checking from authorising and coordinating from performing, and this project
depends on both distinctions. It is not a preference — it is §5.12.

### E62 — "Who is accountable?"

**Bounded answer.** No holder is established for any role. The matrix allocates
**functions to roles**, and *"no organisation is appointed and no individual is
named"*. Accountability in the contractual sense is not established here.

### E63 — "Does `TBD` mean the BIM Manager decides?"

**Bounded answer.** No. See `E54`.

### E64 — "Why are there six disciplines but four parties?"

**Bounded answer.** MEC, ELE and PLM are three task teams within one MEP
Consultant; FIR is a separate Fire Consultant. A discipline code identifies the
**information domain** of a container and nothing else.

### E65 — "Does a filled matrix prove the work was done?"

**Bounded answer.** No. It records what a role is responsible for. Whether
anything was done needs different evidence — and on this project most of it does
not exist. Six containers are allocated; one has a live equivalent observed.

### E66 — "Why does one source count five blockers and another six?"

**Bounded answer.** See `E43`. **Do not resolve the variance.**

### E67 — "Is blocked just a polite word for incomplete?"

**Bounded answer.** No. See `E55`. Governance can intentionally stop a workflow,
and a route that cannot legitimately proceed should visibly halt.

### E68 — "Why not put RVT and PDF in the schedule now?"

**Bounded answer.** Because no approved format standard exists, and *"an observed
format is not an approved delivery requirement"*. Revit, IFC, PDF and NWC appear
in observed context only. Writing one in would create a requirement nobody
approved.

### E69 — "Can we just copy Harrismith for Triviron?"

**Bounded answer.** Use Harrismith as an example of **decision structure**, not
as a populated Triviron template. Its organisation is a declared training model
under `TA-03`; its **container allocations and schedule entries are proposed
governance**; and its **IM Responsibility Matrix carries a split status** —
proposals unless the BEP expressly establishes them, as it does for `P4` and, in
the other direction, for `D4` being unresolved. **None of it transfers
automatically.**

**Common failure.** Saying simply *"the allocations are proposals"* — which
flattens the split status and breaks prohibited claim 5.

### E70 — "Has this workflow actually been implemented?"

**Bounded answer.** No, and the project says so. The CDE strategy classifies
itself `PROPOSED GOVERNANCE` and states it *"does not describe the live
platform"*. `GCR-006` — one complete governed coordination cycle — remains
**open**.
