# Module 3 — Deck Specification

**Status:** Production specification. **Not governance.** Not a built deck.

---

## 1. Global details

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Title** | ISO 19650 Information-Management Principles |
| **Subtitle** | What the framework governs — and where our evidence stops |
| **Module** | Module 3 — ISO 19650 Information-Management Principles |
| **Audience** | Mixed multidisciplinary project audience — discipline leads, project managers, at least one CDE administrator, and at least one person whose interest is commercial or contractual |
| **Planned duration** | **20 minutes**, plus questions. **Never measured** |
| **Slides** | **14** |
| **Objective** | Enable the audience to understand what ISO 19650 is intended to govern, why it concerns information management rather than software, and **where the evidence available to this programme stops** |
| **Central teaching message** | *ISO 19650 provides a structured approach for defining, producing, exchanging and managing the information needed to support decisions throughout an asset's life cycle.* **Teaching wording derived from publicly supported scope — not an ISO quotation** |
| **Opening proposition** | When people hear "the BIM standard" they picture software. That expectation has to be removed before anything else lands |
| **Closing proposition** | ISO alignment is not inherited from a template or a platform; it must be established for the specific project through applicable requirements, appointments, authorised processes and evidence |

**One global honesty position, stated on Slide 1 and never withdrawn:** the
standard has **not been read for this programme**. What is taught is public
scope, jurisdiction-labelled guidance and this project's own evidence.

## 2. Narrative arc

Five sections. The audience should feel the hinge between each.

| # | Section | Slides | Time | The move it makes |
|---|---|---|---:|---|
| **A** | **What ISO 19650 is** | 1, 2, 3 | **4.0** | From "the BIM standard" to *an information-management framework* |
| **B** | **Why information management comes first** | 4, 5, 6 | **4.5** | From producing information to **needing** it |
| **C** | **Who manages information** | 7, 8, 9 | **4.5** | From job titles to **functions, and the things responsibility attaches to** |
| **D** | **How control and planning work** | 10, 11 | **3.0** | From "where files live" to **permitted use and planned exchange** |
| **E** | **Application, limits and transfer** | 12, 13, 14 | **4.0** | From principle to **evidence — and to what cannot be claimed** |
| | **Total** | **14** | **20.0** | |

**Section A is front-loaded and carries the module.** The audience's existing
belief about "the BIM standard" is corrected here or not at all.

**Slide 13 is the longest single slide at 2.0 minutes, and must not be cut.** It
is the only place in the programme where a conformity misunderstanding gets
corrected. **If time is short, recover from Slide 4 or Slide 10** — both have
later modules behind them.

**Slides 10 and 11 are capped at 1.5 minutes each on purpose.** They belong in
detail to Modules 4 and 5. **The cap is the module boundary, expressed as time.**

## 3. Design approach

**16:9. Restrained professional BIM-management style. No ISO branding used as
decoration. No final Triviron visual identity** — that is a later decision,
deliberately not taken here.

### 3.1 Format and hierarchy

| Element | Specification |
|---|---|
| Aspect | **16:9** |
| Title position | **Consistent across all fourteen slides** — top-left, single line where possible |
| Body text | Projection-readable. Nothing smaller than the source-label size in §3.3 |
| Message | **One principal message per slide.** If it needs an "and", it is two slides |
| Whitespace | Disciplined. A thin, honest slide is the correct output where the evidence is thin |
| Diagrams | **Original constructions only.** Rebuilt as native PowerPoint shapes |

### 3.2 Prohibited throughout

| Prohibited | |
|---|---|
| **Any ISO diagram, table or figure** — reproduced, redrawn, adapted or reconstructed | Including from memory, from search results, from third-party versions, or as a "simplified" derivative |
| **Platform screenshots** | Any product interface, folder tree or permissions view |
| **Product logos used as evidence** | Autodesk or any other |
| **Green compliance grammar** | Green fills, ticks, crosses, traffic lights |
| **Scorecards and maturity ladders** | Percentages, levels, progress bars, completion indicators |
| **Copied ISO forms or headings** | Beyond the registered document titles and part numbers |
| Stock imagery, photographs, avatars, human silhouettes | — |

### 3.3 Labels

| Label type | Where | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Source-class label** | Wherever a panel could be mistaken for ISO's own | Small, legible, persistent — **not a footnote** |
| **Jurisdiction label** | **Every** guidance item — Slides 5, 6, 7, 8 | `Official implementation guidance — UK context`, visually distinct |
| **Visible warning** | Slides 3, 4, 5, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14 | On the slide, at readable size |

**The thirteen required visible labels are listed in
[`slide-copy.md`](slide-copy.md) §2 and must appear verbatim.**

### 3.4 Connector semantics — consistent across the deck

| Notation | Meaning | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Solid arrow** | **Supported progression** — a source establishes it | Slides 1, 5, 7, 12; **Slide 10 `WIP → Shared` only** |
| **Dashed arrow** | **Blocked, unresolved or future** | Slides 2, 10, 11, 12 |
| **Plain line** | Relationship **without** authority, sequence or equivalence | Slides 4, 9 |
| **No line** | **No established relationship** | **Slide 8 — the two vocabularies are never connected** |

**Solid arrows only where supported progression exists.** On Slide 10 that means
**exactly one solid connector in the whole diagram**.

## 5. The five deliberately incomplete or imbalanced visuals

**These are automatic production requirements. Completing, aligning, balancing or
filling any of them is a content failure, not a fix.**

### 5.1 Slide 8 — rows remain offset and unpaired

Rows are **deliberately offset** across the gutter. **`lead appointed party` is
never drawn directly opposite `Lead Delivery Party`.** `appointed party` faces
**empty space**. **No arrows, no matched colours, no equals signs.**

**Aligning the rows asserts a terminology mapping nobody has made** — and does it
without a word being spoken.

### 5.2 Slide 10 — `Shared → Published` remains blocked

**Exactly one solid connector** appears in the diagram: `WIP → Shared`, because
its authorising function is established. `Shared → Published` is **dashed or
visibly halted**; `Published → Record / Retained` is unreachable.

**A complete route claims a workflow that has no available authorising
function.** Every design instinct will push toward completing that arrow because
a broken chain looks unfinished. **It is not unfinished. It is accurate.**

### 5.3 Slide 11 — evidence fields remain empty

The **evidence-status column exists and is empty for every event**. No tick, no
completed marker, no progress bar. **No actual date.** Recipient and acceptance
authority stay **unpopulated**.

**Nothing has been delivered on this project.** A populated column is a claim of
executed delivery.

### 5.4 Slide 12 — evidence incomplete; assessment outside the chain

The **evidence stage is drawn open** — not crossed out, not red, not marked
failed. **Conformity assessment sits outside the chain**, attached by a dashed
annotation, **never as an eighth step**.

**Making assessment the terminus implies the chain leads there.** It does not.

### 5.5 Slide 13 — direct-evidence band remains near-empty, one item

Band 1 holds **exactly one item** — that Harrismith *states* ISO 19650 principles
inform its approach. All four bands are the **same size**.

**The imbalance is the content.** A near-empty band beside a nine-item band looks
wrong, and correcting it — by promoting analogues, shrinking band 3, or adding a
reassuring line — **asserts alignment nobody evidenced.**
