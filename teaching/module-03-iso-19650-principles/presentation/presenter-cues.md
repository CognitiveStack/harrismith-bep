# Module 3 — Presenter Cues

**Status:** Delivery cues for the notes pane. **Not a script.**

Fragments, not sentences to read. The full natural-language notes remain in
[`../speaker-notes.md`](../speaker-notes.md) and are authoritative.

**No rehearsal has occurred and no timing has been measured.** Every figure below
is a planned allocation.

---

## 1. The one rule for this module

**When you state something about ISO 19650, know which of three you are doing:**
quoting **public scope**, citing **UK guidance**, or describing **Harrismith**.
**If you cannot tell, do not say the sentence.**

## 2. Thirty-second opening

> "Module 3 is about ISO 19650 — and I want to start by taking something away.
>
> When people hear 'the BIM standard', they picture software. A naming
> convention. Something you comply with by configuring a platform correctly.
>
> That's not what this is. And two things up front, because they change how you
> should listen. **We are not ISO-compliant** — our own BEP says so, in writing,
> deliberately. And **I haven't read the standard for this course.** It's a
> copyrighted document we don't hold. What I can give you is its published scope,
> some official guidance, and our own project as a worked example."

## 3. Slides 1–14

### Slide 1 — ISO 19650 is an information-management framework · 1.5 min

| Field | |
|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Establish the subject as information for decisions, before a software expectation fills the gap |
| **Cues** | *"Taking something away"* · information a project needs to **decide** · specify · produce · exchange · record · buildings, infrastructure, any organisation · **"that's my sentence, not the standard's"** |
| **Source basis** | `X1` published scope — **four statements, and that is the entire external basis** |
| **Jurisdiction** | International |
| **Harrismith / boundary** | Not an analogy — an **admission**: the BEP claims influence and **expressly declines compliance** |
| **Likely question** | *"Is ISO 19650 a BIM software standard?"* |
| **Safe answer** | No. Published scope is information management. Software is how you'd implement it — **no product in it, no vendor, and nothing that makes you compliant by being installed** |
| **Transition** | *"So if it's not about software — what happens when you do have the software?"* |
| **Delivery warning** | **Never begin a sentence with "ISO says."** Say *"its published scope is"*, *"our BEP says"*, or *"that's my framing."* **Ten seconds of caveat, then teach** — do not over-apologise |

### Slide 2 — It governs information, not a software platform · 1.5 min

| Field | |
|---|---|

| **Source basis** | **None external.** All five statements are `H1` — this project's BEP |
| **Jurisdiction** | International (left panel) · This project (right panel) |

### Slide 4 — Information management runs across the asset life cycle · 1.5 min

| Field | |
|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Show decisions run far past handover; kill the single-eternal-model reading |
| **Cues** | need · design · build · handover · **operate (the long bit)** · alter or end · each is a **decision** · **"my words, not official phase names"** · the asset outlives the team · information must make sense **without us** |
| **Source basis** | `X1` life-cycle reach — **one statement** |
| **Jurisdiction** | International (the reach) · This project (the bracket) |
| **Harrismith / boundary** | **A gap, and a drawn one**: asset management and handover are **expressly outside current scope** (BEP §3.3). **Not a failure** |
| **Likely question** | *"Does the same BIM model continue through the whole life cycle?"* |
| **Safe answer** | No — and I'd be wary of anyone selling you that. What continues is **managed information**. Containers get superseded, converted and retired constantly. **A coordination model is usually a poor thing to hand an operations team** |
| **Transition** | *"Information has to serve decisions we may never see — so how would you know what to produce?"* |
| **Delivery warning** | **Never number the phases.** And **do not invent operations work for Harrismith** — a hypothetical gets remembered as a plan |

### Slide 5 — Information requirements come before information production · 1.5 min

| Field | |
|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Show nobody can decide what to produce until someone establishes what is needed |
| **Cues** | *"How does a team know what to produce?"* · **production begins from a requirement, not from time and tools** · decision → need → requirement → production → checked delivery → use · what decision · who · what · when · how reliable · how checked · what form |
| **Source basis** | **`X6` — UK guidance** (appointment by appointment); `X1` framing; the rest is `H1` |
| **Jurisdiction** | **Say "UK" aloud.** `Official implementation guidance — UK context` |
| **Harrismith / boundary** | **No formal information requirements available — and not invented.** Training-developed ones are labelled assumption |
| **Likely question** | *"What happens if the client doesn't know what information it needs?"* |
| **Safe answer** | Extremely common, and exactly our situation. Help them work it out from the decisions they face — or work to a **clearly labelled assumption**. **What you must not do is quietly invent requirements and present them as the client's** |
| **Transition** | *"Requirements first. But requirements for how much?"* |
| **Delivery warning** | **The acronyms.** Someone will offer one. **No definition-level source is registered** — say the concept exists, decline to define the terms, and point at a licensed copy. **The gap is not non-conformity** |

### Slide 7 — Functions are not automatically job titles · 1.5 min

| Field | |
|---|---|
| **Purpose** | Separate activity, function, appointment, title and participant |
| **Cues** | five layers · **guidance frames it as functions, not new job titles** · **ensure the function is fulfilled** — not *create a post* · one person may hold several · **combining does not merge** · approving as one, applying as another — one minute, two authorities |
| **Source basis** | **`X6` only. No `PUBLIC-SOURCE` statement on this slide** |
| **Jurisdiction** | **Say "UK" aloud.** `Official implementation guidance — UK context` |
| **Harrismith / boundary** | Functions defined; **holders empty — every one TBD**. *"Platform access is not delegation"* |
| **Likely question** | *"Does ISO require a BIM Manager?"* |
| **Safe answer** | **I can't tell you what the standard requires about titles — I haven't read it.** UK guidance frames it as functions, with the obligation to **ensure they're fulfilled**. The practical question is whether the work is assigned and being done |
| **Transition** | *"Functions get assigned to somebody. So what do we call those somebodies?"* |
| **Delivery warning** | **The over-extension.** *"So ISO doesn't require a BIM Manager"* is **one step beyond the source** — and someone will make a staffing decision on it. **Name nobody** |

## 4. Closings

### 4.1 Thirty-second module closing

> "Three things to take away.
>
> **One — ISO 19650 is about information management, not software selection.**
> Nothing you buy makes you compliant, and nothing you buy does the thinking.
>
> **Two — principles have to become project-specific requirements, assigned
> authority, and evidence.** That's the work. The principles are the easy part.
>
> **Three — never claim alignment or conformity beyond your basis.** We've got a
> project that took the principles seriously, documented what it decided, and
> wrote down what it hasn't resolved. That's a genuinely good position. It is not
> a compliance claim — and the moment we dress it up as one, we lose the thing
> that made it worth showing you."

### 4.2 Ten-second fallback closing

> "It's about information, not software. Principles have to become requirements,
> authority and evidence. And never claim alignment beyond what you can actually
> show."

**The fallback is a different script, not the same one hurried.** Say less, not
faster.

## 5. Recovery — one sentence per section

**If a section runs long, use its recovery sentence and move.**

| Section | Recovery sentence |
|---|---|
| **A** — What ISO 19650 is | *"Two published parts, two different questions, and I haven't read either — so what follows is scope, guidance and our own project."* |
| **B** — Why IM comes first | *"Information serves decisions across the asset's life, requirements come before production, and the right amount is whatever the purpose needs."* |
| **C** — Who manages information | *"Functions, not titles; two vocabularies nobody has mapped; and responsibility attaches to identifiable containers."* |
| **D** — Control and planning | *"A CDE controls permitted use, delivery is planned against a purpose — and on this project one route is deliberately stopped."* |
| **E** — Application and limits | *"Principles become requirements, authority, configuration and evidence — and our evidence runs out before the end."* |

### 5.1 The recovery warning

**Never compress Slide 13 into a compliance score.**

Under time pressure the pull is to summarise the four bands as a verdict —
*"broadly aligned"*, *"most of the way there"*, *"about half"*. **Every one of
those asserts an assessment nobody performed.**

**If Slide 13 must be shortened, drop items from bands 2 and 3 — never the
categories, never band 1's single item, and never the conclusion.** The minimum
viable Slide 13 is: four categories named, one directly evidenced item, the fact
that gaps exist, and **`No conformity conclusion supported`**.
