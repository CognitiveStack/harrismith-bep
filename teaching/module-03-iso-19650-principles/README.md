# Module 3 — ISO 19650 Information-Management Principles

**Status:** Active module. Structure, external-source control framework and
Slides 1–3 baseline only. **Not governance, and not a complete module.**

---

## 1. Objective

Enable a multidisciplinary audience to understand what ISO 19650 is intended to
govern, why it is an information-management framework rather than a software
standard, and **where the evidence available to this programme stops**.

| # | The audience should understand |
|---|---|
| 1 | What ISO 19650 is intended to govern |
| 2 | Why it concerns information management rather than software |
| 3 | Why information requirements come before information production |
| 4 | Why information must be managed across the asset life cycle |
| 5 | The difference between concepts and principles and a delivery-phase process |
| 6 | **Why information-management functions are not automatically job titles** |
| 7 | Why appointments and project-specific decisions remain necessary |
| 8 | How task teams and information containers fit the management model |
| 9 | How CDE states and exchanges support controlled use |
| 10 | Why the correct **quantity and quality** of information matters |
| 11 | **How Harrismith illustrates some principles without proving conformity** |
| 12 | What a future Triviron project must verify and decide |

Items 6, 10 and 11 are the three most likely to be got wrong, and the module is
structured around defeating them. Item 11 is the module's honesty constraint and
is not negotiable in delivery.

## 2. Central teaching message

> ISO 19650 provides a structured approach for defining, producing, exchanging
> and managing the information needed to support decisions throughout an asset's
> life cycle.

**Teaching wording derived from publicly supported scope.** It is consistent with
the official public summaries of Parts 1 and 2 recorded in
[`external-source-register.md`](external-source-register.md), but **no source
sentence available to this repository says it**. It is not an ISO quotation and
must never be presented as one. See [`source-map.md`](source-map.md) §4.

## 4. What makes this module different

Modules 1 and 2 were built **entirely from repository evidence**. Every statement
could be traced to a controlled Harrismith document, and no external source was
needed for any slide.

**Module 3 cannot be.** Its subject is a copyrighted international standard that
**is not present in this repository** and has not been read for this programme.
Three consequences follow, and they shape every file in the module:

1. **The available evidence is public metadata, not requirements.** Official
   public summaries describe *scope*. They do not provide clauses, definitions,
   tables or obligations, and they must not be extended into any.
2. **Implementation guidance is not the standard.** UK BIM Framework material is
   useful and is used — but it is jurisdiction-specific guidance, separately
   labelled throughout, and never presented as ISO text.
3. **Harrismith is an illustration, not evidence of conformity.** No clause-by-
   clause assessment exists, and none is possible from the material available.

[`external-source-register.md`](external-source-register.md) and
[`source-map.md`](source-map.md) are the load-bearing files of this increment for
exactly this reason. They are not administrative overhead — they are what makes
the module safe to deliver.

## 5. Source hierarchy

Applied throughout the module. **A lower level never silently overwrites a higher
one.**

| Level | Source class | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Published ISO standard or official ISO public metadata | The published scope of `ISO 19650-1:2018` |
| **2** | Official implementation guidance, jurisdiction-labelled | UK BIM Framework material — **UK context** |
| **3** | Harrismith repository evidence | BEP §4.6, §6.1, §13.4 |
| **4** | Supported interpretation | A conclusion that follows from levels 1–3 |
| **5** | Teaching synthesis | Presenter framing with no source sentence behind it |

Two rules carry most of the weight:

- **A UK implementation convention is never labelled a universal ISO
  requirement.**
- **A Harrismith term is never relabelled as ISO terminology** because the
  concepts look similar. Relabelling is a mapping decision, and no such decision
  has been taken.

## 6. Copyright and quotation control

**ISO standards are copyrighted, and this repository holds none of their text.**

| Prohibited | Required instead |
|---|---|
| Reproducing complete clauses | Accurate paraphrase of public scope |
| Reconstructing ISO tables or diagrams | Original teaching diagrams, labelled as such |
| Quoting more than a brief phrase | Public abstract-level statements |
| Creating a substitute copy of the standard | Reference to title and edition |
| Implying access to text not held here | An explicit "verify against a licensed copy" |

No clause reference appears anywhere in this module, because **no clause has been
read**. Inventing one would be worse than omitting it. Where an audience needs
the exact requirement, the answer is to consult a licensed copy of the standard.

## 7. Scope

**In scope:**

- what the published Parts 1 and 2 are stated to cover, at public-summary level;
- the distinction between concepts and principles and a delivery-phase process;
- why information requirements precede information production;
- the life-cycle framing of information management;
- functions versus job titles, and why appointments still matter;
- how containers, states and planned exchanges make responsibility manageable —
  **at concept level only**;
- how Harrismith illustrates some of this, and where the illustration stops;
- what Triviron must verify before making any alignment claim.

**Out of scope for Module 3:**

| Out of scope | Belongs to |
|---|---|
| CDE workflows and state mechanics in detail | **Module 4** |
| Responsibility matrices row by row; delivery planning in detail | **Module 5** |
| Coordination, review, approval and assurance in detail | **Module 6** |
| Triviron BEP development | Module 7 |
| Workshop facilitation | Module 8 |
| Any clause-level ISO requirement | **No module** — not available to this programme |

**Module-boundary rule.** Module 3 explains **why** these controls exist and how
they relate. Slides 10 and 11 must stay at concept level: one CDE slide and one
delivery-planning slide, neither teaching mechanics. If a slide starts explaining
*how a transition is executed*, it has crossed into Module 4.

## 8. Relationship to Modules 1 and 2

Module 3 depends on three positions already established and does **not**
re-derive them:

| Carried forward | From |
|---|---|
| The BEP records the project's agreed method | Module 1 |
| Functions and authorities must be assigned; permission is not authority | Module 2 |
| `lead appointed party` and `appointed party` are **not Harrismith terms** | Module 2, `source-map.md` §2 |

The third is where Module 3 begins. Module 2 recorded that those terms appear
**zero times** across `bep/`, `supporting/` and `docs/`, and deferred them here.
Module 3 introduces them as **ISO-associated terminology** and states plainly
that Harrismith uses `Owner / Appointing Party` and `Lead Delivery Party`
instead — a difference to be mapped deliberately, not smoothed over.

## 9. Terminology control

**No Harrismith term is replaced by an ISO-associated term in this module.**

| Harrismith term | ISO-associated term | Status |
|---|---|---|
| `Owner / Appointing Party` | appointing party | **Similar, not established as identical** |
| `Lead Delivery Party` | lead appointed party | **Not equivalent — must not be substituted** |
| *(no term)* | appointed party | **Absent from Harrismith** |
| `WIP` / `Shared` / `Published / Authorised` / `Record / Retained` | ISO-associated state language | **Resembles; conformity not demonstrated** |

Where both vocabularies appear on a slide, they appear in **two visibly separate
columns**, never merged into one list. See [`source-map.md`](source-map.md) §6.

## 10. Honesty constraints on delivery

Four things must be said, or the module misrepresents both the standard and the
example:

1. **Harrismith does not conform to ISO 19650, and no such claim is made.** The
   BEP says so itself: ISO 19650 principles *inform* the approach — a statement
   of influence — and **no formal compliance, assessment or certification is
   claimed** (BEP §11.2, §13.4).
2. **No clause-by-clause conformity assessment exists**, and none can be produced
   from the material available to this programme.
3. **The standard has not been read for this programme.** What is taught is
   public scope, official guidance and Harrismith evidence — each labelled.
4. **Both published editions are under revision.** The 2018 editions are what is
   taught; drafts are recorded and are **not** current requirements.

Point 3 matters most. An audience that believes the presenter is quoting the
standard will treat every teaching simplification as a requirement.
